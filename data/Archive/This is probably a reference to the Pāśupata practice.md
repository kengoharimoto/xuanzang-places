# This is probably a reference to the Pāśupata practice  
  
　　此寺昔日有沙彌，常除灰，因入神定。維那挽之，不覺皮連骨離。濕婆仙代沙彌除灰處，國王與濕婆仙立廟，圖其形像，以金傅之。  
  
宋雲 Sung-un  
  
宋雲 mentions a Śaiva temple? He mentions smearing ashes so sounds like the Pāśupatas (on p. 192 in the Japanese translation, in relation to a temple in the neighborhood of Uddyana. Maybe it is a small shrine inside a Buddhist temple, though. And the reference might not have anything to do with the Pāśupatas.   
  
此寺昔日有沙彌，常除灰，因入神定。維那挽之，不覺皮連骨離。濕婆仙代沙彌除灰處，國王與濕婆仙立廟，圖其形像，以金傅之。  
  
(There is a mention to the Pārsis and Mahādeva 天神 in this passage?) This may indeed be a mention to the Pāśupatas.  
  
Although Beal and Nagasawa both translate 挽 as "to draw" or "to pull" the character also means "to moan"  
  
濕婆仙 seems like it is the same as simply 濕婆  
  
The sentence immediately preceding the passage also suggest co-existence of Buddhism and Śaivism:  
  
山中有昔五百羅漢床，南北兩行相向坐處，其次第相對。有大寺，僧徒二百人。太子所食泉水北有寺，恒以驢數頭運糧上山，無人驅逐，自然往還。寅發午至，每及中餐。此是護塔神濕婆仙使之然。  
  
長沢 identifies 善特山 to Mekha-Sanda. Google takes me to Shahbaz Garhi near Mardan where an Aśokan rock edict is. According to Nagasawa, this place is 檀特山 in 魏書 v. 102 西域伝 烏萇国  
  
Perhaps this is the same place 玄奘 too mentions as a place of Pāśupata practice?  
  
The mention of the Śaiva practice in 善特山 was missed by Beal (p. xcix).  
  
善特山 is 弾多落迦 of 玄奘  
  
The mountain 玄奘 mentions to have a Śaiva temple and the Pāśupata is identified with Karamar mountain near Mardan
