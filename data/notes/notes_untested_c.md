### 05.04.04 大施場 (Mahādānabhūmi, the Grand Arena of Almsgiving) — the sandy plain at the Prayāga confluence; no site

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Gaṅgā–Yamunā confluence, Triveṇī, Allahabad. Cunningham (1871, pp. 446–47): between
the city and the confluence, since carried away by the rivers. Beal (p. 233) gives no site.
**Evidence.** Cunningham (ASR I, pp. 295–98) notes the sandy plain has been eroded. The Allahabad gazetteer (Muttra,
Allahabad, Fatehpur 1884) repeats that the city lay W of a large sandy plain at the confluence; Allahabad 1911 argues
that the confluence has moved (05.04). The Life (T50:0234b) gives the same place, 14–15 li round.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the Gaṅgā–Yamunā confluence (p. 465).
**Assessment.** The general position at the confluence is not in doubt; the ground itself has gone, so nothing more
exact can be tested. Probable, at the level of the area.

### 06.02.x1 宮城側伽藍 (monastery beside the palace city, 3,000 Sammitīya monks) — Mukherji's building before Tilaurakot's E gate, or the monastic complex E of the city; depends on Tilaurakot

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, p. 52): the foundations of a large quadrangular building he excavated in front of
the E gate of Tilaura-kot. No other authority.
**Evidence.** Partial excavation only; no inscription, no date. The parent is open between Piprahwa–Ganwaria and
Tilaurakot (06.02).
**Recent scholarship.** Geophysics round the stūpa Mound XI outside the E side of Tilaurakot revealed a monastic
complex about 250 m N–S by 100 m E–W adjoining it on the S, with a pot of 497 punch-marked coins at the S end of its
boundary wall; Murakami (2016, pp. 240–241) says the complex recalls Xuanzang's monastery beside the palace city with
3,000 Sammitīya monks (T51:900c). Coningham et al. (2019, pp. 237, 241, 254) list "the Eastern Stupa and Monastery"
with Mukherji's plan of 1899, date the coin hoard to the 3rd century BCE and note walled ponds round the monastery;
they make no identification.
**Assessment.** A proposal inside the Tilaurakot frame, now with a large monastery E of the city that agrees in
position with Mukherji's building; but nothing dates its use to the 7th century or names a Sammitīya community, and it
is only as good as Tilaurakot. Open.

### 06.02.x2 淨飯王正殿・摩耶夫人寢殿 (Śuddhodana's hall, Māyā's chamber, conception vihāra) — Tilaurakot (Mukherji's NW area, or the walled central complex with a building over its wall) or Ganwaria's larger complex (Srivastava)

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, pp. 20–21, 49–51): the NW mound/area of the brick fort of Tilaura-kot, with palace
foundations about 5 ft below ground and octagonal and square structures.
**Evidence.** Mukherji's trenches exposed walls but no inscription; Mitra's later dig at Tilaurakot (IAR 1961–62, p. 74)
found nothing "to establish or disprove" the city. The rival is Srivastava's at Ganwaria beside Piprahwa: the larger
brick complex, about 39 m square, "the residential complex of … Sakya king Suddhodhana" (IAR 1974–75, p. 41), and
shrines built over houses, dug to test Xuanzang's vihāras on palace sites (1976–77, p. 51). Ganwaria shows no level
after the 4th century (06.02).
**Recent scholarship.** Falk (2017), following Srivastava's own plans, takes the Ganwaria complexes for monasteries, not
a palace, and reports them burnt about AD 300; the IAR 2012–13 trenches (Mani and Mishra, pp. 152–153) found nothing
after the Kushan period there. Murakami (2016, pp. 240–241) reports a walled complex 100 × 100 m with a brick wall 1.5
m wide at Mound V in the centre of Tilaurakot (geophysics 2015), dug from January 2016: a strong N gate and, E of it,
building A, with ten or more rooms, built on top of the complex's wall. He takes the complex for Faxian's and
Xuanzang's palace of Śuddhodana and compares building A with Xuanzang's vihāra over the hall, without asserting the
match. Coningham et al. (2019, pp. 235, 237) call it "a palatial walled complex" with four gates and list "CWC – The
Sangharama" inside it, without date or argument.
**Assessment.** Both are inferences from the parent identification, which is now open (06.02). At Tilaurakot a walled
central enclosure with a monastic building over its wall gives the arrangement Xuanzang describes, a vihāra over the
hall, though in the centre rather than in Mukherji's NW area; it is undated, unnamed, and the palace reading is an
inference. The Ganwaria complex is more probably a monastery deserted before Xuanzang. Open.

### 06.02.x3 阿私多仙相太子處 (Asita's stūpa) — Mukherji's sixteen-sided brick structure at Tilaurakot; depends on Tilaurakot

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, pp. 20–21, 51–52): the sixteen-sided solid brick structure with a portico, NE of
the octagonal building he took for the conception vihāra ("I at once concluded that this must have been the Stupa of
Asita").
**Evidence.** The bearing (NE of the conception vihāra) matches Xuanzang, but the reference point is itself Mukherji's
proposal (06.02.x2). No inscription or date.
**Assessment.** Internally consistent, unconfirmed, and only as good as Tilaurakot. Open.

### 06.02.x4 角力擲象窣堵波・象墮阬 (stūpa where the prince threw the elephant, and the elephant pit) — unlocated; Mukherji's mound S of Tilaurakot is probably an iron-working mound; Vost's Kudan tank

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, p. 52): a small mound about 500 ft S of the S gate of Tilaura-kot, and for the
pit a ditch 100 ft E of it. Vost (1906, reported by Coningham et al. 2019, p. 250): the tank at Kudan for the elephant
pit (Hastigarta).
**Evidence.** Xuanzang puts the stūpa at the S gate and the pit beyond the moat (T51:0901a). Strickland et al. (2016,
pdf pp. 1, 3–4, 10) dug Lohasariya, a low slag-covered mound about 150 m S of the city that Mukherji's 1899 plan had
marked as a cardinal stūpa: an artificially raised iron-working mound, dated by radiocarbon from the 6th–5th to the
2nd century BCE, with no Buddhist remains. That it is Mukherji's mound 500 ft S of the S gate is this study's
inference from the distance; the excavators do not say so. Coningham et al. take the Kudan tank for a pit dug for
brick clay.
**Assessment.** If the inference holds, Mukherji's stūpa is an industrial mound; Vost's tank rests on position alone.
Both belong to the Tilaurakot frame (06.02, open). Unlocated.

### 06.02.x5 太子妃寢宮 (Yaśodharā's chamber) — Mukherji's buildings NW of Tilaurakot's central tank

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, pp. 51–52): buildings excavated NW of the central tank of Tilaura-kot.
**Evidence.** Partly traced foundations; no inscription. Xuanzang puts the chamber beside the palace vihāras.
**Assessment.** A placing within the Tilaurakot frame with nothing specific to Yaśodharā. Open.

### 06.02.x6 太子學堂故基 (the prince's school-house) — Tilaurakot (Mukherji) or Ganwaria (Srivastava)

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, pp. 51–52): a building partly traced NE of the central tank of Tilaura-kot.
**Evidence.** At Ganwaria, Srivastava took a block of rooms, verandahs and two courtyards for a school, "which would
corroborate the statement of the Chinese traveller" (IAR 1975–76, p. 49). Neither has an inscription.
**Recent scholarship.** Falk (2017) reads the Ganwaria buildings as monasteries burnt about AD 300, which removes the
'school' reading's support from the plan.
**Assessment.** Two proposals by analogy, each tied to its own Kapilavastu (06.02, now open). Open.

### 06.02.x7 踰城處精舍 (vihāra at the SE corner of the city where the prince left on horseback) — unlocated; a local "Kanthaka stūpa" E of Tilaurakot, untested

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, pp. 53–54) found nothing and would seek it near the gate before his saṅghārāma
(06.02.x1) or at Sandwā.
**Evidence.** Xuanzang has a vihāra with an image of the prince on his white horse at the SE corner of the city
(T51:0901b). Murakami (2016, p. 240) reports that the stūpa Mound XI outside the E side of Tilaurakot is locally
called the Kanthaka stūpa and linked with the departure, and says this needs verification: a local name for a stūpa,
not the vihāra Xuanzang describes.
**Assessment.** Unlocated; the tradition is untested and belongs to the Tilaurakot frame (06.02, open).

### 06.02.x9 釋種誅死處 (stūpas of the massacre of the Śākyas, NW of the city) — Führer's Sagarwa stūpas N of Tilaurakot, unconfirmed; Carlleyle's Bhata falls with Bhuila Dih

**Identifications.** Führer, followed by V. A. Smith (in Mukherji 1901, pp. 8–17) and Mukherji (pp. 28–29, 54–56): the
stūpas in the Sagarwa forest, about 2 miles N of Tilaura-kot. Carlleyle (ASR XII, pp. 111–112, 172–173): Bhata
(Kosahra), 1½ miles NW of Bhuila Dih.
**Evidence.** Führer dug 17 small brick stūpas at Sagarwa, with bricks Smith reports as marked with weapons. Coningham
et al. (2019, pp. 247–248) add that his excavations of 1896 exposed a large brick monument of twelve rooms, like the
cruciform temples at Kudan, by the W edge of the tank, with a copper casket of bones, two gold nāgas and semi-precious
stones at its centre, and the small stūpas SE of it; geophysics in 2016 and 2018 found more buried brick structures.
They report Führer's identification without endorsing it. Sagarwa lies N of Tilaurakot, where Xuanzang has NW
(T51:0901b).
**Assessment.** Carlleyle's site falls with Bhuila Dih (06.02). Sagarwa has many small stūpas, as Xuanzang's legend
requires, but no date or inscription, and it stands only in the Tilaurakot frame (06.02, open). Open.

### 06.02.x10 自在天祠 (Īśvara temple outside the gate) — the Tauleshvara liṅga temple at Taulihawa; depends on Tilaurakot

**Identifications.** V. A. Smith (in Mukherji 1901, pp. 8–17) and Mukherji (pp. 29–30, 54–56): the Tauleshvara
(Tauliva-īśvara) liṅga temple at Tauliva (Taulihawa). Mukherji rejects Führer's temple at Bikuli, which "stood out of
the way" and did not form the city's boundary.
**Evidence.** The name and the Śaiva cult; no early remains reported at Tauliva. Bikuli has carved bricks and liṅgas.
**Recent scholarship.** Falk (2017, n. 9) reports that the liṅga in the Taulihawa temple is a reused piece of the
upper shaft of the Gotihawa Aśokan pillar; the cult object is therefore not old, though the shrine may be.
**Assessment.** A name-and-cult link, from the Tilaurakot frame only, now weaker. Open.

### 06.02.x11 角藝射鐵鼓窣堵波 (stūpa outside the S gate where the prince competed in archery) — unlocated; Mukherji's Derwa mounds S of Tilaurakot untested, the nearby Lohasariya mound industrial

**Identifications.** Mukherji (1901, pp. 54–56): one of the two stūpa-like mounds at Derwa, a furlong S of the citadel
of Tilaura-kot.
**Evidence.** Xuanzang puts the stūpa outside the S gate, left of the road (T51:0902a). Strickland et al. (2016, pdf
pp. 1, 3–4, 10) show that Lohasariya, the low mound about 150 m S of the city that Mukherji's 1899 plan marked as a
cardinal stūpa, is an iron-working mound (radiocarbon 6th–5th to 2nd century BCE) without Buddhist remains. It lies
near the Derwa mounds (a furlong is about 200 m); this study takes it rather for Mukherji's mound of 06.02.x4 (500
ft), an inference from the distances only.
**Assessment.** Nothing confirms a stūpa at Derwa, and the one excavated mound S of the city is industrial. Unlocated,
in the Tilaurakot frame only (06.02, open).

### 07.01 婆羅痆斯國 (Vārāṇasī) — Benares; the ancient city excavated at Rajghat

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Benares on the W bank of the Ganges. Vivien (p. 360), Cunningham (1871, p. 499), Beal
(p. 44), Watters (p. 46), Majumdar (p. 715) and Dey agree.
**Evidence.** Ghosh's summary of the Rajghat excavation (IAR 1957–58) dates the ancient city on the river from NBP to
medieval times. Cunningham (ASR I, p. 115) and the sealings that identify Sarnath (Dikshit, AR 1927–28) fix the Deer
Park beside it. The Life (T50:0235b) has the same 500 li and the city on the Ganges.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees on Benares (p. 559) but raises a bank problem: the city is on the Ganges' left
bank, while Xuanzang's 都城西臨殑伽河 should put it on the right, so either the river moved or the old city lay across it.
The difficulty is not real: Xuanzang uses the same words for Kanyākubja (國大都城西臨殑伽河), a city on the W bank with the
river on its E, so the phrase means "on the west, by the Ganges", and Rajghat, the excavated ancient city, is on the W
bank.
**Assessment.** Secure.

### 07.01.02 婆羅痆河西無憂王窣堵波・石柱 (Aśoka stūpa and pillar W of the Varaṇā) — Lat Bhairon; nothing in these sources beyond Mizutani

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the broken Aśoka pillar at Lat Bhairon, NE of Benares city, confirmed by V. A. Smith and
destroyed in the riot of 1908 (as summarised; the Lat Bhairon riot was in 1809); 婆羅痆河 = the Barnā. Watters (p. 48) gives no site.
**Evidence.** None coded in the survey, reports or gazetteers.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji takes the river as the Varaṇā (Barnā) and the pillar as Aśoka's, of which fragments
were found and are now kept inside a mosque (Mookerji, *Asoka*, p. 85; Ji p. 561); he names no site. Mookerji's
fragments are those of the Lat Bhairon pillar, so Ji's "surviving fragments" qualifies the note's "destroyed" without
moving the place.
**Name.** 痆 is a retroflex ṇ (孃, 女黠反), so 婆羅痆 is va-ra-ṇa: the Sanskrit river name Varaṇā, which the Bālamanoramā
glosses "varaṇā nāma nadī kāśyā utarataḥ [sic] prasiddhā" and the Kṛtyakalpataru (Tīrthavivecana) also names;
Mizutani's Barnā is its modern Hindi form. 婆羅痆 could also be a short form of Vārāṇasī's name, but the river reading is
the natural one, and the site is unaffected (data/names/).
**Assessment.** Probable on Mizutani's authority and the position NE of the city by the Barnā; untested here.

### 07.03.03 吠舍釐王所建舍利窣堵波 (the Licchavis' relic stūpa) — Altekar's mud stūpa NW of Basarh, with a casket a quarter full

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the relic stūpa found by A. S. Altekar. No other authority.
**Evidence.** Altekar dug by Xuanzang's directions, NW of the city and SE of the Aśoka pillar (IAR 1957–58, pp. 10–11):
a mud stūpa of NBP date, 25 ft across, enlarged four times (first in Mauryan times, with polished Chunar fragments),
with a soapstone casket "only a quarter full" in a breach of the core. Ghosh's summary (same year) calls it possibly
one of the eight relic stūpas. That fits the *Record of India*: Aśoka opened the stūpa and took nine-tenths of the
relics. No inscription.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji gives no site; he cites only the Vinaya (根本說一切有部毘奈耶雜事 39) for the Licchavis' seventh
share of the relics (p. 590). He attaches Altekar's stūpa and its "re-buried" casket to the Aśoka stūpa instead
(07.03.04, p. 591), which the text does not allow: Xuanzang's Aśoka stūpa stands beside the lion pillar NW of this
one, whereas Altekar's stūpa lies by Basarh, SE of the Kolhua pillar, and it is this stūpa whose relics Aśoka is said
to have taken nine-tenths of.
**Assessment.** An excavated pre-Mauryan stūpa, opened in antiquity and found where Xuanzang puts it, matches the account
closely. Secure, without an inscription.

### 07.03.04 無憂王窣堵波・石柱 (Aśoka stūpa and lion pillar) — the Kolhua pillar and the stūpa beside it

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the lion-capital pillar near Bakhra, at Kolhua. Majumdar (p. 717): the lion pillar and
tank E of Bakhra.
**Evidence.** Cunningham measured over 44 ft and put the original at "about 50 feet", as Xuanzang says (ASR I, pp.
62–64; XVI, pp. 13–15). Bloch found the pillar, the stūpa N of it and the Rām-kund tank S of it "tally exactly" with the
account (AR 1903–04, pp. 81–86; EC 1903–04, pp. 14–15); Muzaffarpur 1907 repeats. Clearance exposed a brick stūpa 24.5 m
across in front of the pillar (IAR 1973–74, pp. 67–68), and Sita Ram Roy two mud stūpas by it (1959–60, pp. 14–16).
L. C. Singh's season of 1990–91 (IAR 1990–91, pp. 3–4) exposed the āyakas of the main stūpa's last phase and votive
stūpas packed round the pillar on a Gupta lime floor, and S of the stūpa a shrine and caitya later turned into rooms.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees on the pillar: the lion-capital pillar near Bakhra, lower half buried
(p. 591). He takes the stūpa beside it as Altekar's, with Aśoka's re-buried casket (after 世界考古學大系 8, p. 97, figs
157–58); that confuses Altekar's stūpa by Basarh (07.03.03) with the brick stūpa at the pillar.
**Assessment.** A standing pillar with a lion, of the height given, and an excavated stūpa beside it. Secure.

### 07.03.x1 獼猴池 (the monkeys' tank) — the Rām-kund S of the Kolhua pillar, exposed by excavation

**Identifications.** Cunningham (ASR I, pp. 62–63; XVI, pp. 15–16) and Muzaffarpur 1907: the brick-faced tank S of the
pillar, now Ram-kund, which "evidently is the monkey tank".
**Evidence.** Bloch (AR 1903–04, p. 84) measured it at 200 × 100 ft: "As to their location no possible doubt exists."
The Survey exposed a Kushan brick-lined tank in seven tiers with two ghats, S of the pillar: first partly, 20 m S of
the pillar, with a drain running into it from the votive stūpas (L. C. Singh, IAR 1990–91, pp. 3–4), then further (IAR
1991–92, pp. 4–5; 1992–93, pp. 5–6), and in 1993–94 completely: c. 65 × 33 m, with bathing ghats on the W and S arms
and a retaining wall all round (IAR 1993–94, pp. 10–11), close to Bloch's 200 × 100 ft. No report names Xuanzang.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji gives no site (Ram-kund is not mentioned); he cites the 玄應音義 (the tank beside
Āmrapālī's garden), the Vinaya and the relief of the monkey's gift on the N gate at Sanchi (p. 591).
**Assessment.** An excavated tank in the position Xuanzang gives, beside a secure pillar (07.03.04). Secure.

### 07.05 尼波羅國 (Nepāla) — Nepal, the Kathmandu valley; capital not identified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Nepal; capital not identified. Vivien (p. 369) and Dey: the Kathmandu valley; Cunningham
(1871, p. 516), Beal (p. 81) and Watters (p. 83): Nepal.
**Evidence.** Hearsay. Route test: 1,400–1,500 li NW from Vṛji gives 163 km to the Kathmandu valley against about 347 km
predicted, bearing 17° off (`segment_tests.tsv`, flagged on distance); a mountain road would lengthen the figure.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji narrows the country to the Kathmandu valley and holds that Xuanzang did not go there
(p. 613); he cites Fan Xiangyong (《大唐西域記闕文考辨》, 文史 13) for the passages on Nepal lost from the received text and kept
by Daoxuan and Daoshi, and Wang Xuance's account of the fire pond (pp. 617–18).
**Assessment.** The country is not in doubt; the capital is untested.

### 08.00 摩掲陀國 (Magadha) — South Bihar; its centres are fixed by excavation and inscriptions

**Identifications.** Mizutani: S Bihar, centred on Gayā and Patna districts. Vivien (p. 371), Cunningham (1871, p. 521),
Watters (p. 85), Majumdar (p. 719) and Dey agree; the gazetteers (Gaya, Shahabad and Patna, Saran 1877) repeat
Cunningham's bounds.
**Evidence.** The places inside it are secure on site evidence: Pāṭaliputra (08.02.01), the Mahābodhi temple (08.06.04),
Nālandā by its own sealings (09.03.01). The Life (T50:0236a) crosses the Ganges S into it.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: roughly the Patna and Gayā districts (p. 620).
**Assessment.** Secure.

### 08.02.x1 無憂王地獄 (Aśoka's "hell") — the Agam Kuan well, Patna, on a Jain legend; doubted by Page

**Identifications.** Waddell, reported by Patna 1907: the Agam Kuan, where Jain priests tell of a monk thrown into a
furnace and found on lotuses, Xuanzang's legend.
**Evidence.** Spooner gives the well's other name as "Asoka's Hell", half a mile E of Kumrahar (EC 1915–16, p. 30). Page
(AR 1925–26, p. 31): the superstructure is Muhammadan and "there is little to connect it with Mauryan times". Xuanzang's
marker is a stone pillar N of the old palace; no pillar is reported at the well.
**Assessment.** A legend and a name, without remains of the period or the pillar. Open.

### 08.05.01 伽耶城 (Gayā) — Gayā town (Brahma-Gayā)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Gayā town, Brahma-Gayā. Vivien (p. 374), Cunningham (1871, p. 524), Beal (p. 113) and
Dey agree.
**Evidence.** Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 40): from his Śīlabhadra monastery (Kauwa-dol) Gayā is 12 miles SW, as Xuanzang's
40–50 li SW. Cunningham (ASR I, p. 53) can match the holy spring 30 li N only by reading 130 li (Patal Ganga). The
Brahmin pilgrimage town fits Xuanzang's thousand Brahmin families.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Brahma-Gayā, the old town covering modern Sahebganj (N) and old Gayā (S)
(p. 663).
**Assessment.** Probable to secure; the distance fits, though the spring needs an emendation.

### 08.05.01a 伽耶山 (Gayāśīrṣa) — Brahmayoni hill, SW of Gayā

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Brahmayoni, with no trace of Aśoka's stūpa. Cunningham (1871, p. 524): Brahm-juin, about
a mile SW of Gayā, a small temple on the stūpa's site. Dey agrees; Vivien (p. 374): the Gayasir hill. Gaya 1906 names
Brahmayoni as Gayāśīrṣa.
**Evidence.** Route test: 5–6 li SW of the town gives 2.4 km at 204°, within the expected range (`segment_tests.tsv`).
No remains of the stūpa reported.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Brahmayoni hill, S of Gayā, about 400 ft high (p. 664).
**Assessment.** The authorities agree and the bearing and distance fit. Probable.

### 08.06.21 尼連禪那河浴處窣堵波 (stūpa where the Bodhisattva bathed in the Nairañjanā) — Tika Bigha on Cunningham's map only

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Tika Bigha by the Nairañjanā, S of Bodh Gayā, from Cunningham's map. No other authority.
**Evidence.** Cunningham (ASR XI, p. 147) lists the stūpas on the Lilajan bank as sites F–N of his plan, without
excavation or finds that single out the bathing place.
**Recent scholarship.** Anand 2019 (blog) moves the austerity group to the E bank round Bakraur (Dharmāraṇya,
Mātaṅgavāpī), arguing from the pilgrims' bearings; he has only unexcavated surface mounds, and the Ratanāgiri rock 200 m
E of Bakraur that he cites is Faxian's, not Xuanzang's.
**Assessment.** A label on a plan, and a rival argued from bearings alone. Open between Tika Bigha and the E bank.

### 08.06.x1 佛經行處 (the Buddha's promenade N of the Bodhi tree) — the raised walk outside the N wall of the Mahābodhi temple

**Identifications.** Cunningham (ASR XVI, pp. iii–iv): the promenade found "almost intact" in the clearance N of the
temple.
**Evidence.** Two rows of stone pillar bases, each lettered in Aśokan script (vowels S, consonants N), showing a covered
walk; Xuanzang describes a raised brick base over 3 ft high running E–W N of the tree. Probably the "Jewel-walk" of
Gaya 1957 (08.06.01).
**Assessment.** A standing structure of the kind and in the place described, early in date. Secure.

### 08.06.x2 婆羅門兄弟鑿池 (the Brahmin brothers' tank S of the enclosure) — the Budhokhar (Buddhapokhar) Tal

**Identifications.** Cunningham (ASR XI, p. 145) and Gaya 1957: the Budhokhar Tal, about 400 ft square, S of the temple.
**Evidence.** Cunningham gives 1,620 ft round against Xuanzang's 700 paces. In ASR I (p. 10) he had given the same tank
to Mucilinda, and moved it (08.06.18). No excavation.
**Assessment.** A large tank S of the enclosure, as Xuanzang says; Cunningham's own shift shows the attribution rests on
position. Probable.

### 09.02.02 洩瑟知林 (Yaṣṭivana) — Jeshtiban plateau by Jethian, where the name survives

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Jeshtiban, not Cunningham's Jakhtiban. Cunningham (1871, p. 528), Beal (p. 147) and Watters
(p. 147, tentative): Jakhtiban. Majumdar (p. 721, after Stein): Jeshtiban; Dey: Jethian.
**Evidence.** Gaya 1906 and 1957, after Stein: a small plateau ¾ mile E of Jethian with traces of old buildings, still
called Jeshtiban, "an almost perfect preservation of the ancient name". Cunningham (ASR III, pp. 139–40) found bamboos
but no Aśokan stūpa. Tapoban lies 2 miles SW, as the hot springs 10 li SW (09.02.03). The Life calls it 杖林山.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Jeshtiban, the Life's 杖林 (p. 711).
**Assessment.** The name, old remains and the bearing to Tapoban support Mizutani. Probable; no excavation.

### 09.02.03a 大山 (the great mountain SE of Yaṣṭivana) — the Handia range, with Stein's stūpa platform at Sahudrasthan and Bimbisāra's walled road

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Cunningham (1871, p. 529) and Beal (p. 148): Handia hill (1,463 ft).
**Evidence.** Gaya 1906 and 1957, after Stein, put the stūpa of the three months' preaching at Sahudrasthan, at the end
of a spur SE of Jethian near the Saffi Ghat: a shrine on a platform of old bricks, with brick debris on the slopes. A
road on a walled foundation runs about 600 yards W from below it; it "fully bears out" Xuanzang's road built by
Bimbisāra. The texts do not say whether the spur belongs to Handia.
**Assessment.** The stūpa site and the road give the area field evidence. Probable.

### 09.02.03b 孤山 (Vyāsa's isolated hill) — a rock recess at the SW end of the Handia range (Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Pansabda recess SW of Handia hill, after Majumdar (p. 721, Stein). Cunningham (p. 529):
not identified. Gaya 1906/1957, after Stein: "perhaps" a rocky recess at the S foot of the isolated hill of Bhaluahi,
at the SW end of the range, half a mile from the Saffi Ghat; probably the same proposal under two names.
**Evidence.** No remains described. Xuanzang puts the hill 3–4 li N of the great mountain; "SW of Handia" does not
match unless the great mountain is the Sahudrasthan spur (09.02.03a). Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 84) saw a stūpa-like mound
near Sonagiri from Baibhār, never visited. The route test is not usable (see the report: wrong anchor).
**Assessment.** Stein's own "perhaps", and a bearing that does not obviously fit. Open.

### 09.02.x1 下乘・退凡窣堵波 (the two stūpas on Bimbisāra's road) — the two brick stūpas half-way up the causeway on Chhatha-giri

**Identifications.** D. N. Sen in Patna 1907: traces of the two stūpas on the "Bimbisara road". Marshall (AR 1905–06,
p. 92) and Nazim (AR 1936–37, p. 47).
**Evidence.** Marshall found both "right in the middle of the causeway" and opened them; Nazim cleared "a couple of
ancient brick stupas in the middle of the road half way up". The hill is secure (09.02.09).
**Assessment.** Excavated structures in the position Xuanzang gives, on a secure hill. Secure.

### 09.03.x2 觀自在菩薩立像 (standing Avalokiteśvara, Nālandā) — Sastri's image at the NE corner of the Site 3 stūpa; rival readings

**Identifications.** Hirananda Sastri (EC 1919–20, p. 36): the standing Avalokiteśvara in a small shrine at the NE corner
of the great stūpa of Site No. 3.
**Evidence.** The image was found in situ; the stūpa S of it fits Xuanzang's next item (09.03.x3). Spooner read Site 3 as
the shrine of the three months' stay (09.03.06), and Cunningham put the image at his mound C (ASR I, pp. 29–30).
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji offers another image: a 5th-century standing Avalokiteśvara found near Site 12 (after
Takata Osamu, 印度南海の仏教美術, p. 104), and leaves open whether it is Xuanzang's (p. 760). A second candidate, no better
placed than Sastri's.
**Assessment.** A real image in a plausible place, under one of two incompatible readings of Site 3. Open.

### 09.03.x3 如來剃剪髮爪窣堵波 (stūpa of hair and nails) — the great stūpa of Site 3 on Sastri's reading

**Identifications.** Hirananda Sastri (EC 1919–20, p. 36): the great stūpa of Site No. 3, which "can safely be identified"
with it.
**Evidence.** A stūpa encased several times, with votive stūpas and corner temples; no relic deposit or inscription
naming the hair and nails is reported. Spooner gives the same monument to 09.03.06.
**Assessment.** Depends on choosing Sastri's reading over Spooner's (09.03.06, low). Open.

### 09.03.x5 滿冑王銅佛像 (Pūrṇavarman's copper Buddha) — the Sarai mound temple, E of the Nālandā monasteries

**Identifications.** V. K. Misra (IAR 1975–76, p. 3): the Sarai mound temple, where a 7th–8th-century inscription
mentioning bronze Buddhas installed by king Pūrṇavarman was found earlier.
**Evidence.** The mound lies E of the excavated remains (IAR 1973–74, pp. 67–68), as Xuanzang's 200 paces E outside
the wall. Bisht (IAR 1978–79, p. 67) laid bare the lotus seat and feet of a colossal standing Buddha of earth and lime,
18–20 m tall when intact. The inscription is reported, not edited, in these sources.
**Assessment.** Donor, position and a colossal standing image agree, though the image found is not copper. Probable.

### 09.03.x6 帝釋窟東峯伽藍・亘娑窣堵波 (E-peak monastery and Haṃsa stūpa, Indraśailaguhā) — Giriyek: the E-peak terrace and Jarasandh-ka-baithak

**Identifications.** Cunningham (ASR III, pp. 145–48): the oblong terrace on the lower E peak of Giryek and the brick
stūpa "Jarasandh-ka-baithak" (28 ft across). Patna 1907: the stūpa, reservoir and summit terraces opposite Giriak.
**Evidence.** Cunningham found a figure with "a large goose carved on the pedestal" and lac seals showing a stūpa with a
goose (ASR I, pp. 16–22); the stūpa is the only one on the hill. The hill is secure (09.03.11).
**Assessment.** The single stūpa, the goose imagery and the monastery terrace match. Secure, without an inscription.

### 10.04.02 跋始婆僧伽藍 (*Bhāśiva monastery) — Bhasu Bihar, 4 miles W of Mahasthan, where the name survives

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the ruins at Bihar and Vasu-Bihar, 4 miles NW of Mahāsthān. Majumdar (pp. 724–25):
Bhasu Bihar, 4 miles W.
**Evidence.** Cunningham (ASR XV, pp. v, 102–04): the village name, the Bihar mound (700 × 600 ft) as the monastery, a
30-ft brick stūpa mound, and a ruined temple N of it as the Avalokiteśvara vihāra. Bogra 1910 and CII 3 (p. 286) accept
it; Dikshit visited (EC 1920–21, p. 23). The city is settled by the Mahasthan inscription (10.04). The Life writes
跋姞婆.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees with Mizutani (ruins between Bihar and Vasu-Bihar, 4 miles NW of Mahāsthān)
and adds that the site has been excavated, with many Gupta-period finds, and that the Aśoka stūpa and the
Avalokiteśvara vihāra both have traces, citing N. Ahmed's preliminary report on the Mahasthangarh excavations (p. 62;
Ji pp. 792–93). L. Joshi reads the name as *bhasuā*.
**Name.** 跋 is bh (並) and 始 is ś (書), so Julien's vāsibha fails on the sibilant and is weak on the initial; the
characters give *Bhāśiva or *Bhāśibha. The village name Bhāsu Bihār beside the ruins (ASR XV) keeps bh- and the
sibilant (ś in Bengali), so the name does survive, as the heading says. Low confidence for the form, which is not
attested in Sanskrit; the Life's 跋姞婆 looks like a graphic error for 始 (data/names/).
**Assessment.** Name, distance and a monastery–stūpa–temple group as described, and an excavation with Gupta-period
finds (reported by Ji from Ahmed). Secure, without an inscription; the excavation report itself is not seen here.

### 10.09.02a 西北山伽藍 (monastery on the hill NW of Puṣpagiri) — open; Udayagiri (Bhubaneswar) by analogy only, Jaina on excavation

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Udayagiri (Bhubaneswar). Cunningham (1871, p. 587): the Udayagiri and Khandagiri hills;
Beal (p. 205): tentative.
**Evidence.** It stands or falls with Puṣpagiri (10.09.02, now open between Udayagiri–Khandagiri and the Assia hills
N of the Mahānadī), and the Oḍra boundary N of the Mahānadī may exclude Bhubaneswar (10.09). The one building
excavated on the top of Udayagiri, an apsidal laterite shrine, D. Mitra judged Jaina, "as the Udayagiri and the
adjacent Khandagiri hills contain monuments of no other faith" (IAR 1958–59, pp. 38–40); nothing Buddhist was found.
The Udayagiri of the Assia hills (Jajpur) is another site, whose monastery its sealings name Madhavapura (IAR
1988–89, p. 67); the shared modern name proves nothing.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Udayagiri (p. 815).
**Assessment.** A pairing by analogy, now without Buddhist remains on the hill. Open; if Puṣpagiri lies in the Assia
hills, the NW hill must be sought there.

### 11.01 僧伽羅國 (Siṃhala) — Sri Lanka, capital Anurādhapura

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ceylon, capital Anurādhapura. Vivien (p. 399), Cunningham (1871, p. 637), Beal (p. 235),
Watters (p. 232) and Dey agree.
**Evidence.** Hearsay (the Life says so). Route test: 3,000 li SE by sea from Malakūṭa gives 306 km against about 717 km
predicted, bearing 10° off (flagged on distance), as expected of a sea passage.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Sri Lanka, capital Anurādhapura; hearsay (pp. 866–68).
**Assessment.** Secure as a country; the capital rests on agreement.

### 11.01.04a 摩訶毘訶羅 (Mahāvihāra) — the Mahāvihāra, S of Anurādhapura

Mizutani: the Mahāvihāra S of old Anurādhapura, its remains extant; Beal (p. 247): 7 li S of the capital. Watters (p. 235)
gives no site. The Life names neither fraternity. Agreement only; nothing in these sources.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: S of old Anurādhapura, with remains near the Ruvanveli stūpa (p. 879).

### 11.01.04b 阿跋耶祇釐 (Abhayagiri) — the Abhayagiri vihāra, N of Anurādhapura

Mizutani: Abhayagiri, N of Anurādhapura, founded by Vaṭṭagāmaṇi Abhaya. Beal (p. 247) cites the Dīpavaṃsa and Faxian.
Agreement only; nothing in these sources.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: N of the royal city, built by Vaṭṭagāmaṇi Abhaya (pp. 879–80).

### 11.04 跋祿羯呫婆國 (Bharukacchapa) — Broach (Bharuch) on the Narmadā; the Life's NW fits

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Broach. Julien (p. 154), Vivien (p. 400), Cunningham (1871, p. 374), Beal (p. 259),
Watters (p. 241), Majumdar (pp. 699–700) and Dey agree.
**Evidence.** Bombay 1909 repeats it with the local Bhṛgu legend. Route test: from Nasik (11.03) Broach lies at 336°,
66° off the Xiyu ji's "west" (flagged); the Life (T50:0243a) says NW, which fits. Distance fits. Soundara Rajan dug
the town-mound at Broach, "the ancient Bharukachchha" (IAR 1959–60, pp. 18–19): a walled town from c. the 3rd c. BC,
its Period II (Kṣatrapa coins, a brick-revetted rampart, a 6th–7th-c. caturmukha-liṅga) lasting "without break almost
to the seventh-eighth century A.D.". No Buddhist find.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Broach, and he too prefers the Life's NW (pp. 898–99).
**Assessment.** Agreement, the Life's bearing (which removes the only difficulty) and an excavated town occupied into
Xuanzang's time. Secure as the city; none of his monasteries is identified.

### 11.08 伐臘毘國 (Valabhī) — Vala near Bhavnagar

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Valabhī, capital at Vala. Cunningham (1871, pp. 363, 370), Majumdar (p. 697) and Dey: the
ruins at Vala. Vivien (p. 404): E Kathiawar. Watters (p. 246) rejects only Bhaonagar.
**Evidence.** Bombay 1909: "the buried city at Vala". Route test: mean |z| 0.5, bearing 29° off over three segments
(`candidate_scores.tsv`). The Life (T50:0243b) has the same 1,000 li N. The M. S. University, Baroda, dug at Vala
(Sonawane, Parekh and Momin, IAR 1979–80, p. 24): at Maya-no-Khado a Phase III of the 5th–8th centuries, covering the
Maitraka capital of Xuanzang's day, but with only beaten-earth floors and walls of reused bricks; no monastery and no
inscription naming the place.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees on Vala (18 miles NW of Bhavnagar, 21°52′N 71°57′E) and adds that
inscriptions, sealings and sculpture from the site, now in the Prince of Wales Museum, Bombay, prove an old city whose
description in the inscriptions matches Xuanzang's (p. 913).
**Assessment.** Agreement, a good route fit, and inscriptions and sealings from the site (reported by Ji). Secure.

### 11.12 鄔闍衍那國 (Ujjayanī) — Ujjain

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ujjain. Julien (p. 167), Vivien (p. 408), Cunningham (1871, p. 560), Beal (p. 270),
Majumdar (p. 726) and Dey agree; Watters (p. 250): "generally supposed".
**Evidence.** Rajputana 1908 and Jhalawar 1964 repeat it; Sircar (EI 34) keeps Ujjayinī distinct from Mālava. Route
test: 2,800 li SE from Bhinmal gives 410 km at 16° off, unflagged. The Life gives the same distance and the Aśoka "hell"
stūpa. N. R. Banerjee dug the Garh-Kalika mound on the N outskirts of Ujjain, "representing the ancient Ujjayini", and
found occupation without a break from before 600 BC to the 14th century (IAR 1956–57, p. 20), with a citadel rampart
and moat of the earliest period (1957–58, pp. 32–33) and a terracotta casket-lid of the 1st c. AD inscribed
"Nagabudhisa pravajitasa", of a Buddhist monk (pp. 34–36). The post-Gupta deposit (Period IV) was much disturbed and
gave nothing specific of Xuanzang's time, nor his monasteries or the "hell" stūpa.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Ujjain, capital of N Avanti (p. 923).
**Name.** 那 gives a final -na, so 鄔闍衍那 renders ujjayana, the first of Mizutani's forms, not the Sanskrit ujjayanī of
the heading (which names the city and stands); gemination is not marked. The identification is unaffected
(data/names/).
**Assessment.** Agreement, a name in continuous use, and an excavated city on the site occupied through Xuanzang's
time, with Buddhists there from the 1st c. AD. Secure as the city; his monuments are not identified.
