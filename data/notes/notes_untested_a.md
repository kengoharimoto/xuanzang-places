# Notes on places not yet assessed: juan 1 (set a)

Range: 34 ids in juan 1 that Mizutani marks `agreed` and that had no note (`untested_ids_a.txt`). None has a
gazetteer, ASR, Annual Report, *Epigraphia Indica*/CII or *IAR* hit (all counts 0): British Indian sources do not
reach Central Asia. The evidence is therefore the authorities, Mizutani's notes, the Life and the route tests.
Where an assessment says "secure on the name", the name survives but these files hold no site evidence, so the
confidence is `medium` by the rule that agreement alone is not `high`.

---

### 01.01a 殑伽河 (Skt. gaṅgā) — the Ganges; cosmological river with a surviving name

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Beal (p. 11), Watters (p. 31): the Ganges.
**Evidence.** Name continuous (Mizutani: Mvy. 3304); the river flows SE to the sea, as the text says. The Life
(T50:0232c) describes it at its source in Śrughna (cf. 04.09.03).
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the Ganges (p. 42).
**Assessment.** Secure: the name and course of an extant river match.

### 01.01b 信度河 (Skt. sindhu) — the Indus

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Beal (p. 12), Watters (p. 31), Dey: the Indus.
**Evidence.** Name continuous; flows SW to the sea. The Life (T50:0230c) has Xuanzang cross it at Udakhāṇḍa, 3–4 li
wide.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the Indus (p. 42).
**Assessment.** Secure: the name and course of an extant river match, as for the Ganges.

### 01.01c 縛芻河 (vakṣu) — the Amu Darya (Oxus)

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Vivien (p. 283), Beal (p. 12), Watters (p. 31), Dey: the Oxus/Amu Darya.
**Evidence.** Name continuous (waxšu, Vakhsh); flows NW. Xuanzang uses the same river as the axis of Tukhāra
(01.20.02) and crosses it; the Life (T50:0250b) joins the Pamir lake river to it.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees on the Oxus, but says that strictly 縛芻 is the Vakhsh and should be
distinguished from the Amu Darya formed by the Panj and the Vakhsh (p. 42). Xuanzang's usage is wider: the Pamir
lake's western stream joins the 縛芻 at Wakhan (12.17.02a), i.e. on the Panj.
**Name.** Of Mizutani's forms (Ind. cakṣu, sucakṣu, vakṣu), only vakṣu fits: 縛 is v-, and 芻 is kṣu (初, as kṣ always
is). The Purāṇic cakṣu is therefore not what Xuanzang wrote; he follows the vakṣu/waxšu form, which Sanskrit has as
vaṅkṣu (Raghuvaṃśa 4, vaṅkṣutīra). The Abhidharmakośa (III) names exactly Xuanzang's four rivers of Anavatapta,
gaṅgā sindhuḥ śītā vakṣuś ca, and Xuanzang marks the older 博叉 as 訛, i.e. corrects it to vakṣu. The form is secure,
and the identification is unaffected (data/names/).
**Assessment.** Secure.

### 01.05 阿耆尼國 (Agni) — the Karashahr oasis

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Karashahr region where the Yulduz enters Bagrash-kul. Vivien (p. 264), Beal (p. 17),
Watters (p. 47): Karashahr (= Yanqi).
**Evidence.** Name equation Agni = Argi/焉耆 (Mizutani, after Henning). The Life (T50:0226b) adds the Father Teacher
spring and the king's grudge against Gaochang. No site evidence in these sources.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Yanqi (Karashahr), bounded at Yushugou on the E and through the Tieguan
valley to Korla on the SW (Huang Wenbi 1958); he adds the later Uighur name Solmi (Geng Shimin and Zhang Guangda,
唆里迷考, 1980) (p. 49).
**Name.** 阿耆尼 is a-gi-ni by the key, with no r: Xuanzang writes a pre-consonantal r with a -t coda (伐 var, 達 dhar), so
the local Saka Argiña, Tocharian *Arkeñe and the old 焉耆 (Argi) are not what he wrote. The characters give Skt. Agni, a
learned Sanskritisation of the local name; the Uighur Maitrisimit colophon's Aknidiš < Agnideśa for Yanqi (Müller and
Henning, reported by Ji p. 51) shows the Sanskrit name in Buddhist use. Medium confidence; the identification is
unaffected (data/names/).
**Recent scholarship.** The 18th-century "present name" gloss in the Ōtani manuscript of the Tibetan Xiyu ji reads
Aksu (ak sū); Takata (2020, p. 354) shows it is an error, prompted by the translator's spelling u shi ri (Uch), and
puts Agni at Karashahr. No weight.
**Assessment.** Probable to secure on the name and the Tang equation; the authorities agree. Mizutani stands.

### 01.05a 阿耆尼國大都城 — Stein's Bagdad-shahr south of Karashahr; only one proposal

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Bagdad-shahr S of Karashahr (Stein). Watters: somewhat W of Karashahr, among several
ruins (p. 47, tentative).
**Evidence.** None beyond Stein's identification as reported by Mizutani; no description or measurement of the ruin
against the 6–7 li circuit. The Life puts the capital on a river bank.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji reports two sites without choosing: Stein's Bagdad-shahr S of Yanqi and Huang Wenbi's
Tang capital 4 li E of 四十里城子 in Yanqi county (*Talimu pendi kaogu ji*, 1958) (p. 49).
**Assessment.** Open: two proposals, Stein's Bagdad-shahr and Huang Wenbi's site E of 四十里城子 (via Ji), and Watters
looks to the west; neither is measured against the 6–7 li circuit in these sources. Stein's site stays on the map.

### 01.06 屈支國 (Kuci) — Kucha

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Julien, Vivien (p. 265), Beal (p. 19), Watters (p. 58): Kucha.
**Evidence.** Name Kuci/kuči (Lüders, Pelliot, via Mizutani); Mizutani lists the Kumtura, Qyzil and Subashi sites.
Route test: from Karashahr 900 li to 301 km (0.335 km/li), within range. The Life (T50:0226c) records the reception
by the king and Mokṣagupta.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees, and adds the capital (Han 延城, Tang 伊邏廬城): probably the 皮朗 old city near
Kucha, without evidence given (p. 56).
**Recent scholarship.** Zhang and Zhu (2019, p. 146) take the capital for the Pilang old city (皮朗古城), citing the brief
report on the 2016 excavation of the Kucha old city (*Xinjiang wenwu* 2017(2), not read here); they give no evidence
of their own. The Xinjiang Institute's 2025 Youyilu cemetery excavation (project summary, Wang Yongqiang; pdf pp. 1–2)
sectioned the E wall of the Kucha old city (龟兹故城) under modern Kuqa (rammed earth, 8.5 m wide and 1.98 m high as
preserved) and its moat (3 m deep, 8 m wide), beside 510 tombs of the Warring States, Wei-Jin and Tang periods. The
Tang brick tomb M138 has the epitaph of 尹公, a commissioner of the Four Garrisons, buried "都护府城东乌垒原", east of the
Protectorate city: so the walled old city was the Tang Anxi seat at Kucha. The wall is not dated in the summary. The
18th-century Tibetan Xiyu ji glosses 屈支 correctly as khu ṭe (Kuche) (Takata 2020, p. 354).
**Assessment.** Secure as a district on the name. For the capital there is now Tang site evidence: an epitaph places
the Anxi Protectorate city at the walled Kucha old city (Pilang) under modern Kuqa (Youyilu 2025), where Ji and Zhang
and Zhu put the capital; that it is the city of 630 is likely but not shown. Mizutani stands.

### 01.06.02 昭怙釐伽藍 — the twin Subashi monasteries astride the river NNE of Kucha

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the temple ruins on both banks of the 銅廠河 near Subashi, c. 20 km NNE of Kucha
(Stein, *Innermost Asia* p. 806; Pelliot: Toch. *coguri*). Beal (p. 21), Watters (p. 62): none.
**Evidence.** Xuanzang's two monasteries, East and West, separated by a river 40+ li N of the deserted city, match
the paired standing ruins on either bank. The Chinese name is explained from Tocharian.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: ruins on both banks of the 銅廠河 at Subashi, N of Kucha (p. 61).
**Assessment.** Secure: standing ruins matching the description, with no rival proposal.

### 01.06.04 阿奢理貳伽藍 (āścarya) — Duldur-Aqur opposite Kumtura, or the Kizilgaha caves NW of Kucha (open)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Duldur-Aqur, opposite Kumtura (Akiyama). Beal (p. 22), Watters (p. 63): none.
**Evidence.** Pelliot found a library of Tocharian B documents there (Mizutani's note); nothing in these files ties
the ruin to the name. The Life (T50:0226c, 阿奢理兒寺) puts it NW of the city, consistent with the Xiyu ji.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Douldour-agour across the Kumtura river W of Kucha, after Pelliot (p. 64).
**Recent scholarship.** Zhang and Zhu (2019, pp. 146–148), from the third national survey of immovable relics and
their own fieldwork, put the monastery at the Kizilgaha caves (克孜尔尕哈石窟, the place of worship) with the Bozkhana temple
site (博其罕那佛寺, the living quarters), c. 10 km NW of the Pilang old city, Kucha's capital. Shahetur (夏合吐尔, the
Duldur-Aqur ruin) lies 30 km SW of the capital, against the Xiyu ji's NW of the assembly place and the Life's NW of
the city; the river crossed is the Kucha river, and the king's visit before noon and the send-off by the whole city
want a monastery near the capital. At Kizilgaha a Kuchean inscription in cave 25 records a visit by king Suvarṇadeva
(苏伐叠, r. 624–646) in his 19th year, and caves 11, 13 and 14 show the royal family (p. 147): royal worship in
Xuanzang's time, but not the monastery's name.
**Assessment.** Open between two candidates, each argued from position: Duldur-Aqur across the Muzat W of Kucha
(Pelliot, Akiyama, Mizutani, Ji), where Pelliot found a Tocharian B library, and the Kizilgaha caves with Bozkhana NW
of the city (Zhang and Zhu 2019), which fits the bearing better and has royal inscriptions of Xuanzang's time. Neither
site has an inscription naming the monastery. Duldur-Aqur stays on the map, open.

### 01.07.02 大清池 (熱海) — Issyk-kul

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Vivien (p. 267), Beal (p. 25), Watters (p. 67), Cunningham (p. 645): Issyk-kul.
**Evidence.** The alternative names 熱海 "hot sea" and 鹹海 translate Issyk-kul "warm lake"/Tuz-kul; the lake is long
E–W, salty and ringed by mountains, as described. The Life (T50:0227a) gives 1,400–1,500 li round.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Issyk-kul (p. 70).
**Recent scholarship.** The 18th-century "present name" gloss of the Tibetan Xiyu ji, to si khe la, is Kyrgyz Tuz-köl
"salt lake", a local name of Issyk-kul also in the Qing *Xiyu tongwen zhi* (圖斯庫勒) (Takata 2020, pp. 354–355): a
correct gloss, matching the text's own 鹹海.
**Assessment.** Secure: the extant lake matches the description and the name.

### 01.07.04 窣利 (Suɣδ) — Sogdiana, as Xuanzang defines it from Suyab to Kesh

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Sogdiana, extended NE to Suyab by the Sogdian trade colonies; the rival views
(Chavannes, Shiratori, Watters) concern the form of the name, not the region.
**Evidence.** Name Soghd. suɣδ; Xuanzang defines the region himself. The Life has no general account.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees on Sogdiana, but explains the wide extent from Suyab to Kesh by older usage,
the Sughd of the Muslim period being much narrower, where Mizutani invokes the Sogdian colonies (pp. 73–74).
**Assessment.** A definitional region; the agreement is complete. Mizutani stands.

### 01.09 赭時國 (Chāch, "stone") — Tashkent

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Vivien (p. 276), Beal (p. 30), Watters (p. 85): Tashkent.
**Evidence.** Name Chāch/Shāsh = "stone", translated 石國. Route test: from Nujkath 200 li to 34 km, short but
within range. The Life (T50:0227c) names it only.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees, and puts the old town of Chach at Binkath near the Chirchik, with its plan in
Belenitskii et al. (1973) (p. 83).
**Recent scholarship.** The chief town of Chach in the 7th–8th century is disputed. Mukhamedov (2023, p. 40) reports
the views (after Buryakov and others): Kanka earlier and Mingurik (in Tashkent) later as capitals; or Kanka the
capital and Mingurik a fortress; or Kanka the seat of the Chach tegins, Mingurik, Tarband and Tunkat of the tuduns;
the yabghu's camp Jabgukat at the Aq-ota ruin NE of Tashkent. Kubaev and Yusupova (2024, pp. 58, 65) report Kanka (70
km SW of Tashkent, occupied 4th c. BC–12th c. AD) as Kharashkat and the "Shi-Zheshi" of the Chinese sources; their
2021 excavation found the city temple of the 3rd–6th century levelled in the 8th. Neither discusses Xuanzang.
**Assessment.** Secure as a district on the name; no site evidence. The town Xuanzang saw is open among Kanka,
Mingurik in Tashkent and Ji's Binkath; none is argued from his text. Mizutani stands.

### 01.09a 葉河 (Yaxartes) — the Syr Darya

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Julien (p. 16), Beal (p. 30), Watters (p. 85): Syr Darya (Jaxartes).
**Evidence.** Name Iaxartēs (Strabo); rises in the northern Pamir plateau and flows NW, as the text says. The Life
(葉葉河) has Chāch to its east and Sutrūṣṇa to its west.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the Jaxartes (pp. 83–84).
**Recent scholarship.** The 18th-century "present name" gloss of the Tibetan Xiyu ji at 東臨葉河 reads ta la' si
(Taraz); Takata (2020, p. 355) shows it is wrong: the 葉河 is the Syr Darya, and Taraz lies over 300 km from it.
**Assessment.** Secure.

### 01.11 窣堵利瑟那國 (Sutrūšna) — Usrūshana; capital Shahristan or Ura-tyube

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Usrūshana, capital Bunjikath = Shahristan or Ura-tyube itself. Vivien (p. 279), Beal
(p. 31), Watters (p. 88): Ura-tyube.
**Evidence.** Name continuous (Arab. Usrūshana, Sutrūshna). Route test: from Ferghana W 1,000 li to 275 km, within
range. The Life counts the 1,000 li from Chāch.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji: Usrūshana, between the Hissar and Zarafshan ranges at the W outlet of Ferghana; he
gives its chief town Bunjikath as Panjikent, 60 km E of Samarkand, and reports Barthold's old town at Ura-Tübe (pp.
86–87). Bunjikath of Usrūshana and Panjikent in Sogd are different towns; the Mug documents and wall paintings Ji
cites belong to Panjikent and do not bear on Usrūshana.
**Name.** The characters write s-t-r-š-n: 窣堵 is stu/sutu, 利 ri/ru, and 瑟 the closed š (生) before 那 na. Of the Arabic
forms only Sutrūshana/Sutrūshnah has the t (Le Strange p. 474; Barthold "Ushrusana or Sutrūshana"); Surūshna and
Usrūshana lack it, as does Minorsky's *Surushna (Ḥudūd comm. p. 354). The Sui 蘇對沙那 and Tang 率都沙那 agree. Mizutani's
"Skt. sutṛṣṇa" is a pseudo-Sanskrit reading, and Ji's "Sutrena" is unsupported. Sutrūšna is secure (high confidence
for the form); the identification is unaffected (data/names/).
**Assessment.** The country is secure; the capital is open between Shahristan and Ura-tyube, c. 20 km apart.
Mizutani stands.

### 01.19 貨利習彌伽國 (Khwārizm) — Khwarizm on the lower Oxus; the route figures do not fit

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Khwarizm, both banks of the lower Amu Darya. Vivien (p. 283), Beal (p. 35): Khwarazm.
Watters (p. 98), Dey: Khiva, which lies inside Khwarizm.
**Evidence.** Name continuous (Chorasmia). Route test: from Vadi (01.18) SW 500 li gives 371 km against 120

**Assessment.** The identification is secure on the name; the figures are wrong, not the place (the stage may be

### 01.20 羯霜那國 (Kesh, 史國) — Shahr-i-sabz

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Kesh = Shahr-i-sabz, 65 km SSW of Samarkand on the Kashka darya. Vivien (p. 283),
Beal (p. 26), Watters (p. 100): Kesh/Shahr-i-sabz.
**Evidence.** Name continuous (Kish/Kesh). Route test: from Samarkand SW 300 li to 67 km (predicted 72 km), within range
on distance (a calibration segment), but bearing 35° off. The Life chains it from Khwarizm.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Shahr-i Sabz, 75 km S of Samarkand (p. 98).
**Name.** 羯 ka(t), 霜 šā(ŋ), 那 na. Of Mizutani's forms, Kassārin, Kassabīn and Kazurān do not fit (no r or b, and 那 is
an n-final syllable) and are not Kesh at all: they are the canal Nahr al-Kassārīn / Rūd-i Kazurān at Samarkand
(Barthold, *Turkestan*; Le Strange). The name is Kišš/Kiss (Iṣṭakhrī Kiss, Muqaddasī Kishsh), which Marquart already
equated with 羯霜那 (Ērānšahr, after Specht); the characters write an n-extended *Kašan(a), not attested. Medium
confidence; the identification is unaffected (data/names/).
**Assessment.** Secure on the name. Mizutani stands.

**Assessment.** Secure: an extant defile matching the description, the historical name, and all authorities.

### 01.20.02 覩貨邏國 (Tukhāra) — Tokharistan; extent disputed, region not

**Identifications.** Mizutani: northern Afghanistan between the Pamirs, Persia, the Iron Gate and the Hindu Kush.
Vivien (p. 285), Beal (p. 37), Watters (p. 105), Dey: Tokharistan of the Arab geographers.
**Evidence.** Name continuous (Tochari, Tukhāra). Xuanzang gives its bounds himself.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Tukhāra under the Western Turkish yabghu, in which Xuanzang describes 29
states (p. 102).
**Name.** 貨 is 曉 (x/kh), not ṣ, so of Mizutani's forms Tukhāra fits and the epic Tuṣāra does not; Xuanzang follows the
Bactrian pronunciation with x, as in his other Iranian names (Indic kh would be 佉). The form is secure, and the
identification is unaffected (data/names/). Bactrian: Sims-Williams' τοχοαραστανο "Tukhwarastan" (BDNA II p. 270)
rests on *Toxwār- "Tocharian" (Sogd. ʾtxwʾrk), and 貨 is 合口 (xwā, as in 貨利習彌伽 Khwārizm), so 覩貨邏 writes *Tu-xwā-ra with
the w that Skt. Tukhāra lacks. Xuanzang heard the Iranian *Tuxwār, and Tukhāra is the Sanskritised form; this concerns
the name only (data/names/bactrian.tsv).
**Kuwayama (2002).** In Sui and early Tang usage 吐火羅 was a district within the wider Tokharistan of the Arab
geographers, the lower Kunduz–Surkhāb plain round 活, the Turkish yabghu's winter seat, which Xuanzang rightly calls 活
(pp. 133, 135). Xuanzang's 覩貨邏國 with its own bounds is the wider region, so the two usages differ in extent only.
**Recent scholarship.** Iwai (2004, pdf pp. 6, 13) finds the pottery sequence changing in step N and S of the Amu
(Chaqalak Tepe; Dalverzin Tepa) from the late 3rd century, archaeological support for Xuanzang's two-bank 覩貨邏;
Sogdian-type wares appear only N of the river from the 7th century, so the Islamic south-bank Tokharistan reflects a
later split. Kuwayama (2020, p. 125) counts Xuanzang's list as 8 territories N of the Amu (Termez to Kumedh) and 21 S
of it, 29 in all; without the three W and SW of Balkh (鋭秣陀, 胡寔健, 呾剌健), which he puts outside Tokhāra, 26, near the 24
or 25 州 of the Tang 月氏都督府. Miyamoto (2015, p. 314) instead takes Xuanzang's Tokharistan for the Bactrian-speaking area
(the 25-letter script read left to right is Bactrian, and Bactrian documents were written in Gūzgān), reaching W of
Balkh to Gūzgān. Miyamoto (2018, pp. 47, 53) identifies the 27 君長 with the Bactrian khar: one khar's domain is one
"country" (see 01.30).
**Assessment.** The region is secure, on both banks of the Amu as Xuanzang has it, now with Iwai's ceramic evidence
for one area. Its western limit stays disputed: near Balkh (Kuwayama 2020) or including Gūzgān (Miyamoto 2015).
Mizutani stands.

### 01.21 呾蜜國 (Tarmidh) — Termez; hearsay

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Vivien (p. 290), Beal (p. 38), Watters (p. 105): Termez, on the north bank of the
Oxus.
**Evidence.** Name continuous; "down the Oxus on its north bank" fits. Not in the Life's itinerary, hence hearsay
(Mizutani). The Buddhist sites excavated at Old Termez are reported by Ji (below).
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees and adds site evidence: the excavations of 1964–66 at Old Termez, near the
mouth of the Surkhan, found a Greek fort, Kushan stūpas, the rock-cut cave monastery of Kara-Tepe and sherds with
Brāhmī and Kharoṣṭhī inscriptions; he cites Knobloch, *Beyond the Oxus* (1972) (p. 104).
**Name.** Bactrian: Tarmid, in ταρμιδδιγο "(inhabitant) of Tarmid" (BDNA II p. 269), with "a native of Tarmid" in doc.
Q (year 449 = c. 672; BDNA I p. 88). 呾蜜 tar-mid matches it syllable for syllable, closer than Arabic Tirmidh; the
identification is unaffected (data/names/bactrian.tsv).
**Recent scholarship.** Kuwayama (2020, pp. 123–24) reads 順縛芻河北下流至呾蜜國 as the road from Derbent down the Surkhan Darya
to Termez, but takes the Life, which omits Termez and has Xuanzang cross the Oxus and reach 活 several hundred li on,
probably by boat, for the actual itinerary (12.05). The identification is unaffected.
**Assessment.** Secure: the name is continuous, and the Buddhist monasteries excavated at Old Termez (Kara-Tepe, the
Kushan stūpas) match the text's "more than ten monasteries". Mizutani stands.

### 01.29 縛伽浪國 (Baghlān) — Baghlan in the Kunduz river valley

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Baghlān, c. 60 km SW of Kunduz. Vivien (p. 288): the Ghori valley between Baghlan and
Kunduz. Beal (p. 43): Baghlan, c. 34 miles S of Kunduz.

**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the middle Doshi, c. 60 km SW of Kunduz (p. 113).

under the Turks in Xuanzang's time; their Tang seat 活路 (Huoluo) was a town there, not 活 (pp. 132, 135, 138). Agrees on
the district.
**Recent scholarship.** Miyamoto (2012, pp. 427, 431) reports Grenet's suggestion that Warlu/活路 is Qal'a-ye Ghūrī, an
unexcavated walled town site S of Surkh Kotal, and widens Kadagstan to the Surkhāb basin, its centre perhaps moving N
and S over time. This concerns the district round 縛伽浪, not its name.
**Assessment.** Secure as a district on the name. Mizutani stands.

### 01.32 縛喝國 (Bākhl) — Balkh

### 01.32.01 納縛僧伽藍 (Nava-saṃghārāma) — the Nau-bahār of Balkh; stupa probably Top-i-Rustam

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Nau-bahār of the Islamic geographers; its 200-ft stupa probably Top-i-Rustam,
excavated by Foucher. Beal (p. 44), Watters (p. 110): none.
**Evidence.** The name survives as Nau-bahār (Nava-vihāra) at Balkh in the Islamic sources. Foucher's excavation is
reported by Mizutani, without a find naming the monastery. The Life (T50:0228b) has the same relics.
**Foucher (1942–47).** The study's identification is Foucher's. His excavation (28 January–14 May 1924) proved
Tōp-é-Rustam, SW of the old city on the road out of the Nau-behār gate and the only tōp there, to be a Buddhist stūpa
restored before 630. He admits that the monastery and the vihāra are not found and that the layout does not match the

**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji gives the Nau-Vihār of the Arab geographers (Yijing's 納婆毗訶羅) but no site;
Top-i-Rustam is not mentioned (p. 118).
**Assessment.** The monastery is secure by name at Balkh. Its stūpa is probably Top-i-Rustam: Foucher's excavation
shows a large Buddhist stūpa in use in Xuanzang's time in the right quarter, though nothing names it and the monastery
has not been found. Mizutani is strengthened.

### 01.33 鋭秣陀國 — Sar-i-pul, on Beal's tentative suggestion only

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Sar-i-pul on the Ab-i-Safid, c. 100 km SW of Balkh (Islamic Anbār), after Beal.
Beal (p. 48): near Sir-i-pul (tentative). Vivien (p. 290): no synonym; a small mountain country SW of Balkh. Watters
(p. 113): none.
**Evidence.** The original name is unknown. The Life (T50:0228b, 銳末陀) has Xuanzang visit it at its king's
invitation, with Gūzgān, SW of Balkh. No figures.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji leaves the site open, giving only Marquart's restoration Zumathān (pp. 125–126).
**Name.** 鋭秣陀 gives *Yu-mar-dha: 鋭 is y- (以), 秣 mar/mat (明 + -t, as in 颯秣建 Samarkand), 陀 d/dh. Marquart's Zumathān
(Ērānšahr p. 227, reported by Ji p. 125; the Arabic is unreadable in the OCR of Ērānšahr) fits -math- and the position
on the Hindu Kush slopes S of the Oxus, but y- for z breaks the key (z is 習 or 寔), and the final -ān is not written.
No *Yumar- or *Zumar- was found in the Ḥudūd, Barthold or Le Strange. Zumathān is a weak alternative, low confidence;
the name gives no support to Sar-i-pul (data/names/). Bactrian: the city at Sar-i Pul was Amber (αμβηρο, "Amber in
early Islamic sources (modern Sar-i Pul)", BDNA II p. 191; doc. Ss, not edited in BDNA I–II), Mizutani's Islamic
Anbār, a name with an initial vowel and -mb- that 鋭秣陀 cannot render. So either Xuanzang names a different unit or
Sar-i Pul is wrong. 秣陀 might render Bactrian Madr (μαλρο/μαδδρο, "a city and district, modern Madr", BDNA II p. 230,
home of a servant of the khars of Rob in doc. N), but 鋭 stays unexplained and the look-alike is not adopted
(data/names/bactrian.tsv).
**Recent scholarship.** Miyamoto (2015, p. 315) leaves 鋭秣陀 unidentified, and puts the centre of Gūzgān (01.34) at
Sar-i Pul, the Tang 遏蜜城 (pp. 312, 291), which adds to the case against Sar-i-pul here. The *Xin Tangshu* passage he
quotes (p. 291 n. 66) sets the 大秦州 of the same Gūzgān commandery at a 叡蜜城; 叡 sounds like the 鋭 of 鋭秣陀, a lead not
tested here. Sims-Williams (2002, p. 1050) confirms by Bivar's Arabic inscription near Sar-i Pul that the town was
Ambēr, where a Bactrian contract of 708 (his reckoning) was written: the name 鋭秣陀 would have to render is settled.
**Assessment.** Open, and Sar-i-pul is weakened: the position (SW of Balkh) is agreed, but Sar-i-pul rests on one
tentative suggestion, and its own 7th-century name, Bactrian Amber (Islamic Anbār), is not what 鋭秣陀 transcribes (see
Name). If Sar-i Pul was the seat of Gūzgān (Miyamoto 2015; 01.34), it is the less likely here. Marquart's Zumathān
is weak and unlocated. Mizutani's site is kept as the working point only.

### 01.36 揭職國 (*Garčig, "mountain land") — the mountains S of Balkh (Darrah Gaz); the mapped point is too far south

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Darrah Gaz (Tamaris valley), S of Balkh up the Darrah Yusuf (Beal, Watters, Foucher).
Beal (p. 48): Gaz or Darah-Gaz, c. 20 miles S of Balkh. Vivien (p. 293), Watters (p. 115): none.
**Evidence.** Name Gaz/Gachi. Route test: Mizutani's point (placed in Dara-i-Suf, 94 km from Balkh) gives z +2.8 for
"S 100 li"; Beal's 20 miles (32 km) gives z +0.6, bearing 0°, and is the best-fitting choice for juan 1
(chain_best). Onward to Bamiyan both fit (z −0.1 and +0.7). The Life gives no figure.
**Foucher (1942–47).** Mizutani's source: Xuanzang's road went up the Balkh-āb and the Darrah-Yūsuf to the Qarā-kotal,
and Kie-tche is the Darrah-Gāz ("Vallée des Tamaris"), with the "ten monasteries" along the Balkh-āb; he visited none
of them (I, pp. 124, 129).
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji: some put it at Darrah Gaz, but "there is as yet no strong evidence" (p. 128). He
adds doubt, not a rival.
**Name.** The name is not Gaz. 揭 has the -t coda that renders r, and 職 is tɕik, so the characters give *Garčig
(Pelliot: *Karcik or *Kacik). Sims-Williams (CRAI 2002 [2004], p. 1054 n. 21) calls the equation with Gaz "impossible
phonologiquement" and accepts Yoshida's identification of 揭職 with Bactrian γαρσιγο, the base of γαρσιγοστανο
"Gharchistan" (Yoshida 1998, pp. 35–36, n. 11). The word means "mountain land", and in the Bactrian documents ("the
judge of Tukharistan and Gharchistan") it seems to name the whole mountain zone south of Tokharistan, which fits
Xuanzang's placing of 揭職 at the southern edge of Tokharistan (600 li beyond it he 出都貨邏國境; Yoshida's "southern border
of Tokharistan" paraphrases this, it is not the text's wording); Gharchistan proper lay further west, as Yoshida
admits. Ji (1985, p. 128) had already reported Pelliot's doubt. The Bactrian fortress Gaz (docs. R, c. 675, and S, c.
693, BDNA I pp. 92, 94) is a different name: Sims-Williams places it "in the district of Andar", either near Dar-i
Andara by Maymana or at the Darra Gaz / Jazzah of the Islamic sources, a fort on Guzgan's eastern edge 30 miles S of
Balkh (CRAI pp. 1053–54). Data: data/names/bactrian.tsv; texts in data/ref/simswilliams/.
**Assessment.** The place is the mountain country S of Balkh on the road to Bāmiyān, as all agree, and the 100 li
puts Xuanzang's halt near the valley mouth c. 30 km S of Balkh, where Beal has it, not 94 km up the valley. The name,
however, is *Garčig, "mountain land" (Yoshida, Sims-Williams), a district name, not the village name Gaz, so the
name no longer points to Darrah Gaz in particular. Probable for the region; the point near the mouth of the Darrah
Gaz stays as an approximation.

### 01.37 梵衍那國 (Bāmiyān) — the Bamiyan valley

**Identifications.** Mizutani, Vivien (p. 292), Beal (p. 49), Cunningham (pp. 22–23): Bamiyan. Watters (p. 115):
Bamiyan, the capital perhaps partly at Ghulghula.
**Evidence.** Name continuous; the standing colossi (01.37.01, 01.37.01a) match Xuanzang's description. Route test:
from Gaz SE 600 li to 138 km, within range. The Life (T50:0228c) has the Lokottaravādin monks and the hard snow
road.
**Foucher (1942–47).** Visits of 1922 and 1923: the old town began c. 1.5 km before the 53 m Buddha, astride the road
and the river, with the Buddha cliff to its NE (I, pp. 132, 134).
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: Bamiyan town, c. 150 miles W of Kabul (p. 130).
**Name.** Bactrian: Bamyan (βαμοιανο, "a city and district, modern Bamiyan", BDNA II p. 201), named beside Rob as a
neighbouring power ("of Rob or of Bamyan", doc. U, BDNA I p. 110) and as a captured "fortress Bamyan" in a letter. Its
-y- is that of 衍 in 梵衍那 bam-yan-a, against MP Bāmikān; the identification is unaffected (data/names/bactrian.tsv).
**Kuwayama (1990, 2002).** Bāmiyān; Xuanzang's 東西二千餘里，南北三百餘里 is the basin closed by the Hazārajāt (W), the Shibar pass
(E), the Tokharan states (N) and the Koh-i-Baba (S) (1990, p. 1442). The first Chinese record is the 帆延 of the
*Suishu* and of Pei Ju's 西域圖記 (c. 606); the 范陽 of the *Weishu*/*Beishi* 吐火羅 chapter, long taken for Bāmiyān, is a Tang
name in Badakhshan (the 范湯州 of the 月氏都督府 at 拔特山城, Huichao's 蒲特山), so nothing on Bāmiyān reached China before the
mid-6th c. (1990, pp. 1425–30; 2002, p. 149; p. 157 explains the slip differently). If the two standing colossi are
Xuanzang's, the royal city lay SW of the West Buddha, and nothing there is worth a capital (現存西大佛の西南には都城に値する遺迹は何もない,
1990, p. 1435); the Islamic geographers' town on a hill could be Shahr-e Gholghola or Shahr-e Zuhak if Xuanzang's
bearing is set aside (2002, pp. 157–58).
**Recent scholarship.** Tarzi (2007, pp. 884, 918–19) reads 王城東北 for the 55 m Buddha as putting the royal city W and
SW of it, "adossée à la montagne", partly rock-cut and partly built, and dug there. At VR 1 (an old caravanserai W of
the bazaar) cave dwellings give way to built houses, with pre-Islamic "Turkish" pottery (6th–9th c.) under
Samanid–Ghaznavid and Ghurid levels; at VR 2 (Sorx Qol, W of the 55 m niche at the foot of the cliff) he reached a
glass-makers' quarter built extra muros against what he takes for the rampart of the pre-Islamic royal city, occupied
from the late Turkish to the Ghurid period. The rampart itself is not dated there. The Ghurid capital (1155–1221) ran
from the present airport to Tape Almās, with Gholghola as its citadel (p. 923).
**Assessment.** Secure for the country. The capital's site is open: Foucher put the old town astride the road and
river SW of the colossi, Kuwayama finds nothing of a capital's size there, and Ghulghula and Zuhak are the other
candidates. Tarzi's soundings W of the 55 m niche, a rampart with occupation against it from the late pre-Islamic
period, favour Foucher's SW town but do not show that it stood there in 630; Tarzi gives Ghulghula only as the Ghurid
citadel. Mizutani stands.

### 01.37.01 立佛石像 — the western (53 m) Buddha at Bamiyan

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the 53 m Buddha. Vivien (p. 292), Beal (p. 51): the Bamiyan colossi. Watters
(p. 118): Talbot's 173-ft image. Majumdar (pp. 673–74): the largest, 180 ft.
**Evidence.** A rock-cut standing Buddha NE of the royal city, 140–150 chi high (the Life: 150): the larger colossus
matches in form, position and size. Later work (not in these sources): destroyed in 2001.
**Foucher (1942–47).** Agrees: the 53 m Buddha, NE of the town (I, pp. 132, 134).
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the 53 m Buddha, after Godard and Hackin (p. 131).
**Kuwayama (1990, 2002).** Agrees: the West Buddha (55 m by his measure), NE of the royal city, a rough core cut in
the conglomerate and coated with straw-tempered clay, the drapery modelled on pegs and ropes (1990, pp. 1437–38; 2002,
p. 159). Date: no text records the making; the colossi belong to Bāmiyān's prosperity on the road over the western
Hindu Kush, opened around the mid-6th c., and the 先王's reclining-Buddha monastery between them (01.37.02) to the king
just before Xuanzang's host, late 6th c. (1990, pp. 1417–25, 1439–44, 1452–53; 2002, pp. 155, 159). He reports and
sets aside Carter's view (1985) that the 55 m Buddha was made after Xuanzang (2002, p. 159).
**Recent scholarship.** The ICOMOS team dated 21 organic samples from the rubble by AMS radiocarbon (Blänsdorf et al.
in Petzet ed. 2009, pp. 231–35): chaff from the clay plaster, the ropes and pegs of the drapery armature and the
anchoring beams agree, so the clay finish is original, and for the West Buddha they give 591–644 (2σ; 1σ 605–633), the
East Buddha being c. 50 years older (544–595). The stone core cannot be dated. The West Buddha was thus finished
shortly before Xuanzang's visit c. 630, or at the latest a few years after it at 2σ. Their paint study (Blänsdorf,
Pfeffer, Melzl, ibid., pp. 237, 261–63) finds the West Buddha's robe painted pink, then minium, lead white and red
iron oxide, and no gilding or metal foil on the samples (mostly robe), so the 金色晃曜 is not traced on the surviving
surface. Tarzi (2007, pp. 886, 921) calls the identification "à cent pour cent sûr" and puts the statue in Bāmiyān's
second period, from the second half of the 6th c.
**Assessment.** Secure: a standing monument matching the description, with all authorities agreeing. Kuwayama's date
(after the mid-6th c.) concerns the monument, not the identification; the radiocarbon dates of the clay finish
(591–644) agree with it and make Carter's post-Xuanzang date unlikely without excluding it.

### 01.38.05 舊王伽藍 — the Old Royal monastery: Burj-i-Abdallah at the N end of Begram

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Burj-i-Abdallah enclosure at the N end of Begram, Foucher's "old city". Beal
(p. 67): on the S bank of the river NW of the capital, perhaps the Ghorband (tentative).
**Evidence.** Xuanzang: NW of the royal city, S bank of the great river. With the capital at Begram (01.38a), the
Ghorband position matches Beal's reading. Mizutani locates the area, not a monastery ruin; no excavation here
identifies it.
**Ji Xianlin et al. (1985).** Ji agrees: the 100 × 200 m enclosure at the N end of the Begram town site (p. 155).
**Kuwayama (1990, 2002).** NW of the city on the S bank of the Panjshir and close to it, since Xuanzang gives no
distance (1990, p. 749; 2002, p. 162, p. 278 n. 9); no ruin chosen. He takes it for the 王寺 "built by the king of
Kashgar" (沙勒王之所造也) outside the capital where Dharmagupta first stayed (Xu gaoseng zhuan): the royal monastery had
become the "old king's" by 629, perhaps after a change of dynasty (1990, pp. 1518–19, ch. 3 n. 1). The skull-bone
relic that the Xiyu ji places in the second 舊王伽藍 is in a temple inside the city for Daoxuan's 西國志 and in the city's
沙糸寺 for Huichao; it may have been moved in (1990, p. 1195).
**Assessment.** Probable as an area, dependent on Begram; the building itself is not identified. Kuwayama's reading of
the text (S bank, close to the city) supports the area, and his link with Dharmagupta's 王寺 gives the monastery a
6th-century history, not a site. Mizutani stands.
