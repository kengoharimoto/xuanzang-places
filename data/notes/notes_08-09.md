# Notes on disputed places: juan 8–9 (Magadha)

Places in juan 8–9 that meet at least one of the criteria in `NOTES_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, in master-table order.
Gazetteer volumes: HU11 = Hunter, *Statistical Account* Patna, Saran (1877); HU12 = Hunter, Gaya, Shahabad (1877);
BG26 = Patna District Gazetteer (1907); BG27 = Gaya District Gazetteer (O'Malley 1906); RV24 = Gaya revised edition
(1957, which reprints much of 1906); BG33 = Monghyr (1909); RV27 = Monghyr revised (1960). Most volumes exist in two
scans; counts below are of volumes and passages, not hits.

## Juan 8

### 08.02.03 八萬四千塔 (無憂王堵波) — no site proposed anywhere

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no modern site; the stūpa is placed only relative to the prison pillar, and he notes
that Faxian puts Aśoka's first great stūpa 3-odd li south of the city, which does not match Xuanzang (unknown). None of
the seven authorities gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** None of the Patna volumes identifies this stūpa. BG26 (Patna 1907) does place two other Aśokan sites
nearby, Aśoka's "hell" at the Agam Kuan well and the footprint stone at Bulandi Bagh (see New places below). These
could give fixed points for the pillar and the stūpa, but the volume does not take that step.

**Assessment.** Still unidentified. The gazetteers do not change Mizutani's position.

### 08.03.01 屈屈吒阿濫摩僧伽藍 (Skt. kurkuṭa-ārāma) — unidentified; only "near Pāṭaliputra"

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no modern site, only SE of old Pāṭaliputra; he sets aside the southern tradition
that equates it with the Aśokārāma of the "third council" (unknown). Beal (p. 142): the "cock-garden" near Patna, to be
kept apart from Kukkuṭapādagiri near Gayā. Dey: in the Upakaṇṭhika garden on the right bank of the Ganges at
Pāṭaliputra. Neither names a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. No Patna volume names the Kukkuṭārāma.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. The only point in these files is Beal's warning against confusing it with Kukkuṭapāda
(09.01.03). No change.

### 08.03.05 故伽藍 (華氏城西南) — unidentified ruined monastery 200 li SW of Pāṭaliputra

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). No authority names a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. None of the Patna or Gaya passages refers to the ruined monastery with the seats of the four
past Buddhas.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. No source here offers a candidate, and a distance of 200 li with no other landmark
leaves nothing to test.

### 08.04.02 大山 (佛入定山) — Barābar Hill (Mizutani, Beglar) against Cunningham's range NW of Wazirganj

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Barābar Hill (Pravaragiri), 15–16 miles N of Bodh Gayā (agreed). Majumdar (1924,
p. 720) gives Barābar after Beglar, and Beal (p. 136) suggests it tentatively. Cunningham (1871, p. 523) corrects
the bearings and distances and puts his "Buddha's Mountain" in the range between Giryek and Gayā, about 3 miles NW of
Vazirganj. That is a different site, 20 miles or more to the east.

**Gazetteers.** HU12 (Gaya 1877) gives Cunningham's whole itinerary: "thence south again for thirty-two miles to
Buddha's Mountain, which is some three miles to the north of Wazírganj". It repeats Cunningham and adds no local
evidence. The 1906 and 1957 Gaya volumes do not name the mountain.

**Assessment.** The choice is between Beglar's Barābar, which keeps Xuanzang's directions, and Cunningham's site,
which emends them. The gazetteers only repeat Cunningham. Mizutani's Barābar stands, but on the authority of Beglar's
reading rather than on any new evidence.

### 08.04.03 德慧伽藍 (Guṇamati's monastery) — Dharawat (Kunwa Hill), Jahanabad subdivision

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). Cunningham (1871, p. 524): tentatively the low range on
the E bank of the Pewar Nadi near Nidawat. Majumdar (p. 720): Dharawat (Dharmapura), after Beglar, accepted by
Cunningham in ASR XV. Dey: Kunwa hill at Dharawat, Jahanabad subdivision. Vivien (p. 373) only lists the station.

**Gazetteers.** HU12 (Gaya 1877) repeats Cunningham's "near Nidawat". BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 215–217) argues for
Dharawat, and RV24 (1957) reprints the passage. The arguments are that the position "corresponds with the account of
the itinerary"; that monastery ruins lie on the N slope of the Kunwa Hill, the name of which is held to preserve
"Guṇamati"; that there are terraces 60 ft and 250 ft long; and that Cunningham's excavation found "a great building
with its back wall against the hill, as described by Hiuen Tsiang", with granite statues, two quadrangles and cells.
RV24 adds a twelve-armed Avalokiteśvara by a large tank in the village.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers supply what Mizutani lacks. Dharawat combines a fit with the route, a possible
survival of the name, a site on a hillside as Xuanzang describes it, and excavated monastic buildings. This is the
best-supported candidate in these files.

### 08.04.04 戒賢伽藍 (孤山) (Śīlabhadra's monastery) — Kauwadol hill, 20 li SW of Dharawat

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). Cunningham (1871, p. 524): tentatively Bithawa, an
isolated hill 3 miles SW of Nidawat. Vivien (p. 374): perhaps the village of Bhadéra, where the name would survive.
Dey: the isolated hill of Kawa-dol near Bela station.

**Gazetteers.** HU12 (1877) repeats Cunningham's Bithawa. BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 194, 231) and RV24 (1957) argue for
Kauwadol on two grounds. Its position relative to Guṇamati at Dharawat "leaves no doubt", and "the lofty peak shaped
like a stupa" matches Xuanzang's "single sharp crag like a stūpa". The volumes also record the ruins of a Buddhist
temple with a colossal seated Buddha at the hill's E foot.

**Assessment.** Kauwadol depends on Dharawat (08.04.03), and the two support each other: the 20 li SW bearing works,
and the hill's shape and the ruins at its foot fit the description. This is a good candidate where Mizutani has none.
Cunningham's Bithawa and Vivien's Bhadéra rest only on the earlier, uncorrected placing of Guṇamati or on a sound
likeness of the name.

### 08.05.03 鉢羅笈菩提山 (Prāgbodhi) — Mora range; the cave is on Dhongra Hill

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mora hill, about 3 miles NE of Bodh Gayā on the E bank of the Nairañjanā (agreed).
Cunningham (p. 525): Mora Pahar, its SW end 2.5 miles NE of Bodh Gayā. Dey: Mora hill across the Phalgu, "three
miles to the north-west of Buddha-Gaya". That direction is probably Dey's slip, since the river lies E of Bodh Gayā.
Watters (p. 113) gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 198–199, 233–236, after C. A. Oldham) and RV24 (1957) argue for a cave halfway
down the NW slope of Dhongra Hill, the middle part of the Mora/Ganjas range opposite Bodh Gayā. The evidence is a cave
of 16 ft 5 in by 10 ft 9 in behind a wall of rock, with a broken eight-armed goddess bearing the Buddhist formula; a
levelled terrace below it; "the remains of some seven stupas" on the summit; and a distance of 14–15 li from Bodh
Gayā. The volumes explain away Xuanzang's "south-west slope" and distinguish this cave from Cunningham's, which they
call "a natural fissure". RV24's history chapter adds "no archaeological data found". HU12 (1877) has only "a mountain
SE of Gaya".

**Assessment.** The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani. They locate the shadow cave and the stūpas on a particular hill
of his Mora range and give physical remains that match Xuanzang.

### 08.06.01 菩提樹 (垣內) — the Bodh Gayā precinct; the railing confirmed as Aśoka's wall

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Mahābodhi precinct at Bodh Gayā; the enclosure is the railing (agreed). Every
authority agrees (Cunningham p. 526; Vivien p. 376; Majumdar p. 720, who notes that Urel preserves the name Uruvilvā;
Beal; Dey).

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906) argues from the stone railing: Xuanzang's 10-ft wall "corresponds with the height (9
feet 10 inches) of the pillars still existing". The pillars carry inscriptions in Aśokan characters. The present
circuit is about 520 ft against about 250 ft for Aśoka's enclosure, so the railing was rearranged and enlarged.
The volume also reports thousands of votive stūpas found during the restoration, which fits "the precincts were
crowded with them". RV24 (1957, after Barua) places subsidiary sites that the table does not list: the Animeṣa caitya
on the mound NE of the tree, and the Jewel-walk on the brick platform N of the temple.

**Assessment.** The site was never in doubt. The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani's reading of the enclosure, and they
also correct the material: the railing is stone, as Mizutani himself remarks.

### 08.06.04 精舍 (菩提樹東) — the present Mahābodhi temple

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Mahābodhi temple, a shrine hall rather than a relic stūpa (agreed). Cunningham
(p. 526): "beyond all doubt the Vihar" Xuanzang saw. Beal (p. 137) follows him. Watters (p. 116) rejects Cunningham's
equation as "not convincing" against the Chinese texts but offers no other building.

**Gazetteers.** HU12 (1877) repeats the identification. BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 202–205) argues it from the restoration
finds. The dimensions match; the temple is built of blue bricks with plaster; there are tiers of niches with Buddha
figures; the east entrance proved to be "a later addition, as stated by the Chinese pilgrim"; and a polished
sandstone throne, two Persepolitan pillar bases of Aśoka's age and older walls under the basement show "this temple
was built over Asoka's temple". RV24 (1957) repeats this and adds the name Mahābodhi in the inscriptions of 850 and
c. 1157.

**Assessment.** The restoration evidence answers Watters' objection as far as these files go. The gazetteers
strengthen Mizutani.

### 08.06.18 目支鄰陀龍王池 (Mucilinda) — Urel tank (Mizutani's map) or Muchharin (living worship)

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives the Urel tank only as a label on the Cunningham map he reproduces (agreed). None
of the seven authorities places the pool.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, p. 199) and RV24 (1957) say that "pilgrims still worship at Mucharin, the spot where
Buddha was sheltered by the snake-[king]". They also note that the village of Urel preserves the name Uruvilvā. The
passages are coded `repeats`, but in substance they name a different spot.

**Assessment.** The evidence is thin on both sides: a map label against a modern cult site. The files do not say
whether Muchharin and Cunningham's Urel tank are the same place. Treat the identification as open, with Muchharin as a
second candidate.

### 08.06.28 盲龍室 — unidentified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification; his note only gives the legend's source, the 仏所行讃 (unknown).
No authority names a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. The Gaya volumes describe the Bodh Gayā precinct in detail but do not mention the blind
nāga's dwelling 2–3 li from the east gate.

**Assessment.** Unidentified, and the gazetteers do not change this.

## Juan 9

### 09.01.02 石柱 (欝頭藍子) — unidentified pillar in the forest beyond the Mohana

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification of the pillar (unknown). No authority gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing about this pillar. The only pillar east of Bodh Gayā that the Gaya volumes discuss is the
Gandhahastin pillar at Bakraur (09.01.01), which is on the near side of the river.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. It can only be placed as somewhere east of the Mohana on the road to Kukkuṭapāda, and so
it depends on the answer to 09.01.03.

### 09.01.02a 莫訶河 (Mahā) — the Mohana river

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "probably" the Mohana (uncertain). This is unanimous elsewhere: Cunningham (p. 527)
and Beal (p. 139) give the Mohana Nadī; Vivien (p. 375) gives the Mahi or Mahānada, which joins the Nairañjanā to form
the Phalgu; Dey gives the Mahi (Mohana), a tributary of the Phalgu.

**Gazetteers.** They add nothing beyond placing Bakraur "between the Nilajan and Mohana" (BG27, Gaya 1906).

**Assessment.** Mizutani's hedge is unnecessary on this evidence, because all the authorities agree. No change.

### 09.01.03 屈屈吒播陀山 (Kukkuṭapāda) — three candidates: Kurkihar, Hasra/Sobhnath, Gurpa

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the hill 1 mile N of Kurkihār (disputed). He cites Majumdar p. 721, yet Majumdar, as
coded, prefers Gurpa (see the end of these notes). Cunningham (pp. 527–528): the three hills near Kurkihar, which he
argues from the name. Beal (p. 142) follows him and puts the three peaks on Murali hill, 3 miles NNE. Majumdar (1924,
pp. 720–721) reports Stein and Keith for Sobhnath and Banerji (JASB 1906) for Gurpa Hill, 20 miles ESE of Bodh Gayā,
and calls Gurpa "probable". Dey: "not Kurkihar but Gurpā hill". Watters and Vivien give no site.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 195–196, 218–223, 232) follows Stein. Hasra Hill, 4 miles SSW of Wazirganj, has
three spurs from the central Sobhnath summit, with a walled terrace and "a mound composed of large bricks, which
evidently marks the remains of the stupa". The bearings are "in full agreement", and Pāla sculptures from the valley
survive. The volume calls Stein's case against Kurkihar "conclusive". It also prints Banerji's Gurpa: the name comes
from Gurupāda, a tunnel is Kāśyapa's cleft, and the three peaks and the distance fit. RV24 (1957) reprints both and
adds Pāla sculptures from Gurpa, but its history chapter goes back to "modern Kurkihara". HU12 (1877) repeats
Cunningham.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers weaken Mizutani's Kurkihar. Its only support is the sound of the name, and the 1906
volume rejects it explicitly. Hasra/Sobhnath has route-fit and a summit stūpa; Gurpa has the Gurupāda name, the cleft
and three peaks. Both are better supported than Kurkihar. Later work (not in these sources) generally favours Gurpa.

### 09.02.01 佛陀伐那山 (Buddhavana) — still unidentified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no accepted identification. He reports Cunningham's Buddhain hill NNE of Kurkihār as
rejected by Majumdar (disputed). Cunningham (p. 528), Beal (p. 147, tentatively) and Dey all give Buddhain. Majumdar
(p. 721) says it "cannot be" Buddhain, because Stein shows that Jeshtiban lies SW of it, not E. Vivien gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** They add nothing. The one passage coded to this place (HU12, 1877, hit 155) is about Cunningham's
"Buddha's Mountain" N of Wazirganj, which is 08.04.02, not Buddhavana.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. Majumdar's objection rests on the Jeshtiban identification (09.02.02), which the 1906
gazetteer confirms on the ground, so it stands. If Kukkuṭapāda moves to Hasra or Gurpa, Buddhavana has to be sought
about 100 li NE of that site.

### 09.02.03 二溫泉 (杖林西南) — Tapoban hot springs

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Tapoban (agreed). Cunningham (p. 529), Beal (p. 147) and Watters (p. 147) agree.
Cunningham derives the name from *tapta-pāni*.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) argues only about the name. It takes Tapoban as "jungle of devotion", not
Cunningham's *tapta-pāni*, and records five springs in pools of 100–116°F (after Buchanan Hamilton) with a triennial
fair. BG27 (Gaya 1906) and RV24 (1957), after Stein, say Tapoban lies 2 miles SW of Jethian. They count four springs,
two of them with a large flow, and a large mound (a stūpa) by the largest, so that "here are still to be found the two
springs mentioned by Hiuen Tsiang and the remains of the stupa by their side". Pilgrims and the sick still bathe there,
which echoes Xuanzang's sentence.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani. The bearing from Jeshtiban, the two main springs, the stūpa mound
and the continuing cult all fit. The dispute over the name does not affect the site.

### 09.02.03c 小孤山 (石室・阿素洛宮) — the Asura palace is the Rajpind cave (Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: unidentified (unknown), citing Cunningham (p. 529), who says "these two places have
not been identified".

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 228–229) and RV24 (1957), after Stein, argue for "the great cave of Rajpind,
which is clearly the same as" Xuanzang's Asura palace. The cave is on the N face of Chandu hill, about 2 miles from
Jethian. It is about 90 ft deep and 20–25 ft high, with a fissure that "the people believe runs far into the mountain"
and a sheer mass of rock over the entrance. "A striking confirmation" is an old paved road on massive walls that runs
westward from Khiri to a platform near the cave. The same pages place Vyāsa's dwelling (09.02.03b) at a recess at the
S foot of Bhaluahi hill. Majumdar, following the same Stein survey, gives a recess at the SW end of the Handia range.
HU11 (1877) speaks only generally of "a small hill with a rock-cut chamber".

**Assessment.** The gazetteers add a specific candidate that the standard authorities here do not record. The cave's
form matches the legend, and an ancient approach road leads to it. It should enter the table as a probable
identification.

### 09.02.09 姞栗陀羅矩吒山 (Gṛdhrakūṭa) — the hill with the Bimbisāra road (Chhathagiri) rather than "Sailagiri"

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Gṛdhrakūṭa at Rajgir with no modern name. He relies on Takakusu's photographs, the
Bimbisāra road with the bricks of the "dismount" and "turn back" stūpas, and the vihāra foundations on the summit
(agreed). Cunningham (p. 534): part of Sailagiri, "no cave found". Beal (p. 153) and Dey follow him, and Watters
(p. 152) says "possibly correct". Vivien (p. 381): 14–15 li NE of the old city. Julien (preface notes, after Klaproth)
says the mountain is marked "Giddore" on the maps, which is a different place altogether.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) repeats Sailagiri, "no trace now" of the caves. BG26 (Patna 1907, after D. N. Sen)
names the hill Chhathagiri, "with the 'Bimbisara road' or stone causeway leading up to it, which still shows traces of
the two stupas", and the vihāra at the W end of the precipice. It is coded `repeats`.

**Assessment.** BG26's Chhathagiri matches the remains Mizutani describes: the causeway, the two stūpas and the
summit vihāra. It gives his identification a local name. The files do not say whether Chhathagiri is a spur of
Cunningham's Sailagiri or a separate hill. Julien's "Giddore" is a map guess and can be set aside.

### 09.02.11a 卑鉢羅石室 (阿素洛宮) (Pippala cave) — N face of Vaibhāra (Mizutani) against Ratnagiri (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: following Takakusu, a cave on the N face of Vaibhāra. This is an old gold-mine shaft,
with the "Pippala cave" built in front of it (uncertain). Xuanzang, however, puts the cave on Vipula. Cunningham
(p. 531) places Faxian's Pippal-tree cave on Ratnagiri, one mile E of the Son Bhandar. Beal (p. 156) suggests that
Pi-pu-lo = Vaibhāra and that the cave is the Sonbhandar, "it may be so".

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) repeats Cunningham's Ratnagiri and adds only the paved zigzag road to a Jain temple
on that hill. The two copies of the volume count once.

**Assessment.** There are three positions (N face of Vaibhāra, Sonbhandar, Ratnagiri), all built on Faxian's
location or a reading of the name rather than on remains. The gazetteers decide nothing. Mizutani's hedge is right.

### 09.02.12 大石室 (提婆達多入定) — identified only through Faxian

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Faxian's 調達石窟, 3 li E of the old city's N gate; such caves are old gold-mine
workings (uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** Unidentified on the ground. Mizutani's equation with Faxian's cave gives a position but not a
monument, and nothing here adds to it.

### 09.02.14 阿闍多設咄路王舍利塔 (Ajātaśatru's relic stūpa) — probably the mound E of the road (Takakusu)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: probably the raised ground E of the present road, now a Muslim cemetery, after
Takakusu (uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. The Patna volumes describe the walls of old and new Rajgir but not this stūpa.

**Assessment.** Takakusu's mound remains the only candidate. The gazetteers neither confirm nor contradict it.

### 09.02.16 大石室 (第一結集) (Saptaparṇa cave) — N side of Baibhār, not the Son Bhandar

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Saptaparṇa cave on Baibhāra hill, without naming a particular cave (agreed).
Cunningham (p. 531): tentatively the Son Bhandar cave in the S face, while admitting that Xuanzang points to the N
face. Beal (p. 156) rejects the Son Bhandar. Dey: Beglar's group of caves about a mile W of the Pippala cave on the
N side of Baibhāra, and calls Cunningham's view incorrect.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) repeats "on the side of Baibhar". The Jain inscriptions there name the hill
Baibhāra/Vyavahāra. BG26 (Patna 1907) reports two views. Stein places the site at the natural caves on the N scarp,
with a 100-ft terrace walled with unhewn slabs and an overhanging stratum split into seven vertical sections, the seven
"leaves". Marshall places it on the built-up top of a spur on the N side, about half a mile further W of the Veṇuvana,
and doubts that a cave is meant.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani's Baibhāra and narrow the search to its N side. That rules out
Cunningham's Son Bhandar. Which spot on the N side is meant (Stein's caves or Marshall's spur) stays open.

### 09.02.16a 大衆部結集處堵波 (Mahāsāṃghika council stūpa) — unidentified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification; his notes discuss only the schism (unknown). No authority gives a
site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. The stūpa lies 20-odd li W of Ānanda's stūpa, which is itself unlocated, so the route
cannot be tested against the ground.

### 09.02.17a 無憂王堵波・石柱 (迦蘭陀池西北) — probably near Baitarni Godan (Takakusu)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "probably" near the present Baitarni Godan, SW of New Rājagṛha, after Takakusu
(uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. No Patna volume mentions a pillar NW of the Kalandaka pond.

**Assessment.** Takakusu's suggestion is the only one, and these files add nothing for or against it.

### 09.03.01 那爛陀僧伽藍 (Nālandā) — Bargaon; the gazetteer dispute concerns Indraśailaguhā, not Nālandā

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Nālandā ruins at Bargaon, 7 miles N of Rājgir (agreed). All the authorities
agree: Cunningham (pp. 536–537), Vivien (p. 382), Beal (p. 167), Majumdar (p. 721, from inscriptions naming Nālandā),
Dey. Watters (p. 166) says it "may be correct".

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) and BG26 (Patna 1907) repeat Bargaon. They describe the N–S range of stūpa mounds,
the monastery block to the E and the 1,600 × 400 ft field of debris, and they place the Nāga's tank and Bālāditya's
temple (see New places). The passages coded `doubts`/`alternative` (HU11, after Broadley) assume Bargaon. Their
target is Cunningham's Giriyek as Faxian's solitary hill (09.03.11).

**Assessment.** Not in dispute. The gazetteers confirm Mizutani, and the doubting passages belong under 09.03.11.

### 09.03.06 精舍 (如來三月說法處, 那爛陀伽藍西) — probably Site No. 3 at Nālandā

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "probably" Yijing's Mūlagandhakuṭī among the caityas W of the monastery row. He leaves
the matching of the texts with the excavation to specialists (uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** BG26 (Patna 1907) reports Spooner's excavation of Site No. 3, Cunningham's "vihara A" at the S end
of the stūpa range, which Spooner "would identify with the stupa mentioned by Hiuen Tsiang as marking the place where
the Lord Buddha dwelt for three months". The excavation found three successive masonry encasements, stucco Buddhas and
four corner towers.

**Assessment.** This adds a definite structure that fits Mizutani's area, the line of caityas W of the monasteries.
It strengthens his reading but rests on one excavator's suggestion, so the confidence is low.

### 09.03.08 拘理迦邑 (Kolika) — unidentified; Cunningham's Jagdispur mound unconfirmed

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no modern identification; he equates it with the 拘離迦 village of the 仏本行集経
(unknown). Cunningham (p. 538): a ruined mound near Jagdispur, 1.25 miles SW of the Bargaon ruins. Vivien (p. 383)
gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** Cunningham's mound rests only on the bearing and the distance of 8–9 li. Mizutani does not adopt it,
and nothing here supports it further. No change.

### 09.03.10 迦羅臂拏迦邑 (Kālapināka) — = Faxian's Nāla; no site

**Identifications.** Mizutani: probably another name for Faxian's 那羅聚落 (Nāla), Śāriputra's birthplace
(uncertain). Watters (p. 171) and Beal (p. lviii) agree. Cunningham (p. 538) places it about halfway between Nālandā
and Indraśailaguhā, about 4 miles SE of Nālandā, and Dey says the same; neither names a village. Vivien (p. 384)
gives no site and separates Faxian's Nāla from this place.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** The equation with Nāla has wide support, but no site has been found. The gazetteers add nothing.

### 09.03.11 因陀羅勢羅窶訶山 (Indraśailaguhā) — the hill W of Giriyek, with the Gidhadwari cave

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Giriyek hill, 6 miles from Rājgir (agreed). Cunningham (p. 541): Giryek ("Giri-eka"),
with the cave being the Gidha-dwar in its S face. Beal (p. 180) gives the western, higher peak. Majumdar (p. 722,
after Stein): "the rugged hill rising immediately to the W. of the village". Dey gives Giriyek (after Broadley).
Watters (p. 173) calls the double identification with Faxian's hill "open to objections". Vivien (p. 384, after
Kittoe) gives the rock crowned by a Muslim chapel near Bihār fort.

**Gazetteers.** BG26 (Patna 1907) argues for Giriak. The Haṃsa stūpa ("Jarasandha's tower") stands on the ridge, with
a reservoir; the summit has steps and terrace walls, read as the monastery; there is an old walled road up the scarp;
and the Gidhadwari cave on the S face, a natural cave 17 ft high on a masonry platform, "corresponds exactly" to the
cave of Indra's questions. HU11 (Patna 1877), after Broadley, objects only to Cunningham's second equation. Bargaon
lies 6 miles NW of Giriyak, so Faxian's "solitary hill" 1 yojana NE of Nālandā "cannot be at Giriyak". Broadley takes
Behar hill for Faxian's hill instead.

**Assessment.** BG26's remains (the stūpa, the monastery terrace and the cave) strengthen Mizutani's identification
for Xuanzang. Broadley's objection affects only the link with Faxian's solitary hill, which Mizutani's note
brings in, and it may support Vivien's Bihār for Faxian, not for Xuanzang.

### 09.03.13 迦布徳迦伽藍 (Kapotaka) — Bihār (Mizutani) against Dariyapur Parbati (Cunningham's later view)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Bihār (Bihar Sharif), after emending 百五六十里 to 五六十里, following Julien and
Cunningham 1871 (agreed). Cunningham (pp. 542–543): Bihār, reading 60 li, with the Shekhpura hill as an alternative.
Beal (p. 182) reports Cunningham's later view, the village of Pārbati 10 miles NE of Giriyek, which needs the same
emendation. Vivien gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) gives Cunningham's Behar hill = Kapotaka and records Broadley's doubt.
BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 213–214) and RV24 (1957) give Dariyapur Parbati, 6 miles N of Warisaliganj. The evidence is the
hill called Parbati (from Paravata, "pigeon"), its terraces "strewn with ruins", and Beglar's rows of cells. RV24 also
quotes Cunningham's admission that this site lacks "coincidence in position with recorded bearings and distances".

**Assessment.** Both Bihār and Parbati need the distance emended. Parbati adds a pigeon name and monastic remains on
a terraced hill, which matches Xuanzang's description. The gazetteers make Parbati at least as well supported as
Mizutani's Bihār. The bearing remains the weak point for both.

### 09.03.14 孤山 (觀自在菩薩像) — Bihār hill (Mizutani) or the Parbati hill

**Identifications.** Mizutani: at Bihār, the town's name being taken from this Avalokiteśvara vihāra (uncertain;
his note refers back to 09.03.13). Cunningham (p. 543) is "strongly inclined" to the hill NW of Bihār, with its
Muhammadan buildings and Buddhist fragments; Dey agrees. Beal (p. 183) reports Fergusson's equation of the Behar hill
with Faxian's hill and gives no site of his own.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 213–214) and RV24 (1957), following Cunningham's later report and Beglar,
place the Avalokiteśvara temple on the Parbati hill at Dariyapur. The spot is "now covered by the dargah of Haji
Chandar". The passage is coded `repeats`, although it moves the site away from Mizutani's Bihār.

**Assessment.** This place depends on 09.03.13. If Kapotaka is Parbati, the solitary hill with the Avalokiteśvara
image is the Parbati hill, where a shrine stands on the site. If Kapotaka is Bihār, it is Bihār hill. The gazetteers
add an equally supported alternative.

### 09.03.15 一伽藍 (孤山東南四十餘里) — Titarawa or Aphsar; neither secure

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no settled identification. He reports Cunningham's Titarawa ruins 7 miles SE of Bihār,
and later Aphsar with 40 li read as 4 li (disputed). Cunningham (p. 543) gives "the great ruined mound of Titarawa".
Beal (p. 184) reports Cunningham's later Aphsar.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906) "doubts" Aphsar: its remains (a Varāha statue and a mound) are Brahmanical, and
"from the absence of any mention of Aphsanr by the Chinese pilgrims" it was not a Buddhist settlement. The argument is
circular, because it assumes the answer to the question.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. The choice between Titarawa and Aphsar follows the choice of base point (Bihār or
Parbati, 09.03.13–14). The gazetteer's objection to Aphsar is weak and does not change the picture.

### 09.03.15a 大聚落 (殑伽河南) — vicinity of Sheikhpura (Cunningham's later view)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Shekhpur, 20 miles from the Ganges, after emending 70余里 to 170余里 (uncertain).
Cunningham (1871, pp. 543–545) gives near Daryapur, 34 miles W of Monghyr, also reading 170 li. That is a different
site. Beal (p. 184) gives the vicinity of Shekhpura, after Cunningham, ASR XV.

**Gazetteers.** BG33 (Monghyr 1909) and RV27 (1960) repeat Cunningham's later Sheikhpura. The only local evidence
is "few ancient remains" and the Mathokar Tal tank 2 miles W.

**Assessment.** Mizutani follows Cunningham's revised view, and the gazetteers repeat it without new evidence.
Neither candidate has remains to match Xuanzang's "large village", so the identification stays uncertain.

### 09.03.15b 落般膩羅聚落 (Lo-in-ni-lo) — Rajaona (Cunningham, Monghyr gazetteers) the best-argued candidate

**Identifications.** Mizutani: unsettled, "awaits further study" (disputed). Vivien (p. 385) gives Roynallah on
Rennell's map, on the S bank of the Ganges near Balgada. Cunningham (1871, p. 545) gives Rohinila/Rohinala, after
Vivien. Dey reports Cunningham's Rajaona or Kiul and defends Vivien's village as real (Rehuanala on the Ganges). Beal
(p. 184) gives Cunningham's Rajjāna and the "Rowbenny" of the Āīn-i-Akbarī. Watters (p. 176) rejects Julien's
restoration as Rohinila.

**Gazetteers.** BG33 (Monghyr 1909) and RV27 (1960) set out three of Cunningham's proposals: Nongarh (his earlier
view), Kiul/Jaynagar (the name read as Kiul; a ruined city 4 miles along the river; monastery seals of the 10th–11th
centuries) and Rajaona, 2 miles NW of Lakhisarai. They argue for Rajaona. It "still possesses a large sheet of water
to the north", fed by the Halahar, which matches Xuanzang's great pond 2–3 li N of the village, and mounds of stūpas
and temples stretch for 4 miles.

**Assessment.** Only Rajaona is supported by physical features matching the text: the lake to the north and
extensive Buddhist remains. Vivien's Rohinala is a name on a map. The gazetteers make Rajaona the best candidate in
these files, but Cunningham's own wavering argues for medium-low confidence.
