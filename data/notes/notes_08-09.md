# Notes on disputed places: juan 8–9 (Magadha)

Places in juan 8–9 that meet at least one of the criteria in `NOTES_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, or on which the Archaeological
Survey gives a site or field evidence (`NOTES_REVISION_ASR.md`), in master-table order.
Gazetteer volumes: HU11 = Hunter, *Statistical Account* Patna, Saran (1877); HU12 = Hunter, Gaya, Shahabad (1877);
BG26 = Patna District Gazetteer (1907); BG27 = Gaya District Gazetteer (O'Malley 1906); RV24 = Gaya revised edition
(1957, which reprints much of 1906); BG33 = Monghyr (1909); RV27 = Monghyr revised (1960). Most volumes exist in two
scans; counts below are of volumes and passages, not hits.
Survey volumes (ASR, page as printed; roman numerals = preface or prefatory Note): I = Cunningham, seasons 1862–65
(publ. 1871); III = Cunningham 1871–72; VIII = Beglar 1872–73, with Cunningham's preface and a Note by Beglar written
after a later joint visit; XI = Cunningham 1875–78; XV = Cunningham 1879–80; XVI = Cunningham 1880–81.

## Juan 8

### 08.02.03 八萬四千塔 (無憂王堵波) — Cunningham's Panch-Pahari mound C, placed from Faxian

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no modern site; the stūpa is placed only relative to the prison pillar, and he notes
that Faxian puts Aśoka's first great stūpa 3-odd li south of the city, which does not match Xuanzang (unknown). None of
the seven authorities gives a site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XI, p. 157) starts his Pāṭaliputra sites from this stūpa, "because its position rules
the sites of all the others". He puts it at mound C of the Panch-Pahari ("five hills") line of brick mounds at
Nirandpur, half a mile S of the old city, because Faxian puts it 3 li S of the city. Curved facing bricks give a
diameter of "about 100 feet". The footprint vihāra is site D, 300 ft S, and the pillar G (08.02.05). Panch-Pahari had
a history in the survey before this: Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 27) took it for Bhika Pahari, the monks' hill, and his Note
(p. ix) withdraws that. Excavation showed it was "brick, and brick alone", and Cunningham then proposed it for
Upagupta's hill.

**Gazetteers.** None of the Patna volumes identifies this stūpa. BG26 (Patna 1907) places two other Aśokan sites
nearby: Aśoka's "hell" at the Agam Kuan well and the footprint stone at Bulandi Bagh (see New places).

**Assessment.** Cunningham gives a candidate, but he reaches it by Faxian's distance, which is the difficulty
Mizutani points out, and nothing found at mound C ties it to Aśoka. It is a candidate of low confidence. The
gazetteers' footprint stone at Bulandi Bagh is not reconciled with his site D.

### 08.02.05 阿育王石柱 — Kumrahar fragments (Mizutani) against Cunningham's inferred spot G

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the pillar fragments found by Waddell (1903) at Kumrahar, S of the railway line
(agreed). None of the seven authorities places the pillar.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XI, p. 159) infers the pillar's place from the pilgrims' descriptions: "somewhere about
the spot marked G", in front of his footprint vihāra (site D) at Panch-Pahari, because Faxian puts it S of the stūpa
and Xuanzang near the vihāra. Nothing was found there. On the same page he takes the great mound F (Bara Pahari,
160 ft square, 50 ft high) for Mahendra's stone house; a 12-ft trench across it found only broken walls.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** Cunningham's G is a point on his own plan, derived from his placing of the stūpa (08.02.03), and
nothing was found at it. Waddell's Kumrahar has actual fragments of a pillar, found after these reports. The files do
not say how Kumrahar lies relative to the Panch-Pahari line. Mizutani's identification stands.

### 08.03.01 屈屈吒阿濫摩僧伽藍 (Skt. kurkuṭa-ārāma) — unidentified; only "near Pāṭaliputra"

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no modern site, only SE of old Pāṭaliputra; he sets aside the southern tradition
that equates it with the Aśokārāma of the "third council" (unknown). Beal (p. 142): the "cock-garden" near Patna, to be
kept apart from Kukkuṭapādagiri near Gayā. Dey: in the Upakaṇṭhika garden on the right bank of the Ganges at
Pāṭaliputra. Neither names a site.

**Survey.** Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 27) writes of "a small brick mound" SE of Patna, "its name has escaped me, but it is
clearly the ruins of Asoka's Kukkutarama". He gives no measurement or find. Cunningham, after surveying the Patna
mounds (ASR XI, p. 162), says the Kukkuṭārāma was not found. On the same page he puts the tank of "holy water" at the
Pokhar SE of the Panch-Pahari stūpa mound.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. No Patna volume names the Kukkuṭārāma.

**Assessment.** Still unidentified. Beglar's unnamed mound is in the direction Mizutani gives, but it is no more than
a direction, and Cunningham did not find the monastery. The only other point in these files is Beal's warning against
confusing it with Kukkuṭapāda (09.01.03).

### 08.03.05 故伽藍 (華氏城西南) — unidentified ruined monastery 200 li SW of Pāṭaliputra

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). No authority names a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. None of the Patna or Gaya passages refers to the ruined monastery with the seats of the four
past Buddhas. The survey is silent too.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. No source here offers a candidate, and a distance of 200 li with no other landmark
leaves nothing to test.

### 08.04.01 鞮羅擇迦伽藍 (Teladhaka) — Tillara, named in an inscription found there

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Tillāra, 21 miles W of Nālandā; the place-name derives from the monastery's, and
inscriptions confirm it (agreed). Cunningham (1871, p. 523), Beal (p. 102) and Dey agree on Tillara. Majumdar
(p. 720) cites two inscriptions naming Teladhaka. Watters (p. 107) gives no site.

**Survey.** Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 33) reports an inscription on the door jamb of the Sangin Masjid at Tillara that
"distinctly mentions the name Telâdaka", as Cunningham read it: "The identification of Teladaka is thus established
beyond dispute." Beglar notes that the itinerary alone would not have given Tillara, since it lies 25–28 miles due S
of Patna, not SW. In his preface (p. viii) Cunningham calls his identification "now placed beyond all doubt". In
ASR XI (pp. 162, 166) he reconciles the distances, 30–32 miles by the old road via Phatuha (180–200 li) and 21 miles
W of Bargaon (3 yojanas). He takes Broadley's Bulandi mound W of the village for the monastery and its vihāras.
He measured it at only 24 ft high and 350 ft square, and the Muslim owners of the graves on it stopped his
excavation.

**Gazetteers.** BG26 (Patna 1907) adds a Pāla-period inscription naming Tailadhaka on a lintel, the Bulandi mound
with its graves, and a mosque platform built of Buddhist pillars. It follows the survey.

**Assessment.** This is one of the few places in Magadha named on the spot by an inscription. Mizutani's
identification is confirmed, and the survey is the source of his "confirmed by inscriptions".

### 08.04.02 大山 (佛入定山) — Barābar Hill (Beglar, then Cunningham's own final view)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Barābar Hill (Pravaragiri), 15–16 miles N of Bodh Gayā (agreed). Majumdar (1924,
p. 720) gives Barābar after Beglar, and Beal (p. 136) suggests it tentatively. Cunningham (1871, p. 523) corrects
the bearings and distances and puts his "Buddha's Mountain" in the range between Giryek and Gayā, about 3 miles NW of
Vazirganj.

**Survey.** In ASR I (pp. 51–52) Cunningham records the name *Pravara-giri-guhā* from the Anantavarman inscription
in the Barābar caves. He does not connect it with Xuanzang, and explains the pilgrim's silence by a Brahman
occupation of the caves. Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 36) follows Xuanzang's bearing and distance from Tillara without
correction: "in this part of the pilgrim's route I think no correction is needed". That brings him to the Barābar
group, with an old temple basement on the Surajink peak. Cunningham adopts this in ASR XI (p. 162), XV (p. iv) and
XVI (pp. 39–40), where he measures 90 li SW from Teladhaka to "the famous peak of the Barabar Mountain".

**Gazetteers.** HU12 (Gaya 1877) gives Cunningham's 1871 itinerary ("three miles to the north of Wazírganj"). It
was out of date when printed and adds no local evidence. The 1906 and 1957 Gaya volumes do not name the mountain.

**Assessment.** The survey itself abandoned the Vazirganj site. Barābar now has Beglar's uncorrected route, the
*Pravaragiri* name in an inscription on the hill, and Cunningham's assent, and it anchors Dharawat and Kauwa-dol
(08.04.03–04). Mizutani is strengthened.

### 08.04.03 德慧伽藍 (Guṇamati's monastery) — Dharawat (Kunwa Hill), excavated with Gupta seals

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). Cunningham (1871, p. 524): tentatively the low range on
the E bank of the Pewar Nadi near Nidawat. Majumdar (p. 720): Dharawat (Dharmapura), after Beglar, accepted by
Cunningham in ASR XV. Dey: Kunwa hill at Dharawat, Jahanabad subdivision.

**Survey.** Cunningham described Dharawat in 1862 (ASR I, p. 53) without seeing its bearing on Xuanzang. Beglar
(ASR VIII, pp. 36, 39) reached it by Xuanzang's bearing and distance from Barābar and found brick mounds, terraces and
statues dug out along the pass. Cunningham adopted it in ASR XI (p. 162), called it "absolutely certain" in XV
(p. iv), and excavated in 1880–81 (ASR XVI, pp. v, 45–46). He found a monastery on the N slope with a 9-ft back wall
against the hill, about 150 × 100 ft. On the summit was a stūpa "of which every brick was stamped with the figure of a
stūpa", holding 520 cowries and two clay seals in Gupta letters, one reading *Buddha*, the other "...
*Tathāgatasya Buddhasya*". There were also creed seals of the 9th–10th centuries and a punch-marked coin. He holds
that the name Kunwa "is almost certainly a survival" of Guṇamati.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 215–217) and RV24 (1957) give the same case: route fit, the Kunwa name, terraces,
"a great building with its back wall against the hill". This summarises ASR XVI. RV24 adds a twelve-armed
Avalokiteśvara by a large tank in the village.

**Assessment.** This is the best-founded identification in these notes that Mizutani lacks. The route works without
emendation, the monastery stands on the hillside as described, and an early Buddhist stūpa was excavated. No
inscription names Guṇamati.

### 08.04.04 戒賢伽藍 (孤山) (Śīlabhadra's monastery) — Kauwa-dol hill, 20 li SW of Dharawat

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). Cunningham (1871, p. 524): tentatively Bithawa, an
isolated hill 3 miles SW of Nidawat. Vivien (p. 374): perhaps the village of Bhadéra. Dey: the isolated hill of
Kawa-dol near Bela station.

**Survey.** Beglar (ASR VIII, pp. 39–40) followed the 20 li SW from Dharawat on the ground to Kauwa-dol. At its SE
foot he found a large stone temple with granite pillars and a colossal seated Buddha, with the Buddhist creed in Kuṭila
letters on one pedestal. He also checked the next stage: Gayā lies 12 miles SW by the old road, as Xuanzang's onward
route requires. Cunningham (ASR XI, p. 162; XV, p. iv; XVI, pp. 46–49) gives up his Bithawa and "fully concur[s]".
He records the stūpa-shaped granite peak, about 500 ft high; fourteen granite pillars 7 ft high grouped before the
colossal Buddha; remains over 97 × 67 ft with a long E–W wall; and brick mounds at Shamspur at the N foot.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 194, 231) and RV24 (1957) give the same two arguments (position relative to
Dharawat, "the lofty peak shaped like a stupa") and the temple with its colossal Buddha. They echo Beglar and ASR XVI.
HU12 (1877) repeats the abandoned Bithawa.

**Assessment.** Kauwa-dol depends on Dharawat, and the two support each other. The survey measured the remains and
checked the onward distance to Gayā, and Cunningham abandoned his own alternative. It is now well supported where
Mizutani has nothing.

### 08.05.03 鉢羅笈菩提山 (Prāgbodhi) — Mora range; the cave is on Dhongra Hill

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mora hill, about 3 miles NE of Bodh Gayā on the E bank of the Nairañjanā (agreed).
Cunningham (p. 525): Mora Pahar, its SW end 2.5 miles NE of Bodh Gayā. Dey: Mora hill across the Phalgu, "three
miles to the north-west of Buddha-Gaya". That direction is probably Dey's slip, since the river lies E of Bodh Gayā.
Watters (p. 113) gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 198–199, 233–236, after C. A. Oldham) and RV24 (1957) argue for a cave halfway
down the NW slope of Dhongra Hill, the middle part of the Mora/Ganjas range opposite Bodh Gayā. The evidence is a cave
of 16 ft 5 in by 10 ft 9 in behind a wall of rock, with a broken eight-armed goddess bearing the Buddhist formula; a
levelled terrace below it; "the remains of some seven stupas" on the summit; and a distance of 14–15 li from Bodh
Gayā. The volumes explain away Xuanzang's "south-west slope" and distinguish this cave from Cunningham's, which they
call "a natural fissure". RV24's history chapter adds "no archaeological data found". HU12 (1877) has only "a mountain
SE of Gaya". The survey reports coded here do not discuss the hill.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani. They locate the shadow cave and the stūpas on a particular hill
of his Mora range and give physical remains that match Xuanzang.

### 08.06.01 菩提樹 (垣內) — the Bodh Gayā precinct; the railing confirmed as Aśoka's wall

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Mahābodhi precinct at Bodh Gayā; the enclosure is the railing (agreed). Every
authority agrees (Cunningham p. 526; Vivien p. 376; Majumdar p. 720, who notes that Urel preserves the name Uruvilvā;
Beal; Dey).

**Survey.** Cunningham takes the precinct as given. He describes the Bodhi tree on its raised terrace (ASR I,
pp. 5–8) and the Vajrāsana (ASR III, pp. 80–82). His measurements concern the temple (08.06.04) and the tanks
(08.06.18).

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906) argues from the stone railing: Xuanzang's 10-ft wall "corresponds with the height (9
feet 10 inches) of the pillars still existing". The pillars carry inscriptions in Aśokan characters. The present
circuit is about 520 ft against about 250 ft for Aśoka's enclosure, so the railing was rearranged and enlarged.
The volume also reports thousands of votive stūpas found during the restoration, which fits "the precincts were
crowded with them". RV24 (1957, after Barua) places subsidiary sites that the table does not list: the Animeṣa caitya
on the mound NE of the tree, and the Jewel-walk on the brick platform N of the temple (see New places for the survey's
promenade).

**Assessment.** The site was never in doubt. The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani's reading of the enclosure, and they
also correct the material: the railing is stone, as Mizutani himself remarks.

### 08.06.04 精舍 (菩提樹東) — the present Mahābodhi temple, measured against Xuanzang

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Mahābodhi temple, a shrine hall rather than a relic stūpa (agreed). Cunningham
(p. 526): "beyond all doubt the Vihar" Xuanzang saw. Beal (p. 137) follows him. Watters (p. 116) rejects Cunningham's
equation as "not convincing" against the Chinese texts but offers no other building.

**Survey.** Cunningham measured the temple against Xuanzang three times. In ASR I (pp. 5–8) he gives the lost
statue's pedestal as 13 ft 2 in × 5 ft 8 in × 4 ft against the pilgrim's figures. He dated the temple to Amara Siṃha
(c. 600), a date he later dropped. In ASR III (pp. 80–84) he gives a base of 47 ft 3 in × 48 ft 8 in and a height of
160 ft, and argues from brick sizes that the E additions are later. He reads the long sanctum against the Śaśāṅka
story and the ruined walls and stairs on the E front as the two-storeyed pavilion. In ASR XI (p. 145) he measured
the height with a theodolite: "just 160 feet" against Xuanzang's 160–170, with a base of 48 ft 10 in × 47 ft 6 in
against 20 paces. In ASR XVI (pp. iii–iv) he reports the clearance of 1880–81.

**Gazetteers.** HU12 (1877) repeats the identification. BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 202–205) argues it from the restoration
finds: the dimensions, the plastered blue brick, the niches, the E entrance as "a later addition, as stated by the
Chinese pilgrim", and the Aśokan throne and pillar bases under the basement. Much of this repeats what Cunningham
reported. RV24 (1957) adds the name Mahābodhi in the inscriptions of 850 and c. 1157.

**Assessment.** The measured height, base and later E front answer Watters' objection as far as these files go.
Mizutani is strengthened.

### 08.06.18 目支鄰陀龍王池 (Mucilinda) — Cunningham's Teskar Tal, the Urel tank, or Muchharin

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives the Urel tank only as a label on the Cunningham map he reproduces (agreed). None
of the seven authorities places the pool.

**Survey.** Cunningham moved twice. In ASR I (p. 10) he gave the Buchhokar Tal SE of the temple, which "exactly
answers" Xuanzang, as the Burmese embassy had also seen. In ASR XI (pp. 145–147) he gave that tank (now the Budhokhar)
to the Brahman brothers and the Mihap Tal to Indra. Xuanzang puts Mucilinda's tank E of Indra's, but the tank on the
E "belongs to the village of Urel", which Cunningham holds to be Uruvilvā. He therefore reads W for E and gives the
Teskar (Taksal) Tal, 600 × 500 ft, with a small modern temple and Buddhist sculptures on its W bank. On his plan
(pl. XL) C is "Muchilinda's tank" and D, the Urel Tal, is the "Uruvilva tank".

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, p. 199) and RV24 (1957) say that "pilgrims still worship at Mucharin, the spot where
Buddha was sheltered by the snake-[king]". They note that Urel preserves the name Uruvilvā.

**Assessment.** There are three candidates. Cunningham's own final choice, the Teskar Tal, needs an emended bearing.
The Urel tank keeps Xuanzang's "east". Cunningham rejected it only because he gave it to Uruvilvā, and if Mizutani's
map is pl. XL his label may be a slip for C. Muchharin rests on a modern cult. The identification is open.

### 08.06.28 盲龍室 — unidentified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification; his note only gives the legend's source, the 仏所行讃 (unknown).
No authority names a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. The Gaya volumes describe the Bodh Gayā precinct in detail but do not mention the blind
nāga's dwelling 2–3 li from the east gate. Nor does Cunningham's survey of the sites S of the temple (ASR XI).

**Assessment.** Unidentified.

## Juan 9

### 09.01.02 石柱 (欝頭藍子) — unidentified pillar in the forest beyond the Mohana

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification of the pillar (unknown). No authority gives a site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR I, p. 13) describes only the Gandhahastin stūpa and pillar stump at Bakror (09.01.01).
He reports that the shaft was taken to Gayā and set up in Sahebganj in 1789. The Udraka pillar is not separated from
it.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing about this pillar. The only pillar E of Bodh Gayā in the Gaya volumes is the one at Bakror,
which is on the near side of the river.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. It can only be placed somewhere E of the Mohana on the road to Kukkuṭapāda, and so it
depends on the answer to 09.01.03.

### 09.01.02a 莫訶河 (Mahā) — the Mohana river

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "probably" the Mohana (uncertain). This is unanimous elsewhere: Cunningham (p. 527)
and Beal (p. 139) give the Mohana Nadī; Vivien (p. 375) gives the Mahi or Mahānada, which joins the Nairañjanā to form
the Phalgu; Dey gives the Mahi (Mohana), a tributary of the Phalgu.

**Gazetteers.** They add nothing beyond placing Bakraur "between the Nilajan and Mohana" (BG27, Gaya 1906).

**Assessment.** Mizutani's hedge is unnecessary on this evidence, because all the authorities agree. No change.

### 09.01.03 屈屈吒播陀山 (Kukkuṭapāda) — three candidates: Kurkihar/Murali, Hasra/Sobhnath, Gurpa

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the hill 1 mile N of Kurkihār (disputed). He cites Majumdar p. 721, yet Majumdar, as
coded, prefers Gurpa. Cunningham (1871, pp. 527–528): the three hills near Kurkihar, argued from the name. Beal
(p. 142) puts the three peaks on Murali hill, 3 miles NNE. Majumdar (pp. 720–721) reports Stein and Keith for Sobhnath
and Banerji (JASB 1906) for Gurpa Hill, 20 miles ESE of Bodh Gayā, which he calls "probable". Dey: "not Kurkihar but
Gurpā hill".

**Survey.** Cunningham stayed at Kurkihar but moved the hill. In ASR I (pp. 16–17) it is three bare hills touching at
their bases half a mile N of the village. In ASR XV (pp. 4–9) it is the three peaks of the Murali hill, 3 miles NNE.
There a square basement with broken bricks on the middle peak is "most probably" Xuanzang's summit stūpa, and a mound
of about 120 ft square called Sugatghar is the monastery. Kurkihar has 37 figures and ten inscriptions of about
800–1000. None names the hill.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 195–196, 218–223, 232) follows Stein. Hasra Hill, 4 miles SSW of Wazirganj, has
three spurs from the central Sobhnath summit, a walled terrace and "a mound composed of large bricks, which evidently
marks the remains of the stupa". The bearings are "in full agreement", and the volume calls Stein's case against
Kurkihar "conclusive". It also prints Banerji's Gurpa: the name comes from Gurupāda, a tunnel is Kāśyapa's cleft,
and the three peaks and the distance fit. RV24 (1957) reprints both. HU12 (1877) repeats Cunningham.

**Assessment.** The survey gives Kurkihar a summit ruin and a rich Pāla site, but Cunningham's own move between two
hills shows how loosely the text fits there. The case still rests on the name. Hasra (route and summit stūpa) and
Gurpa (name, cleft, three peaks) remain better supported. Later work (not in these sources) generally favours Gurpa.

### 09.02.01 佛陀伐那山 (Buddhavana) — still unidentified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no accepted identification. He reports Cunningham's Buddhain hill NNE of Kurkihār as
rejected by Majumdar (disputed). Cunningham (p. 528), Beal (p. 147, tentatively) and Dey all give Buddhain. Majumdar
(p. 721) says it "cannot be" Buddhain, because Stein shows that Jeshtiban lies SW of it, not E.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR III, pp. 139–140) repeats Budhain, "one of the stations of the Indian survey". Bamboos
are still cut there, but he found no Aśokan stūpa and no cave.

**Gazetteers.** They add nothing. The one passage coded to this place (HU12, 1877, hit 155) is about Cunningham's
"Buddha's Mountain" N of Wazirganj, which is 08.04.02.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. The survey confirms that Budhain has no remains to match Xuanzang, and Majumdar's
objection stands. If Kukkuṭapāda moves to Hasra or Gurpa, Buddhavana must be sought about 100 li NE of that site.

### 09.02.03 二溫泉 (杖林西南) — Tapoban hot springs

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Tapoban (agreed). Cunningham (p. 529), Beal (p. 147) and Watters (p. 147) agree.
Cunningham derives the name from *tapta-pāni*.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR III, pp. 139–140) places Tapoban 1¾ miles from Jethian, with the bamboo forest at
"Jakhtiban" (09.02.02). Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 84) saw vapour over the springs from the top of Baibhār.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) argues only about the name: "jungle of devotion", not *tapta-pāni*. It records
five springs of 100–116°F with a triennial fair. BG27 (Gaya 1906) and RV24 (1957), after Stein, place Tapoban 2 miles
SW of Jethian. They find two springs with a large flow among four, and a large mound by the largest, so that "here
are still to be found the two springs mentioned by Hiuen Tsiang and the remains of the stupa by their side".

**Assessment.** Mizutani is strengthened. The bearing from Jeshtiban, the two main springs, the stūpa mound and the
continuing cult all fit.

### 09.02.03c 小孤山 (石室・阿素洛宮) — the Asura palace is the Rajpind cave (Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: unidentified (unknown), citing Cunningham (p. 529): "these two places have not been
identified".

**Survey.** Beglar (ASR VIII, pp. 84, 89) did not find the cave: "it has not yet been found". From the top of
Baibhār he saw through binoculars a small tumulus by a detached hill near Sonagiri, in the defile W of old Rajgir. He
had "no doubt" it was the stūpa long looked for near Vyāsa's hill (09.02.03b), but he never reached it. He judged a
natural cave on Baibhār "too far east" for the Asura cave.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 228–229) and RV24 (1957), after Stein, argue for "the great cave of Rajpind,
which is clearly the same as" the Asura palace. It is on the N face of Chandu hill, about 2 miles from Jethian, about
90 ft deep, with a fissure that "the people believe runs far into the mountain" and a sheer rock mass over the
entrance. "A striking confirmation" is an old paved road on massive walls from Khiri to a platform near the cave.
The same pages put Vyāsa's dwelling at a recess at the S foot of Bhaluahi hill. Majumdar gives a recess at the SW end
of the Handia range.

**Assessment.** The survey failed to find the cave. Stein's Rajpind cave, reported in the gazetteers, is the only
specific candidate: its form matches the legend and an ancient road leads to it. It should enter the table as a
probable identification. Beglar's sighting near Sonagiri is unverified.

### 09.02.04 矩奢掲羅補羅城 (上茅宮城) (Old Rājagṛha) — the ramparts measured

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Old Rājagṛha (Girivraja) in the valley ringed by hills; parts of the ridge walls
survive; the new city lies outside its N gate (agreed). Cunningham (1871, pp. 529–532), Beal, Watters and Dey agree.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR I, pp. 22–24) surveyed the ramparts. The inner circuit is 24,500 ft (4⅔ miles), "between
the two statements of the Chinese pilgrims". He traced the outer wall over the hills, 13 ft thick, and found the
distances between the hills totalling 41,000 ft, so he reads 50 li for Xuanzang's 150, "a correction which is
absolutely necessary". He gives the new town (09.02.18) as an irregular pentagon of 14,260 ft circuit with a citadel
of 2,000 × 1,500 ft. In ASR III (p. 143) he found the walls 18 ft thick at the S gate between Sonagiri and Udayagiri.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) gives the outer wall as about 8 miles round and 13 ft thick and the inner as about
4.5 miles, which are Cunningham's figures. BG26 adds nothing to the location.

**Assessment.** The site was never in doubt. The survey supplies the measured walls behind Mizutani's "ridge walls
survive" and makes clear that one of Xuanzang's figures has to be emended.

### 09.02.09 姞栗陀羅矩吒山 (Gṛdhrakūṭa) — the Sailagiri spur with the Bimbisāra road (Chhathagiri)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Gṛdhrakūṭa at Rajgir with no modern name. He relies on Takakusu's photographs, the
Bimbisāra road with the bricks of the "dismount" and "turn back" stūpas, and the summit vihāra (agreed). Cunningham
(p. 534): part of Sailagiri, "no cave found". Beal (p. 153) and Dey follow him. Watters (p. 152): "possibly correct".
Julien (after Klaproth): "Giddore" on the maps.

**Survey.** In his report Beglar (ASR VIII) had made the Son Bhandar and its neighbour Buddha's and Ānanda's caves.
His Note (pp. xiv–xxi) retracts this: "I ... am compelled to consider my previous identifications of these two caves
incorrect." He moved them to the NE spur of Sailagiri, where he found a natural cave with two entrances "in the
northern shade" of a craggy peak, with Gupta-style sculptures and a Buddha head. A second cave or a great block lies
50–100 ft NW (Ānanda, 09.02.10). He adds a summit terrace with a small tope, a second tope at a pass, and rock ledges in
the torrent. He traced the stone ramp R.R., on which
Broadley had put two small stūpas, but thought it "a line of defence", not the road to the caves.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) repeats Sailagiri, "no trace now" of the caves. BG26 (Patna 1907, after D. N. Sen)
names the hill Chhathagiri, "with the 'Bimbisara road' or stone causeway leading up to it, which still shows traces of
the two stupas", and the vihāra at the W end of the precipice.

**Assessment.** Beglar's Note brings the survey round to Cunningham's hill and supplies the caves Cunningham could not
find. His ramp is evidently BG26's causeway, but he denies that it is the road, against Takakusu and Mizutani. The
hill is secure. Which remains are the two stūpas and Ānanda's cave is not settled.

### 09.02.11a 卑鉢羅石室 (阿素洛宮) (Pippala cave) — the cave behind Jarasandh-ki-baithak, N face of Vaibhāra

**Identifications.** Mizutani: following Takakusu, a cave on the N face of Vaibhāra. It is an old gold-mine shaft,
with the "Pippala cave" built in front of it (uncertain). Xuanzang, however, puts the cave on Vipula. Cunningham
(1871, p. 531) places Faxian's cave on Ratnagiri. Beal (p. 156) suggests the Sonbhandar.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR III, pp. 140–142) abandoned Ratnagiri. In January 1872 he found and cleared a cave 40 ft
long behind the Jarasandh-ki-baithak, a stone platform 85 ft square on the NE slope of Baibhār: "This, then, was the
Pippal cave ... in which Buddha had actually dwelt". He needed it as the starting point for Faxian's distance to the
Saptaparṇa cave (09.02.16). Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 89) accepts it, though he disputes everything else Cunningham
identified at Rajgir.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) repeats the abandoned Ratnagiri.

**Assessment.** The survey's final site agrees with Mizutani's: a cave on the N face of Vaibhāra with a built
structure in front. Both survey authors accept it. The discrepancy with Xuanzang's "Vipula" remains.

### 09.02.12 大石室 (提婆達多入定) — cave M on the slope of Vipula (Beglar)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Faxian's 調達石窟, 3 li E of the old city's N gate; such caves are old gold-mine
workings (uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Survey.** Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 90) points to a cave "on the slope of Vipula", marked M on Cunningham's plan
(ASR III, pl. 41) but not described in his text: "This cave, therefore, may be appropriately called Devadatta's cave."

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** Beglar gives a particular cave in the position Mizutani takes from Faxian. It strengthens Mizutani,
but it rests on a letter on a plan and no description, so the confidence stays low.

### 09.02.14 阿闍多設咄路王舍利塔 (Ajātaśatru's relic stūpa) — probably the mound E of the road (Takakusu)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: probably the raised ground E of the present road, now a Muslim cemetery, after
Takakusu (uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing. The Patna volumes describe the walls of old and new Rajgir but not this stūpa, and the
survey passages coded here do not name it.

**Assessment.** Takakusu's mound remains the only candidate.

### 09.02.16 大石室 (第一結集) (Saptaparṇa cave) — N side of Baibhār; the survey split

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Saptaparṇa cave on Baibhāra hill, without naming a particular cave (agreed).
Cunningham (1871, p. 531): tentatively the Son Bhandar in the S face. Beal (p. 156) rejects it. Dey: Beglar's cave
about a mile W of the Pippala cave on the N side.

**Survey.** Cunningham's tentative view hardened. Once he had found the Pippala cave (09.02.11a), he wrote of the Son
Bhandar "which we can now say with absolute certainty was the famous Sattapani cave" (ASR III, pp. 140–144). He
argues that it is the only chisel-cut cave, that its socket holes show a front hall (the platform 90 ft long is
Ajātaśatru's hall), and that his own survey puts it in the "northern half" of the mountain. He repeated this against
Beglar in his preface to ASR VIII (p. viii). Beglar (ASR VIII, pp. 92–99, 121) walked Faxian's 5–6 li W from the
Pippala cave. He found "a large natural cavern" facing W on the N slope, invisible from the E, with brick-stepped
approaches, a half-way platform and a ruined caitya just W where Xuanzang puts a stūpa, in what Xuanzang calls the
bamboo forest. The Son Bhandar faces S and is not in "the northern shade".

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (1877) repeats "on the side of Baibhar". BG26 (Patna 1907) has Stein's natural caves on the N
scarp, with a walled 100-ft terrace and a stratum split into seven, and Marshall's built-up spur further W.

**Assessment.** Xuanzang's "north side of the southern hill" and three independent field claims (Beglar's, Stein's
and Marshall's) point to the N side. Only Cunningham, by reinterpreting "north", keeps the Son Bhandar. Mizutani's
Baibhāra is strengthened. The exact spot is open.

### 09.02.16a 大衆部結集處堵波 (Mahāsāṃghika council stūpa) — unidentified

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification; his notes discuss only the schism (unknown). No authority gives a
site.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing, and the survey is silent.

**Assessment.** Unidentified. The stūpa lies 20-odd li W of Ānanda's stūpa, which is itself unlocated, so the route
cannot be tested against the ground.

### 09.02.17a 無憂王堵波・石柱 (迦蘭陀池西北) — SW of New Rājagṛha: Cunningham's hollow mound

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "probably" near the present Baitarni Godan, SW of New Rājagṛha, after Takakusu
(uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR I, pp. 24–25) identifies the stūpa of 60 ft with its 50-ft pillar with "this circular
hollow", a large mound outside the SW corner of New Rajgir, robbed of its bricks like the excavated stūpa at Sarnath.
He found no pillar.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** Cunningham and Takakusu both place the stūpa just SW of the new town. The files do not say whether the
hollow mound is at Baitarni Godan, but the two suggestions agree in area. That is weak support for Mizutani.

### 09.03.01 那爛陀僧伽藍 (Nālandā) — Bargaon, named in inscriptions found on the site

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Nālandā ruins at Bargaon, 7 miles N of Rājgir (agreed). All the authorities
agree: Cunningham (pp. 536–537), Vivien (p. 382), Beal (p. 167), Majumdar (p. 721, from inscriptions naming Nālandā),
Dey. Watters (p. 166) says it "may be correct".

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR I, pp. 28–35) argues from Faxian's and Xuanzang's distances and from two inscriptions
naming Nālandā found on the site. He traced a monastic field of 1,600 × 400 ft, with the courtyards of six
monasteries showing as square cultivated patches. He placed the sub-sites by lettered mounds: A (09.03.06); B, C; the
Avalokiteśvara temple at G; Bālāditya's vihāra at H, E of the Dehar tank; the Tārā vihāra, whose base he excavated
(70½ × 67 ft); the well P; and the Nāga's tank.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) and BG26 (Patna 1907) repeat Bargaon and Cunningham's sub-sites, with Spooner's
excavations added in BG26. The passages coded `doubts` (HU11, after Broadley) concern Faxian's hill (09.03.11).

**Assessment.** Not in dispute. The inscriptions settle it, and the gazetteers echo the survey.

### 09.03.06 精舍 (如來三月說法處, 那爛陀伽藍西) — Cunningham's mound A (Spooner's Site No. 3)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "probably" Yijing's Mūlagandhakuṭī among the caityas W of the monastery row. He leaves
the matching of the texts with the excavation to specialists (uncertain). No authority gives a site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR I, pp. 29–30) identifies the temple "just outside the western wall" with "the ruined mound
marked A, still 63 feet in height", immediately W of the monastery and E of the Punwa tank. His excavation to 17 ft
"exposed the straight walls of a temple". The mounds B and C to the S are the stūpa and the Avalokiteśvara statue,
in Xuanzang's order.

**Gazetteers.** BG26 (Patna 1907) reports Spooner's excavation of Site No. 3, "Cunningham's vihara A", which Spooner
"would identify with the stupa mentioned by Hiuen Tsiang as marking the place where the Lord Buddha dwelt for three
months". The excavation found three successive masonry encasements, stucco Buddhas and four corner towers.

**Assessment.** The identification goes back to Cunningham's survey. Spooner's excavation confirms a major shrine at
that spot, and it lies in the area Mizutani indicates. Two independent field workers now point to the same mound, so
confidence rises to medium.

### 09.03.08 拘理迦邑 (Kolika) — unidentified; Cunningham's Jagdispur mound unconfirmed

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no modern identification; he equates it with the 拘離迦 village of the 仏本行集経
(unknown). Cunningham (1871, p. 538): a ruined mound near Jagdispur, 1¼ miles SW of Bargaon.

**Survey.** The 1871 view comes from ASR I (pp. 28–29). The mound is about 200 ft square with a 70-ft-square
eminence, and a large sculpture (15 × 9½ ft) of the Buddha under the Bodhi tree lies there. The case rests on the
bearing and the 8–9 li.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** The survey shows a Buddhist site in the right place, but nothing ties it to Maudgalyāyana's
birthplace. Mizutani's reserve is reasonable. No change.

### 09.03.10 迦羅臂拏迦邑 (Kālapināka) — = Faxian's Nāla; no site

**Identifications.** Mizutani: probably another name for Faxian's 那羅聚落 (Nāla), Śāriputra's birthplace
(uncertain). Watters (p. 171) and Beal (p. lviii) agree. Cunningham (1871, p. 538; ASR I, pp. 28–29) and Dey place it
about 4 miles SE of Nālandā without naming a village. Vivien (p. 384) separates Faxian's Nāla from it.

**Gazetteers.** Nothing.

**Assessment.** The equation with Nāla has wide support, but no site has been found, in the survey or elsewhere.

### 09.03.11 因陀羅勢羅窶訶山 (Indraśailaguhā) — Giriyek, with the Haṃsa stūpa and the Gidhadwar cave

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Giriyek hill, 6 miles from Rājgir (agreed). Cunningham (1871, p. 541): Giryek, the cave
being the Gidha-dwar in its S face. Beal (p. 180): the western, higher peak. Majumdar (p. 722, after Stein): "the
rugged hill rising immediately to the W. of the village". Dey: Giriyek. Watters (p. 173): the double identification
with Faxian's hill is "open to objections". Vivien (p. 384, after Kittoe): near Bihār fort.

**Survey.** In ASR I (pp. 16–22) Cunningham notes the only stūpa on the hill, "Jarasandha's Tower" (28 ft across;
a 41-ft shaft found nothing). He found nearby a figure with "a large goose carved on the pedestal" and lac seals
showing a stūpa with a goose, and takes it for the Haṃsa stūpa. In ASR III (pp. 144–150) he rejects Broadley's Bihār.
He sums the route from Nālandā (about 9½ miles ESE), puts the Haṃsa monastery on the oblong terrace of the lower E
peak, and finds Patna–Giryek, 50 miles, equal to 7 yojanas. Beglar (ASR VIII, p. 78) has "no doubt" that the cave is
the Giddha-dwar, facing S at the top of the pass. Cunningham repeats Giryek in ASR XI (p. 187). In ASR XV (p. 7) he
moves Faxian's "small isolated hill" to Parbati, conceding Broadley's point for Faxian only.

**Gazetteers.** BG26 (Patna 1907) argues for Giriak from the Haṃsa stūpa, the summit terraces, a walled road and the
Gidhadwari cave, which "corresponds exactly". This is the survey's case. HU11 (1877), after Broadley, objects only to
the equation with Faxian's hill.

**Assessment.** The goose figures, the single stūpa, the monastery terrace and the cave strengthen Mizutani for
Xuanzang. Cunningham's own concession leaves the equation with Faxian's hill, which Mizutani's note brings in, in
doubt.

### 09.03.13 迦布徳迦伽藍 (Kapotaka) — Parbati ("Ghar Pārāvat") rather than Bihār

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Bihār (Bihar Sharif), after emending 百五六十里 to 五六十里, following Julien and
Cunningham 1871 (agreed). Cunningham (1871, pp. 542–543): Bihār, reading 60 li. Beal (p. 182) reports Cunningham's
later Pārbati, 10 miles NE of Giryek.

**Survey.** The survey moved three times. Beglar (ASR VIII, pp. 108–113), on Cunningham's suggestion, adopted Daryapur
Parvati. He found pigeons nesting in its cliffs, the name Paravata, and many Buddhist structures, though the bearing
and distance from Giriyak "do not agree". Cunningham (ASR XI, p. 187) went back to Bihār with Broadley's Soh-sarai
ruins N of the town, reading 50–60 li. Then in ASR XV (pp. 4–10) he settled on Parbati. Either hill suits the
emended distance, but Bihār is "almost due north" of Giryek and Parbati "as nearly as possible north-east". The
village is also "Ghar Pārāvat, or the 'Pigeon's House'", so "there can be no doubt whatever". The hill (1,750 ×
1,000 ft) is terraced with ruins and lies S of the village, as Xuanzang says. Wedge-shaped stūpa bricks come from
the E side of mound G, where he puts the monastery. On mound D he found 16 granite pillar stumps and a clay lump with
four impressions of a Gupta monastic seal ending *saṅghasa*.

**Gazetteers.** HU11 (Patna 1877) gives Bihār with Broadley's doubt. BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 213–214) and RV24 (1957)
give Parbati, echoing Beglar and ASR XV. The "want of coincidence in position" that RV24 quotes as Cunningham's is
Beglar's sentence (ASR VIII, p. 108).

**Assessment.** Both sites need the distance emended. Parbati has the bearing, a pigeon name, the right lie of hill
and village, and a Buddhist saṅgha attested by a seal. Bihār has only the emended distance. The survey's final
view should replace Mizutani's. No inscription names Kapotaka.

### 09.03.14 孤山 (觀自在菩薩像) — the Parbati hill, site A (dargah of Haji Chandar)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: at Bihār, the town's name taken from this Avalokiteśvara vihāra (uncertain). Cunningham
(1871, p. 543) and Dey: the hill NW of Bihār.

**Survey.** In ASR XI (p. 189) Cunningham takes Bihār hill, flat-topped and "covered with Buddhist remains". In
ASR XV (pp. 8–9) he puts the Avalokiteśvara temple at A, the centre of the Parbati hill, "now occupied by the dargāh
of Haji Chandar ... the Musalman cuckoo having as usual occupied the Hindu nest". Beglar had traced rows of
monastic cells there. Cunningham also notes that the Bihār hill is due W of Bihār town, while Xuanzang puts the hill
S of the monastery.

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906, pp. 213–214) and RV24 (1957) give the dargah site, following ASR XV and Beglar.

**Assessment.** This follows 09.03.13. With Kapotaka at Parbati, the solitary hill is the Parbati hill, and site A is
the survey's choice for the temple. The case is an inference from position and sanctity, with no image found.

### 09.03.15 一伽藍 (孤山東南四十餘里) — Tetrawa or Aphsar, according to the base point

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no settled identification. He reports Cunningham's Titarawa, 7 miles SE of Bihār, and
later Aphsar, reading 40 li as 4 (disputed). Beal (p. 184) reports Aphsar.

**Survey.** In ASR I (p. 40) Titarawa, with its 20-ft monastery mound and a Buddha inscribed under Mahīpāla, is a place
"no mention" of which occurs in the pilgrims. From the Bihār base it became Xuanzang's monastery: 1871, then ASR XI
(pp. 184, 189), where pedestal inscriptions name Tentaḍi-grāma, the hill is "just 6 miles, or 36 li", SE of Bihār, and
there are a monastery and great stūpa. With the base moved to Parbati, Cunningham (ASR XV, pp. 10–11) finds "no
remains of any kind" 40 li SE and reads 4 li, to the conical mound of Aphsar. He calls it "an important Brahmanical
site, and only an unimportant Buddhist settlement, as might indeed be inferred from Hwen Thsang's omission to mention
its name."

**Gazetteers.** BG27 (Gaya 1906) "doubts" Aphsar on the ground of the pilgrims' silence. This is Cunningham's own
caveat, repeated, not an independent objection.

**Assessment.** The survey makes the choice depend wholly on 09.03.13. Tetrawa fits the text unemended but only from
Bihār. Aphsar follows from Parbati but needs a second emendation. It stays unidentified, with Aphsar the consequence
of the better-supported Kapotaka.

### 09.03.15a 大聚落 (殑伽河南) — Shekhpura (Cunningham's revised view)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Shekhpur, 20 miles from the Ganges, after emending 70余里 to 170余里 (uncertain).
Cunningham (1871, pp. 543–545): near Daryapur, 34 miles W of Monghyr. Beal (p. 184): Shekhpura, after ASR XV.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, pp. 12–13) revises to Shekhpura from bearing and distance. Xuanzang's next stage
"through forests and gorges" suits it: "the pretty and well-wooded oasis of Shekhpura". He notes the Kali-math-pokhar
tank. No remains are tied to the village.

**Gazetteers.** BG33 (Monghyr 1909) and RV27 (1960) repeat ASR XV, with "few ancient remains" and the Mathokar Tal
2 miles W.

**Assessment.** Mizutani follows the survey's revised view and the gazetteers echo it. It rests on route and
landscape only, so it stays uncertain.

### 09.03.15b 落般膩羅聚落 (Lo-in-ni-lo) — Rajjana (Rajaona), near Lakhisarai

**Identifications.** Mizutani: unsettled, "awaits further study" (disputed). Vivien (p. 385): Roynallah on Rennell's
map. Cunningham (1871, p. 545): Rohinila, after Vivien. Dey reports Rajaona or Kiul and defends Vivien's village. Beal
(p. 184): Rajjāna. Watters (p. 176) rejects the restoration Rohinila.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, pp. 12–15) gives up Rohinila for Rajjana on the W bank of the Kiyul, 2 miles NW of
Lakhi-Sarai. The route-book distance onward to Monghyr (30½ miles against 200 li) fits. The Balguzar Jhil is the great
lake N of the stūpa, and the northern mounds yield Buddha and Padmapāṇi statues: "I conclude, therefore, with some
confidence".

**Gazetteers.** BG33 (Monghyr 1909) and RV27 (1960) set out Cunningham's successive proposals (Nongarh, Kiul/Jaynagar,
Rajaona) and argue for Rajaona. It "still possesses a large sheet of water to the north", and mounds stretch for 4
miles. This is the ASR XV case.

**Assessment.** Only Rajaona has features matching the text: the lake to the N, Buddhist remains and the onward
distance. Cunningham's earlier proposals and the absence of an inscription keep the confidence at medium.
