# Notes on disputed places: juan 10–11

Eastern India, the Deccan and the south, Siṃhala, western India, Sindh, Persia. The places follow master-table order.
Sources are `places.tsv`, `places_mizutani.tsv`, `coded/auth_*.tsv`, the gazetteer rows in `coded/gaz_batch*.tsv`
and the Archaeological Survey rows in `coded/asr_batch*.tsv` (ASR volume and page).
Gazetteer volumes are counted once, even when two scans exist. The hit numbers (G#) point to `gaz_hits.tsv`.

### 10.01 伊爛拏鉢伐多國 (Hiraṇyaparvata) — Monghyr; secure

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital Monghyr (Mudgagiri) (agreed). Vivien (p. 386), Cunningham (pp. 545–6),
Beal (p. 186), Watters (p. 179, tentative), Majumdar (p. 722) and Dey all give Monghyr. They differ only over the
Sanskrit form of the name.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80, p. 16) keeps Monghyr and proposes a new source for the name: the local
Harana-Parvata, from the Kashta-harana ghat, "is clearly the original of Hwen Thsang's I-lan-na-Po-fa-to", not
Julien's Hiraṇya ("Hill of Gold"). The site does not move.

**Gazetteers.** Monghyr 1909 and Monghyr 1960 accept Monghyr and argue for it from the old names Mudgalapuri,
Mudgalāśrama and Mudgalagiri, and from the Pāla inscriptions' Mudgagiri (G#269). They add that the sage's hermitage
at Kashtaharini ghat "is still a Hindu pilgrimage place" (G#270/1584). Bengal 1902 repeats the identification.

**Assessment.** Nobody contests the site. The survey's Harana etymology and the gazetteers' local names bear only
on the name, and Mizutani stands.

### 10.01.02 伊爛拏山 — the hill at Monghyr; Mizutani's Pirpahar is a guess

**Identifications.** Mizutani: perhaps Pirpahar hill, 3 miles E of Monghyr (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 545) and Beal
(p. 186) give the isolated hill at Monghyr. Vivien (p. 386) gives the range of hills ending at the Ganges at Monghyr,
with a chain of hot springs (the "smoking" mountain).

**Gazetteers.** Monghyr 1909 and Monghyr 1960 follow Waddell. They read the smoke and vapour as "historic testimony
to the former existence in this region of an active volcano", shown by the hot springs of the Kharagpur hills
(Sitakund, Bhimbandh, Rishikund) (G#54/1558). They name no single peak.

**Assessment.** Everyone puts the mountain in the Monghyr hills. Vivien and the gazetteers favour the hot-spring
range over any single hill. Pirpahar (Mizutani's own suggestion) has no support in these sources, but it does not
contradict them. Mizutani's general location stands. The specific hill remains open.

### 10.01.04 小孤山 — Uren hill (Waddell); the survey's Mahadeva has no remains

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Uren hill, 20 miles SW of Monghyr (agreed, after Waddell). Cunningham (ASR XV, as
reported by Beal p. 190; not in the 1871 Geography): Mahadeva hill, E of the central Monghyr hills. Watters (p. 180)
and Majumdar (p. 722): Uren.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80, pp. 18–19) proposed "the hill of Mahadeva and the hot springs of
Bhimband", 11 miles from the Kiyul on the western frontier of Monghyr district. The hill is isolated and has two
peaks, and the springs on its north side flow into the Mon river; they are "the hottest in the district", which
matches the "extremely hot" springs of the text. On the ground, though, he found that "Nothing in fact now remains
save the two peaked isolated hills and the hot springs": the cut stones had been carried off to Kharagpur and the
Panch Kumar figures were gone.

**Gazetteers.** Monghyr 1909 and Monghyr 1960 give Waddell's answer to the survey:
- Mahadeva is doubtful because its springs lie on the hill itself and it has "no remains but a small brick linga
  shrine" (G#264/1568).
- Uren's position and shape fit; its top is called Lorik ka ghar, and the yakṣa's name Bakula survives in the
  village Bakura and the cult of Ban-Bakura Nath.
- A footprint rock has a brick mound above it "which may mark the remains of a small stupa" (G#278/1580), with
  votive statues, chaityas and carved names. Bloch (ASI) dates the stupa tracings near Xuanzang's time (G#281/1583).

Jaynagar (G#266/1570) and Bateshwar-Sthan (Bhagalpur 1962, G#1432–3) are passing suggestions.

**Assessment.** Mahadeva rests on the two peaks and the springs; Cunningham's own visit found nothing of the cave,
stupa or footprints. Uren has the footprints and a stupa mound. Mizutani is strengthened.

### 10.02 瞻波國 (Campā) — Champanagar near Bhagalpur; secure

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Campānagara and Campāpura near Bhagalpur (agreed). Cunningham (pp. 546–7), Beal
(p. 191), Watters (p. 182) and Dey agree. Vivien (p. 386) gives only the distance from Monghyr.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80, pp. 32–33) keeps Champa at Bhagalpur. He identifies the herdsman's long
gallery of the Life with the Mayaganj cave at Bhagalpur, where punch-marked silver coins had been found, while
admitting "there are no rocks at the mouth of the Mayaganj cave at the present day" (see the final section).

**Gazetteers.** Bhagalpur 1911, Bhagalpur 1962, Santal Parganas 1910 and Bengal 1902 repeat this and Cunningham's
boundaries of Aṅga (Lakhisarai to Rajmahal). Bhagalpur 1962 puts forward Bateshwar-Sthan (near Colgong) as the
ancient site (G#1432–3). Its reason is that Faxian's 13 yojanas from Pāṭaliputra (about 144 miles) could fit if the
Ganges has changed course. The gazetteer itself says "later researches and excavations may be awaited".

**Assessment.** The Bateshwar suggestion concerns Faxian's Campā and the Vakula hill. It brings no evidence against
Champanagar. Mizutani stands.

### 10.02.02a 孤嶼 — the rocky islets at Kahalgaon (Colgong), with the temple ruins on the summit

**Identifications.** Mizutani identifies only the landscaped pool, perhaps the Gaggarāpokkharaṇī. He leaves the
island itself unidentified (uncertain). Cunningham (1871, p. 546): the rocky island opposite Patharghata, 24 miles E
of Bhagalpur. Vivien (p. 386): the rock of Patharghat. Beal (p. 192) reports Cunningham's later choice, Kahalgaon.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80, pp. 34–35) moved a few miles upstream from Patharghata: "Both bearing and
distance point to the rocky hill of Kahalgaon (Kolgong of the maps), which is just 23 miles to the east of
Bhagalpur." There are three rocky islets; the Ganges has since left them, but the old channel survives as the
Marganga ("dead Ganges"), still the main channel when he steamed up it in 1837. On the summit terrace stood "a large
brick temple with white sandstone pilasters and doorway", door jambs still standing, with heaps of large bricks and
carved bricks — "no doubt the ruins of the temple seen by Hwen Thsang". A small rock-cut temple lies below the
summit.

**Gazetteers.** Bhagalpur 1911 echoes the survey: the islet at Colgong has "the only rock-hewn temple in Bengal",
and "On the summit of the same rock there was a large temple in Hiuen Tsiang's time"; it notes the sacred northward
bend of the river (G#16). The 1911 and 1962 volumes repeat the old district gazetteer's remark that the temple
"appears to have been visited" by Xuanzang (G#166/1848).

**Assessment.** Distance, bearing, an island once in the main stream, and a ruined temple on the summit all fit.
This fills the gap Mizutani leaves; his Gaggarā pond lay at Campā itself, not 150 li east.

### 10.03 羯朱嗢祇羅國 (Kajaṅgala) — Rajmahal (Kankjol); the minority sites lack support

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Rajmahal, formerly Kānkjol (agreed). Cunningham (p. 548): the Rajmahal district, named
after Kankjol, 18 miles S of Rajmahal. Majumdar (p. 723): Kajaṅgala, capital at Rajmahal. Vivien (p. 387): the
village of Kadjéri, below Farrakabad, opposite Gaur. Beal (p. 193, tentative): Kajeri on Rennell's map, 92 miles
from Campā. Dey: Kajra station in Monghyr district, which is west of Campā. Watters (p. 183) gives no site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80, pp. 37–39) repeats that the 400 li from Champa point to the neighbourhood
of Rajmahal, then adds that Kankjol itself, "not more than 70 miles from Bhagalpur by the direct route through the
hills", has "at least as good a claim as Rajmahal to represent the capital". He names no remains there.

**Gazetteers.** Santal Parganas 1910 repeats Cunningham, noting that Kankjol is "still extant 18 miles S of
Rajmahal" (G#1). Hooghly 1972 and Howrah 1972 repeat Majumdar's "territory around Rajmahal".

**Assessment.** Dey's Kajra lies in the wrong direction from Campā. The Kajeri of Vivien and Beal rests only on a
name on a map. The survey leaves the choice between Rajmahal and Kankjol open within the same district, and Mizutani
stands.

### 10.03.01a 大臺 — Teliagarhi (Cunningham, ASR XV)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no book identifies the brick-and-stone platform on the northern border near the
Ganges (unknown). None of the coded authorities name it.

**Survey.** The identification is Cunningham's (ASR XV, 1879–80, p. 39). The walls and towers of Teliyagarhi
(Teliya-gali), "built in alternate courses of stones and bricks", recalled the tower built "partly of stone and
partly of brick"; with its place on the northern frontier and the south bank of the Ganges, "this tower was most
probably situated at Teliya-garhi itself". The place, an old military post commanding a pass into Bengal, had held
many Buddhist and Brahmanical statues, most since moved to Kahalgaon and lost. He himself calls the mixed masonry
"the common style of building in this part of the country from time immemorial".

**Gazetteers.** Santal Parganas 1910 (G#2/85) quotes this survey passage. The "panels filled with figures of saints,
Buddhas and Devas" it cites are Cunningham's paraphrase of Xuanzang's tower, not a description of Teliagarhi.

**Assessment.** This is the only candidate in these sources, against Mizutani's "unidentified". It rests on
position and on a building style that Cunningham admits was general; no part of the tower was found. Worth
recording, with low confidence.

### 10.04 奔那伐彈那國 (Puṇḍravardhana) — Mahasthan, found by the survey and supported by the Bogra gazetteer

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Rajshahi–Bogra area, capital Mahāsthān (agreed). Vivien (p. 388): Burdwan. Cunningham
(1871, pp. 549–50): Pubna. Fergusson (in Watters p. 184, Beal p. 194): near Rangpur. Westmacott (in Beal): Panjara or
Pandua. Majumdar (p. 724) follows Cunningham's later Mahasthan and rejects Pubna. Dey: North Bengal.

**Survey.** Cunningham gave up his Pubna in ASR XV (1879–80): "I was fortunate enough to discover the site of the
ancient city in Mahasthan on the Karatoya River" (p. v). His grounds (pp. 102–112):
- Mahasthan lies "just 100 miles to the east" of Rajmahal, the 600 li of the text, and local opinion holds it the
  ancient capital of Barendra.
- Bhasu Bihar, 4 miles W, matches the Po-shi-po monastery 4 miles W of the capital: a brick stūpa mound 30 ft high,
  a carved-brick temple to its north, and the Bihar mound (700 × 600 ft) as the monastery.
- His measured survey gives the fortified city 4,500 × 3,000 ft (15,000 ft round), with the suburbs "rather less
  than 4½ miles", against 30 li.
- He rejects Westmacott's Bardhan Kuti as a recent place. He found nothing Buddhist at Mahasthan itself, which was
  thick with jungle.

**Gazetteers.** Bogra 1910 repeats the survey's distance argument and adds what Cunningham lacked: the Karatoyā
Māhātmya, the Skanda temple site, and "the buried ruins of a Buddhistic temple" found in 1907, with no Buddhist
remains at Pabna or Pandua (G#122/127/133). Malda 1918 argues for Pandua but concedes the distance problem (G#46–49).
Purnea 1911 gives the Karatoyā as the eastern boundary.

**Assessment.** Distance, the Bhasu Bihar name and mounds, the circuit, the river and the 1907 Buddhist finds all
point to Mahasthan. Mizutani is strengthened.

### 10.05 迦摩縷波國 (Kāmarūpa) — W Assam with Gauhati; the "great river" crossed was the Karatoyā

**Identifications.** Mizutani: W Assam, capital Gauhati, with the great river taken as the Brahmaputra (disputed).
Cunningham (pp. 573–4): capital Kamatipura in Cooch Behar, with the river taken as the Tista. Watters (p. 186),
Majumdar (p. 729), Beal (p. 195), Vivien (p. 389) and Dey give W Assam/Gauhati or the Karatoyā-to-Gauhati tract.

**Gazetteers.** Bengal 1902 and Bogra 1910 (citing Gait and the Yoginī Tantra) say of the old Karatoyā: "It was no
doubt the great river crossed by Hiuen Tsiang on his way to Kamarupa" (G#123/1920). Their evidence is that the
Karatoyā was the western boundary of Kāmarūpa and that the Bakhtiyar Khilji narrative describes it as three times
the width of the Ganges.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers do not touch the capital. Gauhati stands, and Majumdar removes Cunningham's reason for
Kamatipura, since Pubna is not Puṇḍravardhana. They do, however, identify the river crossed at the start of the
900-li march as the Karatoyā near Mahasthan, not the Brahmaputra or the Tista. This fits a Mahasthan capital for
10.04.

### 10.06 三摩呾吒國 (Samataṭa) — eastern delta; the gazetteers reject Jessore

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ganges delta, capital Bād-Kāmta near Comilla (disputed). Cunningham (pp. 574–6): the
delta, capital probably Jessore. Fergusson (in Watters, Majumdar): Sonargaon. Watters (p. 188): Faridpur district.
R. D. Banerjee (in Majumdar pp. 729–31): Comilla. Dey: Comilla, Noakhali and Sylhet from the epigraphy. Vivien
(p. 390): Dacca province and the Sundarbans. Beal: Eastern Bengal.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80) equates Samataṭa with Bāgdi, the delta between the Jalangi and the
Meghna, and names Bikrampur as that division's chief town, with Krishnanagar and Jessore also large towns. He does
not name Xuanzang's capital and reports no remains.

**Gazetteers.**
- Jessore 1912 and 24 Parganas 1914 reject Jessore on Xuanzang's distances from Kāmarūpa. It "appears more
  reasonable to identify the capital with Dacca, as Fergusson does, or with the chief town of the Bikrampur pargana"
  (G#42/10, following M. Chakravarti).
- Nadia 1910 argues that the country included Nadia.
- Khulna 1908 doubts that the young delta of Khulna was included.
- Faridpur 1908 and Bengal 1902 equate Samataṭa with Vaṅga.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers move the capital east of Jessore, to Dacca or Bikrampur, and the survey had already
let Jessore drop to one town among several. That points the same way as Mizutani's Comilla-area Bād-Kāmta, although
none of these sources knows that site. Mizutani stands.

### 10.06.02a 室利差呾羅國 (Śrīkṣetra) — Prome; the Indian alternatives rest on the bearing

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Thare Khettara near Prome (uncertain). He notes that Prome lies SE, not NE, of
Samataṭa. Beal (p. 200, tentative) and Dey agree. Vivien (p. 391) and Cunningham (p. 576): Sylhet (Śrīhaṭṭa).
Watters (p. 189): roughly the Tipperah district.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Prome has the matching name (Śrīkṣetra) but not the direction. Sylhet and Tipperah have the direction
but only a weaker match of names. Since Xuanzang knew this country by hearsay, the name should carry more weight than
the bearing. Mizutani stands, with low to medium confidence.

### 10.06.02b 迦摩浪迦國 (Kāmalaṅkā) — Pegu and the Irrawaddy delta

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Pegu and the Irrawaddy delta (uncertain). Beal (p. 200): the same, as Rāmañña.
Watters (p. 189) reports the same. Cunningham (pp. 576–7, tentative): the Comilla district in Tipera.

**Gazetteers.** Tippera 1910 repeats Cunningham's Comilla: "This Kamalanka is generally identified with Comilla"
(G#138). It offers no evidence beyond Cunningham.

**Assessment.** The gazetteer's "generally" overstates Cunningham's tentative suggestion. The equation with
Yijing's 郎迦戍 supports Pegu. Mizutani stands.

### 10.06.02c 墮羅鉢底國 (Dvāravatī) — Dvāravatī (Siam)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ayutthaya, as Dvāravatī (agreed). Dey: Siam. Beal (p. 200): the classic name of
Sandoway (Arakan), also applied to Siam. Watters (p. 189) rejects Dvāravatī and reads Talapati. Cunningham (p. 577):
perhaps Talaingvati, that is, Pegu.

**Gazetteers.** Tippera 1910 says that "perhaps To-lo-po-ti may stand for Tippera" (G#138). This is a guess from the
sound of the name.

**Assessment.** The reading Dvāravatī, the Mon kingdom of Siam, is the best supported. The Tippera guess adds
nothing. Mizutani's equation with Ayutthaya specifically is a precision issue, not a rival site. Mizutani stands.

### 10.06.02d 伊賞那補羅國 (Īśānapura) — Cambodia

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Zhenla/Cambodia, capital perhaps Sambor Prei Kuk (agreed). Watters (p. 189):
Cambodia. Cunningham (p. 577): the Shan country or Laos.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Īśānapura is Īśānavarman's capital of Zhenla, so Cunningham's Laos is superseded. Mizutani stands.

### 10.06.02e 摩訶瞻波國 (Mahācampā) — Champa (Linyi)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Champa (Linyi), SE Indochina (agreed). Julien (p. 85), Watters (p. 189) and
Cunningham (p. 577): Champa, Cochin-China or Annam. Dey lists "Siam, according to Hiuen Tsiang" (from Beal's Life),
besides Yule's Tonquin–Cambodia.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Dey's Siam is a dictionary cross-reference, not an argued identification. Mizutani stands.

### 10.06.02f 閻摩那洲國 (Yamanadvīpa) — unidentified; Java doubtful

**Identifications.** Mizutani: perhaps Java (Yavadvīpa) (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 577): "almost certainly"
Java. Watters (p. 189): "it cannot possibly have been the island of Java". Beal (p. 200): the Yamadvīpa of the Vāyu
Purāṇa, probably fabulous.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** These sources settle nothing. Java rests on emending the name, so Mizutani's "perhaps" is the right
level of confidence.

### 10.07 耽摩栗底國 (Tāmralipti) — Tamluk; secure

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Tamluk (agreed). All the authorities agree: Vivien p. 392, Cunningham p. 577, Beal
p. 200, Watters p. 189, Majumdar p. 732, Dey. Fergusson's Satgaon is rejected by Watters.

**Gazetteers.** Midnapur–Hugli–Howrah 1875–77 argues that Tamluk, now about 60 miles from the sea, was once a port.
Workers "come upon sea shells at a depth of from ten to twenty feet" in wells and tanks, and the place had the old
name Ratnākar (G#337/359). Midnapore 1911, Howrah 1909, Howrah 1972 (with recent ASI excavations at Tamluk) and the
other Bengal volumes repeat the identification.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers explain why an inland town could be Xuanzang's seaport, and so strengthen Mizutani.

### 10.08 羯羅拏蘇伐剌那國 (Karṇasuvarṇa) — Rangamati, doubted in 1914; the survey's Sapharan rests on a name

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Rāṅgāmāṭi, Murshidabad (agreed; no rival reported). Majumdar (p. 733, tentative),
Smith (in Watters p. 192) and Dey: Rangamati. Majumdar also reports Beglar's Sufaran and Fleet's Rohtasgarh.
Cunningham (1871, p. 578): Singhbhum–Barabhum, capital near Bara Bazar. Vivien (p. 392): the Suvarṇarekhā country.

**Survey.** Beglar (ASR VIII, 1872–73) first announces Dulmi in Manbhum as Karṇasuvarṇa (p. 51), then settles on
Sapharan near Suisa, across the river from Dulmi (p. 191). Cunningham placed it near Bara Bazar from bearings and
distances, but "there are not many ruins there" (one small old temple), "while here the remains are numerous"
(loose sculptures, Jain figures, lingas). He adds that "the name Sapharan is apparently the original of Sufalana",
and that the rajas of nearby Karanpur once ruled Dulmi, giving "Karna Sapharana". Dulmi itself has only "a few
fragments of sculpture". No later survey passage returns to the question.

**Gazetteers.**
- Murshidabad 1914 doubts Beveridge's Rangamati: "the name Rangamati is not uncommon, being derived from the red
  laterite soil", Kansonagar is not in use, and the village is on no map before 1726 (G#59/284).
- Manbhum 1911 echoes the survey's candidates — Barabazar (Cunningham), Safaran (Beglar) — and adds Dalmi (Hewitt),
  "where extensive ruins still exist", all uninvestigated (G#141–2). Puri 1977 places the kingdom in Manbhum.

**Assessment.** Beglar's case is a name and loose sculpture; it disposes of Bara Bazar more than it establishes
Sapharan. The gazetteers weaken Rangamati without a better candidate. Later work (not in these sources):
Raktamṛttikā sealings found near Rangamati answer the 1914 doubts.

### 10.08.02 絡多末知僧伽藍 (Raktamṛttikā) — at Rangamati, doubted with the capital

**Identifications.** Mizutani: at Rāṅgāmāṭi, whose name derives from it (agreed). Julien (in Beal p. 202) gives the
Sanskrit form. Majumdar (p. 733) places it near the capital, identified with Rangamati.

**Gazetteers.** Murshidabad 1914 (G#59/283–4) attacks the very name argument behind this identification: "the name
Rangamati is not uncommon, being derived from the red laterite soil that extends from the foot of the Rajmahal hills
... to the Madhupur jungle".

**Assessment.** The monastery's site stands or falls with 10.08, and the 1914 objection weakens the name link, which
is the only evidence offered here. The same later work (not in these sources) found sealings of this monastery near
Rangamati.

### 10.09 烏荼國 (Oḍra) — northern Orissa; capital most often Jajpur

**Identifications.** Mizutani: N Orissa, capital undecided (disputed). He lists Cuttack, Midnapore (Fergusson),
Jajpur, Yajapur (Watters) and Dhauli/Tosalī (高桑). Cunningham (pp. 584–6), Beal (p. 204) and Vivien (p. 394,
tentative): Jajpur on the Baitaraṇī. Watters (p. 194) and Dey: Orissa only.

**Gazetteers.** Balasore 1907 and Cuttack 1906 repeat "The capital, which has been identified with Jajpur ... lay
700 li south-west of Tamralipti" (G#11/182). Puri 1977 traces a route from Manbhum (Karṇasuvarṇa) to Utkala on the
Magadha–Utkala road (G#1528), which concerns the approach, not the capital.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers only repeat the Jajpur view. The country is not in doubt, and the capital remains
open, with Jajpur the most widely held candidate. Mizutani stands.

### 10.09.02 補澁波祇釐僧伽藍 (Puṣpagiri) — Khandagiri/Udayagiri

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Khandagiri, 4 miles W of Bhubaneswar (agreed). Cunningham (p. 587): the Udayagiri and
Khandagiri hills. Dey (tentative): Udayagiri. Beal (p. 205, tentative): perhaps a stupa found near Aska, or else
Cunningham's hills.

**Gazetteers.** The only coded row (Puri 1977, G#1528) concerns the route from Karṇasuvarṇa and says nothing of
Puṣpagiri. It is miscoded here.

**Assessment.** The difference between Udayagiri and Khandagiri is one of precision. Beal's Aska is a passing
alternative. Mizutani stands.

### 10.09.03 折利呾羅城 (Caritra) — port unidentified; Puri doubted, the Prāchī mouth suggested

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Puri or Nendra on the Mahānadī (disputed). Cunningham (pp. 584–5, tentative): Puri.
Waddell (in Watters p. 195, provisionally accepted) and Majumdar (p. 734): Chitratola at Nendra, 15 miles below
Cuttack. Vivien (p. 394): perhaps the Tchatta of the chronicles. Beal (p. 205): Ptolemy's port of departure, with no
site given.

**Gazetteers.**
- Puri 1977 doubts Puri: "the restoration of the word Che-li-ta-lo as Charitra and its identification with the town
  of Puri are open to doubt" (G#1525). Puri 1908 says that Puri cannot be shown to be one of Kongoda's towns (G#308).
- Puri 1908 also proposes a new site. "Tradition speaks of an old port at its mouth", the mouth of the Prāchī, once
  navigable and lined with temples, brick houses and mounds, "which, if true, might, with some reason, be identified
  with the port of Che-li-ta-lo" (G#307).

**Assessment.** The gazetteers weaken Puri. They add the Prāchī-mouth port near Konarak, a candidate no authority
records, though it rests on tradition only. Nendra (Waddell) remains the better-argued alternative to Puri.

### 10.10 恭御陀國 (Koṅgoda) — Chilka coast; the copper plates extend it into Puri district

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ganjam district, capital Jaugada (agreed). Cunningham (p. 587): Ganjam, with the bay
being the Chilka lake. Fergusson (in Beal p. 206 and Watters p. 196): Khordhagarh near Bhubaneswar, or between
Cuttack and Aska. Majumdar (p. 734): the Koṅgoda-maṇḍala of the inscriptions. Vivien (p. 395): near the Chilka.

**Gazetteers.** Puri 1908 argues for Puri district, with the capital "probably old Tosali or medieval Bhubaneswar"
(G#74/303). Its evidence is the name Kongoda/Kongeda in "copper-plate inscriptions found in Khurda subdivision and
the Ganjam Tributary States". It adds that the bay, the towns "stretching from the hills to the sea", and the
strongly Brahmanical religion "agrees perfectly well" with Puri district. Puri 1977 repeats "modern Ganjam", with
Kushan coins on the Chilka spit (G#1529).

**Assessment.** The copper plates show that Koṅgoda straddled the Chilka, covering Khurda and N Ganjam. That keeps
Mizutani's region but opens the capital to a site near Bhubaneswar, as Fergusson proposed. The gazetteer offers an
equally supported alternative for the capital. It does not overturn the region.

### 10.11 羯𩜁伽國 (Kaliṅga) — region clear; capital open

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Kaliṅga S of Ganjam, capital undecided (disputed). Cunningham (pp. 590–1): Rajamahendri
or Koringa. Fergusson (in Watters p. 198): near Kalingapatam. Vivien (p. 395): Kalingapatnam. Smith (in Watters) and
Majumdar (p. 735): Kaliṅganagara, that is, Mukhalingam. Beal (p. 207): probably Rajamahendri. Dey lists Kaliṅga's
capitals at different periods.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The candidates spread from the Godāvarī to the Vaṃśadhārā. Majumdar's Mukhalingam rests on the
capital's attested name, Kaliṅganagara. Kalingapatnam lies on the coast nearby. Mizutani's "undecided" is fair.

### 10.11.02a 大山嶺上石窣堵波 — Mahendragiri

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mahendragiri (uncertain, after Beal p. 209, "perhaps"). Cunningham (p. 592): the
Mahendra Male range at the head of the Rishikulya, Ganjam.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The two identifications agree in substance on the Mahendragiri massif on the northern border of
Kaliṅga. No ruin is named. Mizutani stands, with low confidence for the stupa itself.

### 10.12 憍薩羅國 (Dakṣiṇa Kosala) — upper Mahānadī with Sirpur; the survey moved to Chhattisgarh

**Identifications.** Mizutani: upper Mahānadī, capital Sirpur (disputed). Cunningham (1871, pp. 595–6): Vidarbha,
capital Chanda. Fergusson (in Beal p. 209 and Watters p. 199): Wairagarh or Bhandak. Beal: Nagpur or Amaravati.
Majumdar (pp. 735–6): Sirpur. Vivien (p. 396): near the Wainganga–Wardha confluence. Dey: Gondwana.

**Survey.** Cunningham abandoned Vidarbha in ASR XVII (1881–82, p. 69): Kosala is Mahā-Kosala, that is,
Chhattisgarh, and Xuanzang's 6,000-li circuit "could have been attained by inclusion of the great district of
Vakataka" (Chanda, Nagpur, Seoni) on the west. He names no capital there. In ASR XXI (1883–84, pp. 93–94) he looks
for the capital of Ptolemy's Adisathri, whom he takes as the people of Chhattisgarh, "at Sirpur on the Mahanadi" —
a remark about Ptolemy, not Xuanzang.

**Gazetteers.**
- Bhandara 1908 calls Cunningham's Chanda "in the last degree hypothetical" (G#394).
- Drug 1910, Raipur 1909 and Bilaspur 1910 give a Buddhist line at Bhandak connected with Sirpur, citing Kielhorn's
  Bhandak inscription (G#382/380/392).
- Grant's Central Provinces 1870 argues for the Haihaya kingdom of Ratanpur, that is, Chhattisgarh (G#388).
- Raipur 1973 reports that the Saugar University excavation at Sirpur in the early 1950s "revealed the ruins of two
  large monasteries", with a colossal Buddha, a Padmapāṇi and a Kaiyuan coin (713–41) (G#1467–8).

**Assessment.** The survey's own move from Chanda to Chhattisgarh, and the gazetteers' from Bhandak to Sirpur, end
where Mizutani does. The Sirpur monasteries are the only excavated Buddhist evidence in these sources. Mizutani is
strengthened.

### 10.12.05 跋邏末羅耆釐山 (Bhramaragiri) — unidentified; the survey withdrew its Chanda site

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Faxian's Pārāvata, perhaps Nāgārjunakoṇḍa (uncertain). Dey: Śrīśailam. Burgess (in
Watters p. 208): Śrīparvata/Śrīśailam. Cunningham (1871, p. 601): a rock monastery 50 miles W or SW of Chanda,
possibly Pantlu-kuri. Beal (p. 214), assuming Bhandak: the Winjhasani and Dewala hills.

**Survey.** In ASR XXI (1883–84, pp. 93–94) Cunningham, now placing Kosala in Chhattisgarh (10.12), takes the hill
city of Faxian and Xuanzang as "most probably" Ptolemy's Balanti-purgon, but concedes that "The position of the city
described by the Chinese writers from hearsay has not yet been ascertained". In ASR XVII (p. 69) he had identified
Balanti-purgon with Bandhogarh in Rewa; he does not draw that conclusion for Bhramaragiri.

**Gazetteers.** The coded rows discuss Faxian's five-storeyed monastery, not Xuanzang's mountain: Thana 1882 compares
it with Kanheri and rejects that (G#1872–3); Ratnagiri 1962 suggests the Konkan caves (G#1548); Salem 1883 assigns
it to the Pallavas in the Krishna region (G#466–7). They reach Bhramaragiri only through Mizutani's equation of the
two monasteries.

**Assessment.** Each candidate depends on where the Kosala capital is placed. With Sirpur, the Chanda-side sites
fall away, and the survey itself withdrew its own. The Śrīśailam–Nāgārjunakoṇḍa area of the Krishna remains
Mizutani's conjecture, unchallenged but unproven.

### 10.13 案達羅國 (Andhra) — capital Veṅgī

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital Veṅgī, that is, Pedda and Cinna Vegi near Ellore (agreed). Dey, Beal (p. 217)
and Majumdar (p. 737), after Fergusson: Veṅgī. Cunningham (p. 604): Elgandel, between Bhimgal and Warangal. Vivien
(p. 395): Warangal. Watters (p. 210): perhaps Vinjir.

**Gazetteers.** Salem 1883 repeats Burnell: P'ing-ch'i-lo is Vengi "with the addition of the locative suffix of
Telugu nouns" (G#471).

**Assessment.** Elgandel and Warangal are Telingana guesses from distance. Veṅgī matches the name, and Burnell's
Telugu locative explains the final syllable. Mizutani stands.

### 10.13.03 孤山 — Dignāga's hill; a Puri claim outside Andhra

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no identification (unknown). He gives only the translation, "20 li SW of the
monastery". None of the coded authorities name it.

**Gazetteers.** Puri 1977 (G#1533) proposes the Biswanath hill near Jagadalpur, N of Delang station: "supposed to be
the same as the Bhorasaila ... the seat of the activities of the Buddhist logician Dignaga". It adds that "the name
of the village Delang is considered to be a prakrit variant of the name Dignaga".

**Assessment.** This is a new candidate, but it lies in Oḍra, not in Andhra where Xuanzang puts Dignāga's hill. Its
only evidence is an etymology. "Bhorasaila" may also echo Bhramaragiri (10.12.05). The question stays open, and the
Puri claim should be recorded as low confidence.

### 10.14 駄那羯磔迦國 (Dhānyakaṭaka) — capital at Dharanikota or Bezwada

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital near Dhāraṇikoṭa, S bank of the Krishna (disputed; Mizutani's choice).
Cunningham (p. 611), Beal (p. 220) and Dey: Dharanikota. Fergusson, Burgess and Sewell (in Watters p. 216 and
Majumdar p. 737): Bezwada. Watters prefers Bezwada. Majumdar says that Dharanikota "does not suit" the description.
Vivien (p. 397): Rajahmundry.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** These sources split evenly, with Watters and Majumdar leaning to Bezwada because of its surroundings
(see 10.14.03). Mizutani's choice of Dharanikota is defensible, but these sources do not settle it.

### 10.14.02a 弗婆勢羅僧伽藍 (Pūrvaśaila) — the Amaravati stupa site?

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Amarāvatī great stupa site (agreed). Burgess (in Dey): the same. Watters
(p. 221): "Neither Amaravati nor Vaikunthapur can be said to have any claim". He reports Rea's Pedda Madur.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The identification depends on the capital (10.14). With a Bezwada capital, the eastern and western
monasteries would lie elsewhere. Mizutani stands only as far as Dharanikota does.

### 10.14.02b 阿伐羅勢羅僧伽藍 (Avaraśaila) — Dhāraṇikoṭa, or the Amaravati tope

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Dhāraṇikoṭa, 1 mile W of Amarāvatī (agreed). Fergusson (in Beal p. 221 and Watters
p. 216): the Amaravati tope itself. Beal finds the position a difficulty. Watters rejects both Amaravati and
Vaikunthapur.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Fergusson and Mizutani assign the great tope to opposite monasteries (Avaraśaila and Pūrvaśaila).
The question depends on the capital. Mizutani stands, with low confidence.

### 10.14.03 大山 (阿素洛宮) — the isolated rock S of Bezwada

**Identifications.** Mizutani: none (unknown). Watters (p. 221): "the isolated steep mountain to the south of
Bezwada". Beal (p. 223): the isolated rock with caves immediately S of Bejwada, from Boswell's report via Fergusson.
Sewell (in Watters): the rock-cut temple at Undavalli.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The only candidate in these sources is the Bezwada hill, which presupposes a capital at Bezwada.
Recorded as an alternative to Mizutani's blank, with low confidence.

### 10.15 珠利耶國 (Coḷa) — the Telugu Coḷa country (Renāṇḍu) of Cuddapah

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Penner basin with Nellore (disputed; "諸種の異説"). The authorities disagree:
- Fergusson (in Watters and Beal): Nellore.
- Cunningham (p. 624): Karnul.
- Smith (in Watters p. 225): Cuddapah district.
- Majumdar (p. 738): Coḷa feudatories in Cuddapah, Kurnool and Bellary.
- Vivien (p. 399): midway between the Krishna and the Penner.
- Dey: the Coromandel coast.

**Gazetteers.** Cuddapah 1915 argues: "The doubt has now been cleared up. The Choolya State referred to by Hiuen

**Assessment.** The inscriptions show a Coḷa line in the Cuddapah area, and they agree with Smith, Majumdar and in

not a capital.

### 10.16 達羅毘荼國 (Drāviḍa) — Kāñcīpura; secure

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital Kāñcīpura (Conjeeveram) (agreed). Cunningham (pp. 627–8), Beal (p. 228),
Vivien (p. 397), Majumdar (p. 739) and Dey agree. Fergusson (in Watters p. 226, who leans to it, and rejected by
Beal): Nagapattinam.

**Gazetteers.** Malabar 1887 repeats "the Pallava kingdom" with Kāñcī (G#447).

**Assessment.** Kien-chi-pu-lo is phonetically Kāñcīpura, the Pallava capital. Fergusson's Nagapattinam rests only
on his reconstruction of the route. Mizutani stands.

### 10.17 秣羅矩吒國 (Malakūṭa) — far south; capital near Madurai

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Kumārī area, capital Madurai (agreed). Cunningham (pp. 629–30, tentative):
Madurai. Majumdar (p. 741, after Hultzsch): near Madurai, in the Mīlalaikkūṟṟam. Burnell (in Beal p. 231): the Kāveri
delta, with Beal suggesting Kumbhakonam. Dey: the Coḷa kingdom of Tanjore. Vivien (p. 399): Malabar.

**Gazetteers.** Bombay 1909 and Bombay 1920 (the latter after J. H. Lord) identify Chaul (Cheul, near Revadanda) with
"the 'Tchi-mo-lo' of the Chinese traveller Hwen Thsang" (G#1930/1957). They support this with the Chemula of a Kanheri
inscription.

**Assessment.** Chi-mo-lo (枳秣羅) is Xuanzang's other name for Malakūṭa at the southern tip, so Chaul on the Konkan
coast is a confusion of sounds, not a rival. Hultzsch's Mīlalaikkūṟṟam supports Madurai over the Kāveri delta.
Mizutani stands.

### 10.17.03 秣剌耶山 (Malaya) — southern Western Ghats

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Cardamom hills (uncertain). He writes "Eastern Ghats" by a slip. Dey: the southern
Western Ghats, that is, the Travancore and Cardamom hills. Beal (p. 232): the Ghats S of the Coimbatore gap.
Majumdar (p. 741): the sandal-producing Malaya in Tinnevelly.

**Gazetteers.** Malabar 1887 (G#448) only repeats Xuanzang's sandalwood mountains of "Wo-la-ye". Its "alternative" is
Minicoy, which concerns the women's island (11.20.02b).

**Assessment.** All the sources agree on the southern Ghats. Mizutani stands, once "Western" is read for "Eastern".

### 10.17.04 布呾洛迦山 (Potalaka) — the Podiyil/Pāpanāsam hills, or legendary

**Identifications.** Mizutani: possibly legendary, or the Pāpanāsam hill (高桑) (disputed). Majumdar (p. 741, after
Hultzsch): the Podiyamalai hill. Watters (p. 232): "not to be identified with any one of the mountains by the
sea-side". Beal (p. 233): no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Hultzsch's Podiyamalai and 高桑's Pāpanāsam probably name the same range above Pāpanāsam in
Tinnevelly (a geographical point not stated in these sources). If so, two independent sources point to one hill,
against Watters' scepticism. Mizutani stands. The
real hill is the better-supported of his two options.

### 10.17.04a 海畔城 — Nāgapaṭṭinam (probable)

### 11.02 恭建那補羅國 (Koṅkaṇapura) — capital unresolved; Annagundi the leading view

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Tungabhadrā–Varadā basins, capital Annagundi (disputed). Yazdani's Kuntalapura
(Kubattūr) is also reported. The authorities disagree:
- Cunningham (p. 632) and Dey: Annagundi.
- Vivien (p. 401): Banavasi.
- Burgess (in Majumdar p. 745): Kopal or Kokanur.

- Da Cunha (in Dey): Bassein.

**Assessment.** Burnell's Kankanhalli adds a candidate, but it rests only on a sound that the gazetteer itself

### 11.03 摩訶剌侘國 (Mahārāṣṭra) — capital Nasik, argued by the gazetteers

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital disputed, inclining to the Nasik area (Pulakeśin II having moved north). The
other views:
- Cunningham (p. 634): Kalyāṇī.
- Fleet and Burgess (in Watters p. 238): Nasik. Majumdar (p. 746) also says Xuanzang's capital was Nasik.
- Vivien (p. 401): Paithan or Devagiri.
- Fergusson: Toka or Paithan.
- Beal (p. 255): near the Tapti or Girna.

**Gazetteers.**
- Nasik 1977 and Akola 1977 argue for Nasik, following Fleet, Burgess and Mirashi. The cave monastery "seems to suit
  the caves at Ajanta and as this monastery lay to the east of the capital, the latter appears to be Nasik rather
  than any other place". They add that Pulakeśin's grant of 630 was found at Lohaner in Baglan taluka (G#1553–4/1544).
- Bombay 1909 and Kanara 1883 repeat Burgess' earlier Bādāmi (G#1955/1911).

**Assessment.** The bearing to Ajanta and the Lohaner grant support Nasik. Bādāmi was the dynastic capital, but the
1883 and 1909 volumes do not argue for it from Xuanzang's data. The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani's preference.

### 11.03.03 阿折羅伽藍 — Ajanta, not Kanheri

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Ajanta caves (agreed). Beal (p. 257), Dey, Burgess and Watters (p. 238, "This may
be so"): Ajanta. Cunningham (p. 636): Kanheri on Salsette, reading "west"; Ajanta only if the eastern bearing is
right.

**Gazetteers.**
- Thana 1882 rejects Kanheri. Xuanzang "seems not to have heard of Kanheri", and his sculptured convent fits Ajanta
  better (G#1872–3).
- Nasik 1977 and Akola 1977 take Ajanta as fixed and use it to place the capital (G#1554).

**Assessment.** The gazetteers dispose of Cunningham's Kanheri, and Ajanta, east of Nasik, fits the text's bearing.
They strengthen Mizutani.

### 11.05 摩臘婆國 (Mālava) — Malwa or central Gujarat; unresolved

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives two views: central Gujarat (Kaira–Ahmadabad) or Malwa, and leans to Malwa from

**Gazetteers.** These volumes only repeat:
- Chittaurgarh 1977: "the western Malwa".
- Jhalawar 1964: west of Ujjain, "possibly in Gujerat".
- Sirohi 1967, after the History and Culture of the Indian People: east of the Mahī.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers add no evidence. The coded Majumdar reads the same Valabhī record as favouring Smith's
Gujarat, which is the reverse of Mizutani's use of it. The question stays open.

### 11.05.03 婆羅門邑 — unidentified

### 11.09 阿難陀補羅國 (Ānandapura) — Vadnagar

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Vadnagar (disputed against the older "near Ahmadabad"). Vivien (p. 406), Majumdar
(p. 728), Smith (in Watters) and Dey: Vadnagar. Dey also notes an Anandpur NW of Valabhī. Cunningham (p. 565):

**Gazetteers.** Sirohi 1967, after the History and Culture of the Indian People: "Anandpura (Ahmedabad district)"

**Assessment.** Vadnagar's old name, Ānarttapura/Ānandapura (Dey), makes it the best supported. The 1967 gazetteer

### 11.11 瞿折羅國 (Gurjara) — capital Bhinmal; confirmed locally

**Identifications.** Mizutani: S Rajasthan, capital Bhinmāl (majority) against Bārmer (disputed). Majumdar (p. 697)
and Dey: Bhinmal. Vivien (p. 407), Cunningham (1871, p. 357) and Beal (p. 270): Barmer (Balmer). Watters (p. 250):
perhaps Bhilmala.

**Survey.** Cunningham repeats Balmer twice in ASR II (1862–65), in passing, within his discussions of the Bālas
and of the Gujars: the capital "was Pilomalo, or Balmer, half way between Amarkot and Jodhpur" (cf. p. 34). He
visited neither Balmer nor Bhinmal, and the survey gives no ground evidence.

**Gazetteers.**
- Western Rajputana States 1909 argues for Bhinmal: "The old name of the place was Bhinmal or Bhillamala (the
  Pi-lo-mi-lo of the Chinese pilgrim)". It describes the ruined gate, "a line of mounds covering the ancient site",
  old tanks and wells, and Sanskrit inscriptions (G#1949).
- Pali 1976, Sirohi 1967 and the Bombay volumes (1909, 1912) repeat Bhinmal/Śrīmāl.
- Only Meerut 1875 and an 1874 volume echo Cunningham's Balmer.

**Assessment.** The attested old name Bhillamāla, which matches the transcription, together with the ruins, decides
for Bhinmal. Balmer in the survey is an unargued repetition of 1871. The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani.

### 11.13 擲枳陀國 (Jejākabhukti) — Bundelkhand, named in inscriptions; capital Khajuraho or Eran

**Identifications.** Mizutani: undecided between Bundelkhand (capital Khajurāho) and Chitor (disputed). Cunningham
(1871, pp. 551, 554), Vivien (p. 408) and Dey: Jajhoti/Bundelkhand, capital Khajuraho (or Mahoba). Watters (p. 251,
in Majumdar p. 725): Chitor.

**Survey.** Cunningham's view moved twice:
- ASR II (1864–65, pp. 412–428): Jajhoti is Bundelkhand, from the spread of the Jajhotiya Brahmans. The capital was
  Khajuraho rather than Mahoba: its ruins, about 4,500 × 2,500 ft (14,000 ft round), correspond "almost exactly"
  with the 15–16 li; of this period he found only the Ganthai temple, mound 28 ("most probably a ruined monastery")
  and a pedestal inscribed *ye dharmā hetu*, with the northern mounds as possible monasteries.
- ASR IX (1873–74, p. 65): following Hall, he re-read the name as Chi-ke-da = Chedi on the upper Narmadā, with
  Maheśvarapura at Seronj (11.14).
- ASR XXI (1883–84, pp. 58, 72): back to Bundelkhand. A Mahoba inscription of Kīrtivarman names the country
  Jejākabhukti, and a Madanpur inscription Jejākasukti; Abu Rihan's Jajahuti had Khajuraho as capital. He reconciles
  the 1,000 li from Ujjain (too short for Khajuraho) by reading it as 84 kos, about 250 miles.

**Gazetteers.** Hamirpur 1909 and Jhansi 1909 (after Indian Antiquary 1908) keep Bundelkhand, echoing the survey's
caste-name argument (G#555), but reject Khajuraho as SE of Gwalior and too far from Ujjain: "the spot that suits the
bearings ... best is Eran or Erakana on the Bina river" (G#554/713). Jalaun 1909 repeats Eran; Banda 1909, Saugor
1905 and the 1874 Bundelkhand volume repeat Khajuraho.

**Assessment.** The inscriptions and the caste names fix the country in Bundelkhand, against Chitor and Chedi. The
capital stays open: Khajuraho has the circuit and a Buddhist pedestal but needs the kos reading; Eran fits the
distances.

### 11.14 摩醯濕伐羅補羅國 (Maheśvarapura) — Gwalior; the survey's Mandla and Seronj both lapse

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Gwalior region (majority) (disputed). Smith (in Watters) and Majumdar (p. 725):
Gwalior. Cunningham (1871, pp. 559–60): Mandla on the upper Narmadā, reading S for N, which Watters (p. 252) rejects.
Vivien (p. 408): Macheri, W of Agra.

**Survey.** Cunningham dropped Mandla in ASR IX (1873–74, p. 65). Having made 11.13 Chedi, he sought Maheśvarapura
in Central India 900 li NE of Ujjain, as the Life has it: "Seronj, to the north-east of Bhilsa, would suit these
conditions almost exactly" (Ujjain to Besnagar measured at 142 miles by perambulator). The Travels' 900 li north
from Chedi would land "in the neighbourhood of Narwar", which he rejected. He cites no remains at Seronj, and once he
returned 11.13 to Bundelkhand (ASR XXI) the Seronj arrangement lost its frame.

**Gazetteers.** Hamirpur 1909 takes Gwalior as Maheśvarapura in its reckoning for Eran (G#554). It argues only from
bearings.

**Assessment.** Seronj fits the Life's distance from Ujjain, but not the Travels' 900 li north of a Bundelkhand
Jejākabhukti. Gwalior agrees with the text's "north" without emendation and with the Eran reconstruction of 11.13.
Mizutani stands.

### 11.15 信度國 (Sindhu) — capital Alor

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Upper Sind, capital Alor (agreed). Cunningham (pp. 284–8) and Vivien (p. 409): Alor.
Majumdar (pp. 690–1) reports Branfill's Vijnot, a ruined town in Ubauro taluk, read as "Vijnavapura". Dey:
Minagar. Haig (in Watters p. 253): Xuanzang's Sindh is "not the Sindh of any period".

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Branfill's Vijnot gives a closer sound for Pi-chen-po-pu-lo, but these sources offer no evidence
beyond the name. Alor, the historical capital of Upper Sindh, stands.

### 11.16 茂羅三部盧國 (Mūlasthānapura) — Multan

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Multan (agreed). He notes that the direction is NE, not E. Cunningham (pp. 265, 268),
Vivien (p. 410), Beal (p. 274), Dey and Smith: Multan. Watters (p. 253) calls Mūlasthānapura "an impossible
restoration" because Multan lies far N of Sindh. Majumdar (p. 689) prefers a Mūlasthāna on the Narmadā in Broach
province.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR V, 1872–73, pp. 114–124) traces the name and the sun temple from Xuanzang through
Biladuri, Abu Zaid, Masudi and Istakhri, and measures the city. The fort is 1¼ miles round with 46 bastions; "the
walled circuit of Multan, including both city and citadel, is 15,000 feet, or very nearly three miles; and the whole
circuit ... including the unwalled suburbs, is from four and half to five miles", agreeing with the 30 li. He knew
the walls closely, having dismantled them in 1854; they are Mughal work on older lines.

**Gazetteers.** Mooltan 1884, Mooltan 1902, Punjab 1919 and Puri 1977 repeat Multan. The 1884 volume's golden Sun
image and 30-li circuit (G#1170/1173) echo the survey.

**Assessment.** The sun temple is diagnostic, and the circuit fits, though it was measured on later walls. The
objection from bearing is real but weaker. Mizutani stands.

### 11.17 鉢伐多國 (Parvata) — Harappa; Shorkot abandoned by its proposer

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Harappa (disputed). Cunningham (1871, pp. 233–4): Shorkot, reading So-lo-fa-to.
Cunningham later (ASR V, in Majumdar pp. 687–8) and Fleet: Harappa. Smith (in Watters p. 255): Jammu. Vivien
(p. 410): perhaps Fatehpur on the Ravi.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR V, 1872–73, p. 107) withdrew Shorkot after visiting it: "I have satisfied myself that
it never could have reached the size of 20 li", whereas "the size agrees almost exactly with that of Harapa"
(about 3 miles round, after Burnes), "and as the position otherwise corresponds", Harappa "must be the very city
visited by the pilgrim". His digging, on ground already stripped by railway contractors, produced stone scrapers,
pottery, and Major Clark's black stone seal with a humpless bull and six unknown characters.

**Gazetteers.** Jhang 1884 repeats the 1871 Shorkot, describing "a huge mound of ruins about 100 feet in height ...
surrounded with a wall of large-sized bricks" (G#1237/1337); it ignores ASR V. Punjab 1919 puts Po-fa-to NE of
Multan.

**Assessment.** Shorkot fails on the measured circuit and needs an emended name; Harappa rests on size and position.
The finds do not date the town to Xuanzang's time (later work, not in these sources, assigns the seal to the Indus
civilization). Mizutani stands, with Jammu too far from Multan.

### 11.18 阿點婆翅羅國 (Atyanabakela) — lower Sindh/Cutch; capital Karachi or Kotesar

**Identifications.** Mizutani: lower Sind, capital Karachi (agreed). Vivien (p. 411) and Lassen: Karachi. Cunningham
(pp. 346–8) and Dey: Cutch, capital Koṭeśvara on the Kori mouth. Majumdar (p. 696): Lower Sindh, not Cutch, since the
capital lay on the Indus.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** These sources split between Karachi and Koṭeśvara. Majumdar's reading of the text, with the capital
on the river near the sea, favours the Sindh side. Mizutani stands, with low to medium confidence.

### 11.20 波剌斯國 (Pārsa) — Persia; capital Persepolis/Istakhr

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Sasanian Persia, capital Persepolis (Istakhr) (agreed). Vivien (p. 289) and Beal
(p. 277): Persia. Beal does not locate the capital. Dey: the capital Su-la-sa-t'ang-na is a transcription of
"Satarochana", now Shahrud.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Dey's Shahrud is an isolated etymology. Mizutani's Persepolis/Istakhr stands.

### 11.20.01a 鶴秣城 — Hormuz

**Identifications.** Mizutani: old Hormuz near Mināb (uncertain). Beal (p. 278): "Ormus?".

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The only proposal, Hormuz, fits the "eastern border" and the name. Mizutani stands.

### 11.20.02b 西女國 — legendary island of women; Minicoy suggested

**Identifications.** Mizutani: a legend, and he judges identification futile (unknown). Watters (p. 257): the
Strīrājya of the Bṛhatsaṃhitā. Beal (p. 279): refers to Marco Polo and Yule.

**Gazetteers.** Malabar 1887 (G#448), quoting Julien's text: "Reference is probably here made to the Island of
Minicoy". It refers to Marco Polo's male and female islands.

**Assessment.** Minicoy lies SW of India, not SW of Persia, and the 拂懍 overlordship in the text points west. The
suggestion is a new candidate in these sources but carries little weight. Mizutani's scepticism stands.

### 11.21 臂多勢羅國 (Pitaśilā) — near Hyderabad (Sind), uncertain

**Identifications.** Mizutani: near Hyderabad (agreed). Cunningham (pp. 318, 323): Haidarabad (Nirankot). Haig (in
Watters p. 257): Nagar Parkar. Majumdar (p. 692): NW of Lower Sindh and SW of Middle Sindh, rejecting the delta.
Vivien (p. 412): no comparison possible.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The three placements differ materially, and none cites remains. Mizutani follows Cunningham, and
confidence is low.

### 11.22 阿軬荼國 (Avaṇḍa) — Middle Sindh (Brahmanabad/Khairpur)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Brāhmanābād or Khairpur (uncertain). Cunningham (pp. 300–1, 310): Middle Sindh,
capital Brahmanabad. Majumdar (p. 691): Middle Sindh. Beal (p. 280): "corresponded with Avanti". Watters (p. 260):
not the Avanti of the canon.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The authorities agree on Middle Sindh. Beal's Avanti concerns the name only. Mizutani stands, with
medium confidence for the region and low for the capital.

### 11.23 伐剌拏國 (Varaṇa) — Bannu

**Identifications.** Mizutani: probably Bannu (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 97), Majumdar (p. 679, with Stein's
endorsement) and Dey: Bannu. Vivien (p. 414) and Smith (in Watters p. 262): Vaneh or the Gomal valley. Watters:
Cunningham's view is open to "serious exceptions".

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Pāṇini's Varṇu and Stein's endorsement favour Bannu. Mizutani stands, with medium confidence.

### 11.23.02 稽疆那國 (Kikāna) — Waziristan or Pishin

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Waziristan, W of Bannu (agreed, after Stein). Majumdar (p. 679): Stein's Waziristan.
Cunningham (p. 99): the Pishin valley near Quetta, the Kīkān of the Arab historians. Vivien (p. 414): Kīkān, near
Shal (Quetta). Watters (p. 262): rejects Cunningham.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The Kīkān of the Arab sources lay toward Quetta, while Stein's Waziristan follows from Bannu (11.23)
and the route. Mizutani stands, with medium confidence.

## Gazetteer passages coded `new` in this range

- **The herdsman's cave near Campā.** Bhagalpur 1911 (G#17/18, second scan G#165) places at the caves of Barari near
  Patharghatta a legend that "Hiuen Tsiang tells" and calls "The remnants of the petrified man were shown to Hiuen
  Tsiang". The caves have yielded punch-marked coins, and there are rock carvings of 84 sages. The legend (the
  cowherd, the radiant bull, the paradise wood, the swallowed fruit) is in the Life, Book IV, under Campā (Beal 1911,
  pp. 129ff). It is not in the Xiyu ji, which is why the place is missing from the master table. The gazetteer's
  location rests only on the caves' antiquity and their nearness to the Colgong–Patharghata rock (10.02.02a).
- **The great river between Puṇḍravardhana and Kāmarūpa.** Bengal 1902 (G#1920) and Bogra 1910 (G#123, scan 316;
  coded under 10.05) identify it as the old Karatoyā at Mahasthan. See 10.05.
- **The herdsman's cave near Campā (survey).** Cunningham (ASR XV, 1879–80, pp. 32–33) puts the Life's long gallery
  at the Mayaganj cave at Bhagalpur, where punch-marked silver coins were found, because "there is no other now known
  at Bhagalpur", though he admits there are no rocks at its mouth. This differs from Bhagalpur 1911's Barari
  caves near Patharghata (above).
