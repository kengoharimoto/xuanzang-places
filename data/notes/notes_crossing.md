# Notes: the desert crossing, Guazhou to Yiwu (the Life only)

Ten places added on 2026-11-23 from the Life, juan 1 (T50:222c–224c): Xuanzang's crossing of 629/630 from Guazhou to
Yiwu (Hami), which the Xiyu ji does not tell (it starts at Gaochang). Mizutani and Ji do not annotate the Life; Ji's
preface note on 玉門 is the only standard identification. Sources: Stein, "The Desert Crossing of Hsüan-tsang, 630
A.D." (*Geographical Journal* 54, 1919, pp. 265–277; Google Vision OCR, `data/ref/stein/gj1919_stein_vision.txt`) and
*Serindia* iii (1921), pp. 1097–1099, 1141–1149 (`data/coded/ar_batch13.tsv`, S021–S031); the recent literature
(`data/coded/lit_batch20.tsv`); P.2005 in Sun's edition (2017; `lit_batch21.tsv`), checked on the manuscript scan
(`route/p2005_editions.tsv`); Li Zhengyu's "玄奘瓜州、伊吾經行考" (2006, read in the revised web text of 2011 with his
fieldwork to 2009; `lit_batch22.tsv`); the route tests in `route/README.md` §11 (`route/crossing.py`,
`route/p2005_posts.py`). Li Zhengyu's other papers, Li Bingcheng 1990–2008, Zheng Binglin and Cao Hong 2010 and Wang
Nai'ang 2020 are on CNKI only and are known here through reviews and footnotes; they are marked "not read".

**Two reconstructions.** Stein (1919) put Guazhou at Kua-chou-ch'eng S of An-hsi and the five beacons on the first
five stages of the An-hsi–Hami road (Pei-tan-tzu … Xingxingxia), at 5 li to the mile. Li Zhengyu, from P.2005 and his
own survey of the post sites, puts Guazhou at Suoyang cheng, the pass at the small city of Maquan on the Huangshui
gou, and the five beacons at the first five posts of P.2005: Xinjing N of Liugong, Baidunzi, Hongliuyuan, Daquan and
Malianjing, one stage south of Stein's; the Wild Horse Spring at Lucaojingzi and the pool at Dashui N of Dunhuang. P.2005's
own distances (`route/p2005_posts.py`, scheme B) agree with Li's placing of the posts, and the posts are ruins with
Tang finds; that the beacons of 629 stood where the posts were set in 678–696 is Li's inference, supported by the
Life's water at the beacons and by P.2005's posts moved to beacons and named after them.

**The li on this road.** The Life's figures are of two kinds: what Xuanzang was told (50 li to the river, five
beacons 100 li apart, the 800-li Moheyan) and what he travelled (80 li to the first beacon, 100 li looking for the
spring). On Stein's surveyed road the beacon figures come out at 0.32 km per li, Xuanzang's usual road li: 480 li from
the river to the fifth beacon = 96 cyclometer miles to Xingxingxia. The Tang official figures run larger: 900 li from
Yizhou to Guazhou (*Yuanhe zhi* 40: 東南取莫賀磧路至瓜州九百里), 0.40 km per li; 800 li from Yiwu to the Yumen pass
(*Xin Tangshu* 40: 南去玉門關八百里), 0.43 from Shuangta-bao; and the posts of the same road in the Dunhuang document
P.2005 (c. 700; Sun 2017, pp. 140–143), about 0.45 between posts. The Life's "800 li" of the Moheyan is the official
pass-to-Yiwu figure, not the desert alone. The posts' span from the first to the fifth, 283.5 official li, stands to the
Life's 400 li between the first and fifth beacons as 0.71: if the beacons were where the posts were later set (Li
Zhengyu), Xuanzang's li is about 0.71 of the official one. Li himself reckons in official li throughout (900 li in
nine travelling days). Whether the 50 li from Guazhou is in official li matters for
Guazhou (below).

### 01.04.x1 瓜州 (Guazhou) — Suoyang cheng, by the texts and the consensus; the Life's 50 li favour a site further west

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, p. 271; *Serindia* iii p. 1098): Kua-chou-ch'êng, the ruined walled town S of
An-hsi, "in view of its central position and surviving local tradition". Tosaki (2000, pp. 74, 76): Suoyang cheng
鎖陽城 (Kuyu cheng 苦峪城), the seat of Jinchang county and of Guazhou in 629; the seat moved later. Li Zhengyu
(2010, not read; via Wang Lei 2020): Suoyang cheng until 727, then the large city at Maquan 馬圈村; in 2006/2011 (read)
he starts Xuanzang from Suoyang cheng, 115 li from Changle city. The UNESCO Silk
Roads dossier (WHC 1442, 2014, printed p. 297): Suoyang City, 62 km SE of the county seat, "presumably" Tang
Guazhou, citing Yan Wenru 1953, Li Bingcheng 1990, Wang Beichen 2006 and Zhao Jianlong's 2012 survey (none read).
**Evidence.** The Life: he stayed over a month; the prefect Dugu Da (獨孤達) and the clerk Li Chang (李昌). Suoyang
cheng is a walled Tang city with an inner and outer town; the Ta'er temple 1 km E was excavated in 2019–23
(Kaiyuan tongbao coins; Wang Jianjun 2026, pp. 92–107), but nothing found names the city. Hui, Cao and Zheng (2010,
not read) put Xuanzang's lodging in the Ta'er temple. Stein's Kua-chou-ch'êng held a Qing ya-men and he cites no early
finds; his case is tradition and position.
**Route test.** From Stein's site the road reaches an old bed of the Su-lo after 10 miles: 50 li at 0.32 km. From
Suoyang cheng to Shuangta-bao on the Shule is about 30 km (Seng 2017, p. 1172) or 36 km in a straight line: 0.6–0.7 km
per li, more than the official li of this road (c. 0.45 between the posts of P.2005) or the nominal Tang li (c. 0.53)
allows; at the li of the beacons (0.32) Suoyang cheng is further still from the river.
**Assessment.** The texts and the dated walled city make Suoyang cheng the Tang seat, and the recent literature
agrees; Stein's site rests on tradition alone. The 50 li, a figure Xuanzang was told at Guazhou, is short for Suoyang cheng even in
official li; a nearer Tang channel of the Shule would resolve it. Suoyang cheng, medium; the conflict with the li is open.

### 01.04.x2 瓠蘆河 (Hulu river) — the Shule (Stein) or the Huangshui gou by Maquan (Li Zhengyu); open

**Identifications.** Vivien de Saint-Martin, Stein (1919, p. 271): the Su-lo Ho (Shule 疏勒河, Mongol Bulungir).
Tosaki (2000, p. 74): the Kulong river 窟窿河, a branch at Shuangta. Li Zhengyu (2006/2011, n. [7]; earlier 1997 via Li
Weili 2020): the Huangshui gou 黃水溝 (lower course Lucao gou 芦草沟) by the small city at Maquan, the 苦水 of P.2005;
Xuanzang crossed it about 10 li upstream, E of Maquan and 2 li W of Qiaozi village, rested in the lee of the Shigong
shan and went N through the Qiaozi gap, then crossed the Shule S of the first beacon.
**Evidence.** The Life: 50+ li N of Guazhou, "wide below and narrow above", too deep to ford; crossed at night ten li
upstream of the pass where the banks were a little over a zhang apart. Stein notes an old bed 2 miles N of the
present channel N of An-hsi. The Shule's channels have moved since Tang times; no source fixes the 7th-century one.
Li's stream lies nearer Suoyang cheng than the Shule at Shuangta, which would ease the 50 li (01.04.x1).
**Assessment.** The river depends on Guazhou and the pass: the Shule on Stein's Guazhou, a stream by Maquan on Li's.
Open, low.

### 01.04.x3 玉門關 (the Tang Yumen pass) — open among Shuangta-bao, the Maquan small city and Liugong

**Identifications.** Ji (1985, preface n. 21, pdf 179): moved east in the Six Dynasties to Jinchang 晉昌, "near
Shuangta-bao 雙塔堡, Anxi", the Tang pass. Li Bingcheng (2001, 2005, 2008, not read; via Xinhua 2002, Li Weili
2020, Seng 2017): near Shuangta-bao on the S bank of the Shule, c. 30 km N of Suoyang cheng, on hills with beacons
and a beacon line to the NW; he admits no ruin of the pass is known. Tosaki (2000, p. 74) takes Shuangta-bao for the
pass of 629. Li Zhengyu (1997, 2010, not read; restated in 2006/2011, read): the small city NW of Maquan, 15 km NW of Qiaozi, where he found two
cities "20 bu" apart as in the *Yuanhe zhi* (玉門關在縣東二十步, Siku text); on Wang Lei's (2020) reading this is the
pass after 727, so even he leaves 629 open. Wang Nai'ang (2020, not read; via Li Weili 2020): the Changle city =
Liugong city 六工城, from remote sensing and dating. Stein (1919, p. 271; *Serindia* iii p. 1099): not located; the
Sui annals put it at Jinchang in 610, and it had moved since.
**Evidence.** The Life: on the river 50+ li N of Guazhou, at its widest point, seen from afar at the crossing. Wang Lei
(2020) dates the move east to the Northern Zhou with the Yiwu road and notes a seal "玉門關之印" on a pass of 733
(Astana tomb 509). Shuangta-bao is now under the Shuangta reservoir.
**Assessment.** Three proposals, no site evidence for any. Ji's Shuangta-bao is the standard and fits 50 li N of
Suoyang cheng in official li; Liugong (40.40 N, 95.60 E) lies W of Suoyang cheng and S of the river, not N of
Guazhou. Open, low; the point is Shuangta-bao.

### 01.04.x4 第一烽 (the first beacon) — the Xinjing post N of Liugong (Li Zhengyu) or Stein's Pei-tan-tzu; open

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 271, 275): Pei-tan-tzu (Baidunzi 白墩子), the first stage from An-hsi on the
Hami road, with a spring. Tosaki (2000, p. 75): the Xinjing post 新井驛 of P.2005 (沙州都督府圖經), 27 li 200 bu N of
Changle; he reads the *Xingzhuang* (關西百餘里有一烽) as putting it c. 100 li from the pass. Li Zhengyu (2006/2011,
n. [1]): the Xinjing post, 31 huali (27 li 200 bu) N of Liugong poch'eng (Tang Changle city) and 4.5 huali N of the
Shule, revising his earlier Shajingzi (2009 book, p. 85); Baidunzi is his second beacon (Guangxian post).
**Evidence.** The Life: 80+ li beyond the crossing; water W of the beacon; the commandant Wang Xiang (王祥) of
Dunhuang. Stein's 16 map miles from the old Su-lo bed match the 80 li at 0.32 km per li. Li's site has fort walls,
an Eastern Han beacon 8 m high, rubbish and sherds; from his crossing E of Maquan it is 40 km in a straight line, "80
li" by the winding way. Baidunzi too has a beacon (Western Han core, Eastern Han and Qing casings), a fort and springs.
**P.2005.** The posts of the Fifth Road (Sun 2017, pp. 140–143, checked on the scan): Xinjing 227 li 200 bu from
Shazhou, then Guangxian and Wushan (run by Guazhou), Shuangquan, the Fifth post, Lengquan, Hutong and Chiya on the
Yizhou border, set up in 678–696. Between posts they fall on the road's water stages at one scale (0.45–0.49 km per li,
`route/p2005_posts.py`); P.2005's own distance from Shazhou (227.7 li = 104 km at that scale) and Li's Chiya
coordinates put the series where Li found the posts (scheme B), one stage south of Stein's.
**Assessment.** The post of Xinjing is located, by document, distance and ruin; that it was the first beacon of 629
rests on Li's equation of beacons and posts. Stein's Baidunzi rests on 5 li to the mile. Li's site is the stronger
candidate, with Stein's the rival; open, medium. The point is Li's grid reading for the beacon (X16720.2, Y4494.7: Gauss–Krüger
zone 16, 40.557 N, 95.600 E).

### 01.04.x5 第四烽 (the fourth beacon) — Daquan, the Shuangquan post (Li Zhengyu), or Stein's Malianjing; open

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 275–276): Ma-lien-ching-tzu (Malianjing 馬蓮井), the fourth stage, with wells.
Li Zhengyu (2006/2011, n. [9] and §(1)): Daquan 大泉, the Shuangquan post 雙泉驛 of P.2005, 35 km NW of Hongliuyuan;
Malianjing is his fifth beacon (the Fifth post).
**Evidence.** The Life: reached the night after the first beacon, under Wang Bolong (王伯隴), Wang Xiang's kinsman;
told to avoid the fifth and take water at the Wild Horse Spring 100 li off. The Life's one day from the first to the
fourth beacon contradicts its own 100 li between beacons; Stein (p. 275) supposes two marches lost from the text, Li
two days (220 li). At 0.32 km per li the five beacons fall on the five water stages An-hsi–Xingxingxia (Stein). At
Daquan Li found post and fort ruins with hemp sandals, bronze arrowheads and Kaiyuan tongbao, a beacon base (levelled
in 2007) and two springs 30 m apart, whence "Shuangquan".
**P.2005.** Shuangquan, the fourth post, was set up in 678 "in the Moheyan desert of the Fifth Road" (就第五道莫賀延磧;
Sun 2017, p. 142). The manuscript gives its distance from Shazhou as 477 li 160 bu (Sun: 260 bu), but the neighbouring
entries give 446–450 li (511 li 40 bu less 64 or 60 li 80 bu): the document's own figure is some 30 li out; Li emends it
(447, or 420 by his own intervals). On the intervals it lies at Daquan.
**Assessment.** Daquan is a Tang post site with a beacon and the two springs that give the name, where P.2005's
distances put the fourth post; that the fourth beacon of 629 stood there is Li's inference. Stein's Malianjing is the
rival; open, medium. The point is 6 km NE of Daquan station, by Li's 12 li.

### 01.04.x6 野馬泉 (Wild Horse Spring) — not found by Xuanzang; Lucaojingzi (Li Zhengyu) or Stein's map guess; open

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 275–276): since Xuanzang turned east to regain the fourth beacon, the spring lay
W of the road; a water stage on Roborovsky's Dunhuang–Hami track c. 30 miles WNW of Ma-lien-ching-tzu fits. Li
Zhengyu (2006/2011, §(2)): Lucaojingzi 芦草井子 in the Zhaobi shan, 49 km W (a little N) of Daquan on the 1:50,000 map,
where water still seeps and reed beds stand head-high; visited with the Guazhou county reserve, heritage bureau and
museum. Zheng Binglin and Cao Hong (2010, not read) discuss it.
**Evidence.** The Life: 100 li from the fourth beacon by report; he went 100+ li, missed it, lost his water, went ten
li back east. The *Xingzhuang* has a sandstorm (遇風沙大起，不知泉處), which Li takes to explain the miss: the spring lies
hidden among the hills and Xuanzang passed S of it. "Yemaquan" is a common desert name (Stein).
**Assessment.** Li's spring fits the bearing and 100 li from his fourth beacon; Stein's is a map reading from his.
Lucaojingzi is not on OSM or in the gazetteers searched; the point is an estimate from Li's description, in the Zhaobi
shan N of the Zhaodong–Hongliuhe stretch (about ±10 km); Stein's estimate lies c. 41.73, 94.77. Open, low.

### 01.04.x7 莫賀延磧 (the Moheyan desert) — the Pei-shan desert N of Malianjing to the Hami oasis

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 273, 275): the ground beyond Hsing-hsing-hsia (the fifth beacon), where the
road drops some 2,000 feet past Sha-ch'üan-tzu and K'u-shui and makes the 35-mile waterless march to the Yen-tun
depression. Tosaki (2001, pp. 61–62): the Tang "Fifth Road" (第五道) ran N from the Xinjing post via the Fifth post
to Rouyuan county in Yizhou. The *Yuanhe zhi* (40, Yizhou) calls the road 莫賀磧路. Li Zhengyu (2006/2011): the gobi
from Malianjing N to the Karlik range; the five beacons guarded the Tang side of the border at the Xingxingxia hills
before Yiwu submitted in 630, and the road took its name from the fifth (第五烽, the Fifth post at Malianjing); he
derives Moheyan from the Xiongnu Huyan (呼衍), with Juyan and Guyan.
**Evidence.** The Life: 800+ li, "of old called the River of Sand" (沙河), no birds, beasts, water or grass. On Stein's
road Xingxingxia–Hami is c. 120 miles (193 km): 0.24 km per li, short for 800 li. But 800 li is the official distance
from Yiwu to the Yumen pass (*Xin Tangshu* 40: 伊吾…在大磧外，南去玉門關八百里), 0.43 km per li from Shuangta-bao: the
officer at the fourth beacon gave the whole road's figure. P.2005 has the post of Shuangquan, the fourth on the road,
"in the Moheyan desert of the Fifth Road" (Sun 2017, p. 142): for the Tang administration the Moheyan began well before
Xingxingxia.
**Assessment.** The desert of the Hami road is agreed in broad terms; its extent is not a site, and the 800 li measure
the whole road from the pass. Medium.

### 01.04.x8 the grass and pool — Dashui N of Dunhuang (Li Zhengyu) or near Changliushui (Stein); open

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 276–277): the first spring and verdure on the Hami side, near Chang-liu-shui
(Changliushui), five marches (106 miles) from Ma-lien-ching-tzu by the road; two marches (some 35 miles) to Hami, as
the Life's two days. Li Zhengyu (2006/2011, §(5)): Dashui 大水 in the north of Dunhuang, 336 Tang li N of Dunhuang on
the Shuogan road (a post from 692, a fort under Kaiyuan, a Qing outpost), with a Han beacon 7 m high and wetlands that
dried up after the 1960s; about 330 li, three days, from Yiwu.
**Evidence.** The Life: after four nights and five days without water, about ten li and then a few li more; several
mu of grass and a pool; a day's rest, two days to Yiwu. The *Xingzhuang* counts three days to Yiwu from the pool, which
is Li's ground against Stein: Kushui has no water and Changliushui is less than two days from Hami.
**Assessment.** The days fit both; Li's site has the water and the vegetation, Stein's the road. Open, low. Li gives no coordinates;
the point is an OSM locality named 大水泉？ (its mapper unsure of the name), which matches his 336 li N of Dunhuang (148 km,
0.44 km per li) and lies W of Hongliuhe as his route requires. Stein's Changliushui lies c. 42.40, 93.91.

### 01.04.x9 伊吾 (Yiwu) — the Hami oasis

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, p. 277; *Serindia* iii p. 1097 n. 11): I-wu, the Tang name of Hami (Kumul). Tosaki
(2001) on the Tang road to Yizhou. Yu Jianjun (2015, news report, not coded) suggests a small temple at Baiyanggou,
c. 60 km W of Hami, for his lodging.
**Evidence.** The Life: a monastery with three Chinese monks; a foreign king; Gaochang's envoys; over ten days' stay.
Yiwu became Tang Yizhou in 630. Li Zhengyu (2006/2011) dates the crossing from the 1st to the 16th of the 8th month
of Zhenguan 3 (24 August – 8 September 629), against Stein's "630". The *Xin Tangshu* (40) puts Yiwu county "beyond the great desert, 800 li south to the
Yumen pass" and P.2005 the Yizhou border (Rouyuan county) at the post of Chiya, 743 li from Shazhou on the corrected
chain. The Tang seat is put at Hami town; no excavation in the sources read names it.
**Assessment.** Hami, agreed by all; the seat within the oasis is not fixed. Medium.

### 01.04.x10 白力城 (Baili) — unidentified; perhaps the Pichan oasis (study's conjecture)

**Identifications.** None in the sources read (Beal 1911: "the town of Pih-li").
**Evidence.** The Life: six days across the southern desert from Yiwu, on Gaochang's border; from there, changing
horses, the royal city by cockcrow the same night. Pichan (辟展, Shanshan), the easternmost Turfan oasis, lies 264 km
from Hami in a straight line (44 km a day for six days) and 61 km from Gaochang, a night's ride with relays.
**Assessment.** The position fits the eastern Turfan oases; no source read identifies the city. Low; the point is a
conjecture.
