# Notes: the desert crossing, Guazhou to Yiwu (the Life only)

Ten places added on 2026-11-23 from the Life, juan 1 (T50:222c–224c): Xuanzang's crossing of 629/630 from Guazhou to
Yiwu (Hami), which the Xiyu ji does not tell (it starts at Gaochang). Mizutani and Ji do not annotate the Life; Ji's
preface note on 玉門 is the only standard identification. Sources: Stein, "The Desert Crossing of Hsüan-tsang, 630
A.D." (*Geographical Journal* 54, 1919, pp. 265–277; Google Vision OCR, `data/ref/stein/gj1919_stein_vision.txt`) and
*Serindia* iii (1921), pp. 1097–1099, 1141–1149 (`data/coded/ar_batch13.tsv`, S021–S031); the recent literature
(`data/coded/lit_batch20.tsv`); the route test in `route/README.md` §11 (`route/crossing.py`). The core Chinese studies
(Li Zhengyu 1997–2010, Li Bingcheng 1990–2008, Zheng Binglin and Cao Hong 2010, Wang Nai'ang 2020) are on CNKI only
and are known here through reviews and footnotes; they are marked "not read".

**The li on this road.** The Life's figures are of two kinds: what Xuanzang was told at Guazhou (50 li to the river,
five beacons 100 li apart, the 800-li Moheyan) and what he travelled (80 li to the first beacon, 100 li looking for
the spring). On Stein's surveyed road the beacon figures come out at 0.32 km per li, Xuanzang's usual road li: 480 li
from the river to the fifth beacon = 96 cyclometer miles to Xingxingxia. The Tang official figure for the whole road,
900 li (*Yuanhe zhi* 40, Yizhou: 東南取莫賀磧路至瓜州九百里), comes out at 0.40, and the Moheyan's 800 li at only
0.17–0.24. Whether the reported figures are in Xuanzang's li or in local official li matters for Guazhou (below).

### 01.04.x1 瓜州 (Guazhou) — Suoyang cheng, by the texts and the consensus; the Life's 50 li favour a site further west

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, p. 271; *Serindia* iii p. 1098): Kua-chou-ch'êng, the ruined walled town S of
An-hsi, "in view of its central position and surviving local tradition". Tosaki (2000, pp. 74, 76): Suoyang cheng
鎖陽城 (Kuyu cheng 苦峪城), the seat of Jinchang county and of Guazhou in 629; the seat moved later. Li Zhengyu
(2010, not read; via Wang Lei 2020): Suoyang cheng until 727, then the large city at Maquan 馬圈村. The UNESCO Silk
Roads dossier (WHC 1442, 2014, printed p. 297): Suoyang City, 62 km SE of the county seat, "presumably" Tang
Guazhou, citing Yan Wenru 1953, Li Bingcheng 1990, Wang Beichen 2006 and Zhao Jianlong's 2012 survey (none read).
**Evidence.** The Life: he stayed over a month; the prefect Dugu Da (獨孤達) and the clerk Li Chang (李昌). Suoyang
cheng is a walled Tang city with an inner and outer town; the Ta'er temple 1 km E was excavated in 2019–23
(Kaiyuan tongbao coins; Wang Jianjun 2026, pp. 92–107), but nothing found names the city. Hui, Cao and Zheng (2010,
not read) put Xuanzang's lodging in the Ta'er temple. Stein's Kua-chou-ch'êng held a Qing ya-men and he cites no early
finds; his case is tradition and position.
**Route test.** From Stein's site the road reaches an old bed of the Su-lo after 10 miles: 50 li at 0.32 km. From
Suoyang cheng to Shuangta-bao on the Shule is about 30 km (Seng 2017, p. 1172) or 36 km in a straight line: 0.6–0.7 km
per li, which needs the Tang official li (c. 0.53–0.56 km) or more; at the li of the beacons (0.32) Suoyang cheng
is too far from the river.
**Assessment.** The texts and the dated walled city make Suoyang cheng the Tang seat, and the recent literature
agrees; Stein's site rests on tradition alone. The 50 li, a figure Xuanzang was told at Guazhou, fits Suoyang cheng
only if it is in official li, unlike the beacon figures. Suoyang cheng, medium; the conflict with the li is open.

### 01.04.x2 瓠蘆河 (Hulu river) — the Shule; which channel is open

**Identifications.** Vivien de Saint-Martin, Stein (1919, p. 271): the Su-lo Ho (Shule 疏勒河, Mongol Bulungir).
Tosaki (2000, p. 74): the Kulong river 窟窿河, a branch at Shuangta. Li Zhengyu (1997, not read; via Li Weili 2020):
an old channel E of the small city at Maquan, the 苦水 of P.2005.
**Evidence.** The Life: 50+ li N of Guazhou, "wide below and narrow above", too deep to ford; crossed at night ten li
upstream of the pass where the banks were a little over a zhang apart. Stein notes an old bed 2 miles N of the
present channel N of An-hsi. The Shule's channels have moved since Tang times; no source fixes the 7th-century one.
**Assessment.** The Shule system, agreed; the channel and the crossing depend on Guazhou and the pass. Medium.

### 01.04.x3 玉門關 (the Tang Yumen pass) — open among Shuangta-bao, the Maquan small city and Liugong

**Identifications.** Ji (1985, preface n. 21, pdf 179): moved east in the Six Dynasties to Jinchang 晉昌, "near
Shuangta-bao 雙塔堡, Anxi", the Tang pass. Li Bingcheng (2001, 2005, 2008, not read; via Xinhua 2002, Li Weili
2020, Seng 2017): near Shuangta-bao on the S bank of the Shule, c. 30 km N of Suoyang cheng, on hills with beacons
and a beacon line to the NW; he admits no ruin of the pass is known. Tosaki (2000, p. 74) takes Shuangta-bao for the
pass of 629. Li Zhengyu (1997, 2010, not read): the small city W of Maquan, 15 km NW of Qiaozi, where he found two
cities "20 bu" apart as in the *Yuanhe zhi* (玉門關在縣東二十步, Siku text); on Wang Lei's (2020) reading this is the
pass after 727, so even he leaves 629 open. Wang Nai'ang (2020, not read; via Li Weili 2020): the Changle city =
Liugong city 六工城, from remote sensing and dating. Stein (1919, p. 271; *Serindia* iii p. 1099): not located; the
Sui annals put it at Jinchang in 610, and it had moved since.
**Evidence.** The Life: on the river 50+ li N of Guazhou, at its widest point, seen from afar at the crossing. Wang Lei
(2020) dates the move east to the Northern Zhou with the Yiwu road and notes a seal "玉門關之印" on a pass of 733
(Astana tomb 509). Shuangta-bao is now under the Shuangta reservoir.
**Assessment.** Three proposals, no site evidence for any. Ji's Shuangta-bao is the standard and fits 50 li N of
Suoyang cheng in official li; Liugong (40.40 N, 95.60 E) lies W of Suoyang cheng and S of the river, not N of
Guazhou. Open, low; the point is Shuangta-bao.

### 01.04.x4 第一烽 (the first beacon) — Stein's Pei-tan-tzu or Tosaki's Xinjing post; open

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 271, 275): Pei-tan-tzu (Baidunzi 白墩子), the first stage from An-hsi on the
Hami road, with a spring. Tosaki (2000, p. 75): the Xinjing post 新井驛 of P.2005 (沙州都督府圖經), 27 li 200 bu N of
Changle; he reads the *Xingzhuang* (關西百餘里有一烽) as putting it c. 100 li from the pass. Li Zhengyu (2006, not
read; via Wang Lei 2020): the five beacons are the five posts of P.2005 (新井, 廣顯, 烏山, 雙泉, 第五).
**Evidence.** The Life: 80+ li beyond the crossing; water W of the beacon; the commandant Wang Xiang (王祥) of
Dunhuang. Stein's 16 map miles from the old Su-lo bed match the 80 li at 0.32 km per li. The P.2005 posts were set up
under Gaozong and Wu Zetian (Wang Lei), after 629, so they may not be the beacons of 629.
**Assessment.** Stein's equation rests on the li and on the water, not on a ruin; Tosaki's on a later post list. No
beacon ruin on either line has been reported as the first. Open, low.

### 01.04.x5 第四烽 (the fourth beacon) — Stein's Ma-lien-ching-tzu or the Shuangquan post; open

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 275–276): Ma-lien-ching-tzu (Malianjing 馬蓮井), the fourth stage, with wells.
Li Zhengyu (2006, not read): the Shuangquan post 雙泉驛 of P.2005.
**Evidence.** The Life: reached the night after the first beacon, under Wang Bolong (王伯隴), Wang Xiang's kinsman;
told to avoid the fifth and take water at the Wild Horse Spring 100 li off. The Life's one day from the first to the
fourth beacon contradicts its own 100 li between beacons; Stein (p. 275) supposes two marches lost from the text.
At 0.32 km per li the five beacons fall on the five water stages An-hsi–Xingxingxia (480 li, 96 cyclometer miles).
**Assessment.** Stein's scheme fits the road and its water, at the price of a lacuna in the Life. Open, low.

### 01.04.x6 野馬泉 (Wild Horse Spring) — not found by Xuanzang; Stein's guess c. 30 miles WNW of Malianjing

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 275–276): since Xuanzang turned east to regain the fourth beacon, the spring lay
W of the road; a water stage on Roborovsky's Dunhuang–Hami track c. 30 miles WNW of Ma-lien-ching-tzu fits. Zheng
Binglin and Cao Hong (2010, not read) discuss it.
**Evidence.** The Life: 100 li from the fourth beacon by report; he went 100+ li, missed it, lost his water, went ten
li back east. "Yemaquan" is a common desert name (Stein). The point is Stein's map reading.
**Assessment.** Unlocated. Low; the point is indicative only.

### 01.04.x7 莫賀延磧 (the Moheyan desert) — the Pei-shan desert between Xingxingxia and the Hami oasis

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 273, 275): the ground beyond Hsing-hsing-hsia (the fifth beacon), where the
road drops some 2,000 feet past Sha-ch'üan-tzu and K'u-shui and makes the 35-mile waterless march to the Yen-tun
depression. Tosaki (2001, pp. 61–62): the Tang "Fifth Road" (第五道) ran N from the Xinjing post via the Fifth post
to Rouyuan county in Yizhou. The *Yuanhe zhi* (40, Yizhou) calls the road 莫賀磧路.
**Evidence.** The Life: 800+ li, "of old called the River of Sand" (沙河), no birds, beasts, water or grass. On Stein's
road Xingxingxia–Hami is c. 120 miles (193 km), Xingxingxia–Changliushui c. 85 (137 km): 0.17–0.24 km per li, short
for 800 li; the figure was reported, not travelled, and may be a round traditional one.
**Assessment.** The desert of the Hami road is agreed in broad terms; its extent is not a site. Medium.

### 01.04.x8 the grass and pool — near Chang-liu-shui (Stein); approximate

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, pp. 276–277): the first spring and verdure on the Hami side, near Chang-liu-shui
(Changliushui), five marches (106 miles) from Ma-lien-ching-tzu by the road; two marches (some 35 miles) to Hami, as
the Life's two days.
**Evidence.** The Life: after four nights and five days without water, about ten li and then a few li more; several
mu of grass and a pool; a day's rest, two days to Yiwu. Stein supposes he went roughly parallel to the road W of it
and missed its wells.
**Assessment.** The days fit; the spot cannot be fixed. Low.

### 01.04.x9 伊吾 (Yiwu) — the Hami oasis

**Identifications.** Stein (1919, p. 277; *Serindia* iii p. 1097 n. 11): I-wu, the Tang name of Hami (Kumul). Tosaki
(2001) on the Tang road to Yizhou. Yu Jianjun (2015, news report, not coded) suggests a small temple at Baiyanggou,
c. 60 km W of Hami, for his lodging.
**Evidence.** The Life: a monastery with three Chinese monks; a foreign king; Gaochang's envoys; over ten days' stay.
Yiwu became Tang Yizhou in 630. The Tang seat is put at Hami town; no excavation in the sources read names it.
**Assessment.** Hami, agreed by all; the seat within the oasis is not fixed. Medium.

### 01.04.x10 白力城 (Baili) — unidentified; perhaps the Pichan oasis (study's conjecture)

**Identifications.** None in the sources read (Beal 1911: "the town of Pih-li").
**Evidence.** The Life: six days across the southern desert from Yiwu, on Gaochang's border; from there, changing
horses, the royal city by cockcrow the same night. Pichan (辟展, Shanshan), the easternmost Turfan oasis, lies 264 km
from Hami in a straight line (44 km a day for six days) and 61 km from Gaochang, a night's ride with relays.
**Assessment.** The position fits the eastern Turfan oases; no source read identifies the city. Low; the point is a
conjecture.
