# Notes on disputed places: juan 2 and juan 3

Range: Lampāka, Nagarahāra, Gandhāra (juan 2); Udyāna, Balūra, Taxila, Siṃhapura, Uraśā, Kashmir, Punach,
Rājapura (juan 3). 46 notes, in master-table order.

Gazetteer volumes used here (each counted once, though most exist in two scans): Peshawar 1883 and 1899;
Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895; Attock 1909; Jhelam 1907; Dehra Dun 1911; Punjab (Rose's Glossary) 1919; Himalayan
Districts II 1884; an unidentified volume of 1874; Bombay Gazetteer XIV (Thana, Places) 1882. Hazara, Shahpur and
Kashmir volumes give no hits in this range. Most of the Panjab passages copy Cunningham, from the *Ancient
Geography* or ASR II and V. The one body of independent argument is Stein's work at Ketas and Murti, which the
Jhelam gazetteer (1907) prints at length.

The Archaeological Survey reports bear on Gandhāra, Taxila and Siṃhapura: Cunningham's ASR II (report for
1863–64), V (1872–73) and XIV (1878–79), and Garrick's report in ASR XIX (1881–82) on Charsadda. Many of the
Peshawar and Rawalpindi gazetteer passages echo ASR II word for word. Cunningham's own view moved at four places:
Varuṣa (Palodheri → Shahbaz Garhi), Siṃhapura (Malot → Ketas in the 1871 book → Malot), the Kunāla stūpa (Karmal
→ the Meri hill) and the stūpa N of the body-offering (Ventura's tope too late → Ventura's tope). Udyāna, Uraśā and
Kashmir have no survey passages in this range.

### 02.02a 濫波國大都城 — capital unknown; Mizutani's only candidate lies outside Lampāka

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital unknown; "possibly Begram on the Logar river 10 km SE of Kabul" (after
Mizuno) (uncertain). Vivien (p. 301): Mandrâour (Mendrâwar), in the angle where the Alingar joins the Kabul river
(tentative). No one else names a site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Mizutani's candidate does not fit the region. Cunningham (p. 50) and Beal (p. 90) bound Lampāka as
the tract north of the Kabul river between the Alingar and the Kunar, and a Begram on the Logar near Kabul lies
well to the west of that. The master table already flags the quotation as odd. Vivien's Mandrawar is the only
candidate inside Lampāka, but it is a guess made without archaeological evidence. The capital stays unidentified,
and Mizutani's suggestion should be treated as doubtful (possibly a slip in his source).

### 02.03 那揭羅曷國 (nagarahāra) — the Jalalabad district; no real dispute

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Jalalabad and the southern Kabul-river basin around it (uncertain; his wording is
hedged, but he names no rival). Vivien (p. 302), Cunningham (p. 51), Beal (p. 91), Watters (p. 184) and Dey all put
it in the Jalalabad district/Nangnihar. Cunningham and Beal add Ptolemy's Nagara (Dionysopolis). Majumdar (p. 674)
gives no view of his own.

**Gazetteers.** Two volumes (Himalayan Districts II 1884; unidentified 1874) mention Nagarahāra only in passing, as
subject to Kapiśa. They add nothing.

**Assessment.** Every authority agrees. Mizutani's "uncertain" reflects his hedged phrasing rather than any
competing site.

### 02.03.01a 那揭羅曷國大都城 — Begram W of Jalalabad (Cunningham) vs Simpson's site at the Surkhab–Kabul junction

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Dasht-i-Begrām near Chahār-bāgh, SW of Jalalabad (agreed; no authority named).
Cunningham (p. 51–52): Begram, c. 2 miles W of Jalalabad. Vivien (p. 306): the ruins at Begram next to the village
of Nagrak, and explicitly not Balabagh. Against them, Simpson's site: Beal (p. 91) puts the town "in the angle
formed by the junction of the Surkhar and Kabul rivers", and Watters (p. 187, tentative) puts it at the Nagar
fortress on Simpson's rock (= Babur's Adinapur). Majumdar (p. 675) reports Simpson's Bala Hissar ruins c. 4 miles E
of Jalalabad without choosing.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The authorities split between Begram (Cunningham, Vivien, Mizutani) and Simpson's junction site
(Beal, Watters). The gazetteers do not address the question. Nothing here decides it, so Mizutani stands with only
moderate confidence.

### 02.03.02 燃燈佛受記窣堵波 — Ahin-Posh (Higuchi) or the "Nagara Goondee" tope (Masson, Simpson)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: possibly the Ahin-Pōsh stūpa E of the old city (after Higuchi) (uncertain). Watters
(p. 187, tentative): the "Nagara Goondee" tope c. 3 furlongs E/SE of the Nagar rock (after Masson and Simpson).

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Each candidate depends on where its proponent puts the capital (02.03.01a): Higuchi measures from
Begram, Watters from Simpson's rock. The question is still open, and the gazetteers do not change it.

### 02.03.03 瞿波羅龍窟（佛影窟） — the Shadow Cave: somewhere in the cliffs S/SW of Jalalabad

**Identifications.** Mizutani: among the Syāh-sang rocks S of Chahār-bāgh, along the cliffs S of Jalalabad; which
cave cannot be told (after Higuchi) (uncertain). Watters (p. 193, tentative): the E bank of a torrent in the hills
SW of Padmapur; the range running SW from the Ahin Posh tope towards Sultanpur is acceptable; the monastery is
perhaps Gunda Chismeh.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Both put the cave in the same range of hills south-west of the city, and neither can point to the
cave itself. The gazetteers add nothing.

### 02.04.02 卑鉢羅樹 — the pipal tree 8–9 li SE of Peshawar: Babur's "stupendous tree", now gone

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown), but his note places the Kaniṣka stūpa (Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri)
just south of the tree. Cunningham (p. 91–92, tentative): 8–9 li SE of Peshawar, probably the "stupendous tree" of
Begram that Babur saw in 1505, which has since disappeared.

**Survey.** The argument first appears in Cunningham's ASR II (report for 1863–64, pp. 88–89): "a great Pipal tree,
at 8 or 9 li, or 1½ mile, to the south-east of the city", Songyun's Bodhi tree, seen by Babur in 1505 and not
mentioned by Abul Fazl in 1594, so "it had previously disappeared through simple old age and decay". No fieldwork
is reported.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 echo ASR II almost word for word ("the holy pipal tree, at 8 or 9 *li*, or
1½ mile, to the south-east of the city"), with the Babur and Abul Fazl dating. They also note that no remains of
the stūpa beside it survive.

**Assessment.** The site of the tree follows from 02.04.03: it stood immediately north of Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri, c. 1½
miles SE of Peshawar, and nothing of it remains. Survey and gazetteers add the Babur and Abul Fazl dating but no
physical evidence.

### 02.04.03 迦膩色迦大窣堵波 — Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri; a stray footnote suggests Manikyala

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri, c. 1 km SE of Peshawar, excavated by Spooner in 1908–09 (agreed).
Vivien (p. 308): under a lieue SE of Peshawar, no site named. Cunningham (p. 92): close S of the holy tree; "No
remains of this great stupa now exist". Majumdar (p. 678): Shah-ji-ki-Dheri, where the relic casket was found.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR II, 1863–64, p. 89) puts the stūpa "close to the holy tree on its south side" and
reports only hearsay: "It is said that no remains of this great Stupa now exist". He did not look for it.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 echo ASR II: no remains. One footnote in the Thana volume of the Bombay
Gazetteer (1882), on the relic mound at Peshawar, says "This is perhaps the great Mânikyâla tope which was opened
by General Ventura in 1830". It cites Wilson's *Ariana Antiqua* and offers no argument.

**Assessment.** The excavated relic casket settles the identification. The survey's "no remains" was hearsay, which
Spooner's excavation overturned. The Thana footnote is a confusion with Manikyala, made far from the region, and
carries no weight.

### 02.04.06 迦膩色迦伽藍 — Kaniṣka's monastery: Gor Khattri, by name and tradition only

**Identifications.** Mizutani: possibly Gor Khattri at the NE corner of Peshawar (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 93)
and Watters (p. 208) both make the same tentative identification with the Gurh-Katri of Babur and Akbar.

**Survey.** The argument is Cunningham's in ASR II (1863–64, pp. 89–90): Vīra Deva of Magadha was sent in the
9th–10th century to "the great Vihara of Kanishka", and "this great monastery was still existing in the times of
Baber and Akbar under the name of Gor-Katari". Babur's Garh-Katri was a resort of Jogis; Erskine put the great
caravanserai on its site. No remains are described.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 echo ASR II: the monastery "survived as Gor Khatri ('Baniya's house')", with
the Babur, Akbar and Vīra Deva references. The 1899 copy is badly garbled by OCR.

**Assessment.** The case rests on the name and later fame of Gor Khatri, argued before the stūpa was located at
Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri. Neither the survey nor the gazetteers test whether Gor Khatri lies west of that stūpa, as
Xuanzang requires. Mizutani's tentative view stands, though weakly.

### 02.04.09 布色羯邏伐底城 (puṣkalāvatī) — Charsadda/Prang; mound surveyed by Garrick; Vivien's Nisatta superseded

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Chārsadda, on the left bank of the Swat a little above its confluence with the Kabul
river, 17 miles from Peshawar (agreed). Cunningham (p. 57–58): Hashtnagar, the towns of Parang and Charsada. Beal
(p. 109) and Watters (p. 214) both say Hashtnagar, tentatively. Majumdar (p. 676): Charsada, certain after
excavation. Dey: Pukely on the Swat. The one different view is Vivien's (p. 308, tentative): approximately Nisatta,
on the left bank of the Kabul river a little *below* the Swat confluence.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR II, 1863–64, pp. 89–90) prefers the Life's 100 li to the Xiyu ji's 50, since the
crossing of the Kabul river 12 miles NE requires it; "the full distance of 16 miles will bring us to the two large
towns of Parang and Charsada". Garrick (ASR XIX, 1881–82, pp. 101–104) went on the ground: he measured the Bala
Hissar mound at Charsadda (an irregular triangle, c. 485,000 sq ft at the top) and located three gates in order to
follow the pilgrim's bearings, concluding that "further research only goes to confirm it". He also sought the stūpa
E of the city and the monastery N of it (see the final section).

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 echo Cunningham ("twin towns Charsada and Prang"), with the bearing and
distance from Peshawar and Ptolemy's Peukelaotis.

**Assessment.** Garrick's survey of the Bala Hissar adds field evidence to Cunningham's reckoning, and Majumdar
reports the site confirmed by excavation. Vivien's Nisatta, made before the survey, no longer competes. Mizutani is
strengthened.

### 02.04.12 化鬼子母窣堵波 — the Hārītī stūpa: Tarangzai by distance only (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani glosses only the name Hārītī and gives no site (unknown). No authority in the coded
books names one. The text puts the stūpa c. 50 li NW of the stūpa near Puṣkalāvatī.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR II, 1863–64, p. 90) counts 50 li (8⅓ miles) NW from the eye-gift stūpa N of Puṣkalāvatī
to the Hārītī stūpa, and 50 li N from there to the Śyāmaka stūpa (02.04.13): "These two places are probably
represented by Tarangzai and Tangi, two small towns on the Swat River", among the eight towns of Hashtnagar. He
reports no remains at either.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Tarangzai is a reckoning of distance and bearing, with no monument behind it. It can be recorded as
Cunningham's candidate, but the stūpa remains unlocated.

### 02.04.13 商莫迦菩薩窣堵波 — the Śyāmaka stūpa: Tangi by distance only (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no site (unknown). Beal (p. 112, tentative): "some 90 to 100 li NNE of Pushkalavati"
by the route, which is a reckoning from the text, not a site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR II, 1863–64, p. 90) puts it at Tangi on the Swat, 50 li N of his Tarangzai (02.04.12),
by distance only. Tangi is also his starting point for the 200 li to Varuṣa (02.04.14). No remains are reported.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Like Beal's reckoning, Cunningham's Tangi restates the route rather than locating a monument. The
stūpa remains unlocated, with Tangi as a candidate by distance.

### 02.04.14 跋虜沙城 — Shahbaz Garhi; Cunningham's Palodheri, abandoned by Cunningham himself

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Shahbaz Garhi, 65 km ENE of Peshawar (agreed), after Foucher and Dani. Majumdar (p.
676) and Dey also say Shahbazgarhi. Majumdar notes that "Cunningham's identification … has been discarded" and that
Cunningham himself (ASR V) later moved to Shahbazgarhi. Cunningham's earlier site, in the *Ancient Geography* (p.
60), was Palodheri ("village of Pali"), and Watters (p. 217) and Beal (p. 112) both repeated it. Beal also wondered
about Takht-i-Bahi. Vivien (p. 309, tentative): Barotch on Cunningham's map.

**Survey.** Cunningham's ASR II (1863–64, pp. 90–91) argues for Palodheri, "10 miles to the north of the great
inscription at Shahbaz garhi": *dheri* means a mound of ruins, "Palo may preserve some portion of the name of
Palusha", the distance of 33 miles from Tangi fits though "its direction is east instead of south-east", and the
Kashmiri-ghar cave lies 3–4 miles ENE (02.04.15). In ASR V (1872–73) the equation has changed without argument in
the passages coded: he writes "Po-lu-sha, or Shahbaz-garhi" (p. 42) and of Songyun's Fo-sha-fu "which I have
identified with Shahbaz-garhi" (p. 65). In the same volume (p. 36) he floats a link between Po-lu-sha and the name
Raja Vara (Virat) attached to the Takht-i-Bahi ruins, but proposes no site.

**Gazetteers.** None identify the town. The Peshawar volumes mention the Shahbazgarhi edict rock only in a margin
heading, which was coded as irrelevant.

**Assessment.** The survey confirms Majumdar's account: Palodheri was Cunningham's view of 1863–64, carried into
the *Ancient Geography*, and he had moved to Shahbaz Garhi by 1872–73. Mizutani stands.

### 02.04.14a 跋虜沙城北窣堵波 — Foucher's Chanaka Dheri undermined by excavation; no replacement

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): Foucher put the stūpa at Chanaka Dheri (Kanaka Chaitya), 20 km N of
Shahbaz Garhi. The Kyoto University excavation from 1959 showed that site to be a Kushan palace, not a monastery.
Mizutani reports this without concluding. No other authority names a site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The only proposed site has been weakened by excavation, and no alternative appears in these
sources. The stūpa is effectively unlocated.

### 02.04.15 彈多落迦山 (daṇḍaloka) — Mekha-Sanda vs the Kashmir Smast cave vs the cave near the Shahbazgarhi edicts

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mekha-Sanda, at the N end of the hills running NE from Shahbaz Garhi (Foucher;
excavated by Kyoto University in 1962–67; two rock-cut caves) (agreed). Cunningham (p. 60): the hill with the great
cave of Kashmiri-Ghar, 3–4 miles ENE of Palodheri (= the Montes Daedali of Justin). Majumdar (p. 676): "About 1½
mile N.E. of the Asokan Inscriptions is a double-chambered cave which answers exactly to the Chinese description of
Sudana's cave". Watters (p. 217) is inclined to read 2,000 li with the Fang-chih and so put the mountain far away.
Beal gives no site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR II, 1863–64, pp. 91–92) chose the Kashmiri-ghar cave because it lies "within three or
four miles of Palodheri, almost in the very position indicated". He knew it from Court and Loewenthal (a largely
natural cave of several chambers, with flights of steps and buildings) and in 1848 had copies made of an
inscription in it from Chargolai, 7 miles away. When he moved Varuṣa to Shahbaz Garhi (02.04.14), the survey did
not re-measure the mountain in the passages coded.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 repeat Cunningham (citing ASR V): the "Kashmir Smats" cave temple near the
summit of the Sakri ridge of Pajja, 8 miles NW of Bazar, with "the ruins of a small city" below; the cave "has not
been thoroughly explored yet".

**Assessment.** Cunningham's Kashmir Smast was fixed from Palodheri, which he himself gave up, and it lies far from
Shahbaz Garhi. Majumdar's cave and Mizutani's Mekha-Sanda both sit in the hills NE of Shahbaz Garhi, which fits the
text's "20 li NE". Mizutani stands.

### 02.04.16 獨角仙人窣堵波 — the Ekaśṛṅga stūpa: Sahri-Bahlol below Takht-i-Bahi (Cunningham) or Butan (Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). Majumdar (p. 677) reports two views without choosing:
Cunningham (ASR V) put it at "Takht-i-Bahai", and Stein at "Butan, a large important Buddhist site, in the Palai
valley" near the S foot of the Shahkot pass.

**Survey.** Cunningham's site (ASR V, 1872–73, p. 42) is in fact Sahri-Bahlol, with Takht-i-Bahi as the mountain:
the Ṛṣi's place lay "to the south of a high mountain which was 16 miles to the north-west of Po-lu-sha, or
Shahbaz-garhi", and "The high mountain I would identify with Takht-i-Bahi, from which Sahri-Bahlol lies south 2½
miles". The stūpa and monastery are the Dhamami stūpa opened by Bellew; Cunningham dug ten statues out of a mound
there. Three pages later (p. 45) he identifies the same Dhamami stūpa with Faxian's and Songyun's eye-gift stūpa
(see the final section).

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The survey turns Majumdar's "Takht-i-Bahai" into a real site with excavated remains, measured from
Shahbaz Garhi. But Cunningham used the same stūpa for two different legends, so the identification is loose.
Stein's Butan, also made on the ground, remains the rival. The stūpa should be recorded as open between these two,
with low confidence.

### 02.04.17 毘摩天女像（崇山） — the Bhīmā image: Karamar hill (Cunningham) vs Takht-i-Bahi vs "near Jamalgarhi"

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Karamar hill, ENE of Shahbaz Garhi (agreed; no authority named). Beal (p. 114,
tentative): projecting 150 li NW from Ohind "would bring us near Jamalgarhi". Dey: Takht-i-Bahi, "containing the
Yoni-tirtha and the celebrated temple of Bhimā Devī". Watters (p. 221) explains the image as a dark-blue rock but
gives no site.

**Survey.** Karamar is Cunningham's (ASR II, 1863–64, p. 92): "a very lofty mountain called Karamar, 3,480 feet in
height, which is exactly 8 miles to the south-east of Palo-dheri". He read the bearing as SE from his Palodheri,
not NE, and identified no shrine on it.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The text's bearing is 50 li NE of Varuṣa (Shahbaz Garhi), which suits Karamar. Mizutani's hill is
thus Cunningham's, now reached from the right starting point without altering the bearing. Beal's and Dey's sites
come from a different reckoning (Beal from Ohind) or from a name association; by Dey's own figures, Takht-i-Bahi
(28 miles NE of Peshawar) lies short of Shahbaz Garhi (40 miles NE), not 50 li beyond it. Mizutani stands, though
no shrine has been found on the hill.

### 03.01.01a 瞢揭釐城 — Mingora (Italian mission) vs Manglaor (Cunningham, Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mingora, c. 5 miles WSW of Manglaor, with the Butkara monastery site in its S suburb
(after the Italian mission) (disputed). Everyone else names Manglaor/Mangora as the capital: Vivien (p. 314,
Manglavor), Cunningham (p. 94–95, tentative), Beal (p. 121, tentative), Watters (p. 223), Majumdar (p. 678;
"supported by Stein") and Dey.

**Gazetteers.** None. The Himalayan Districts II volume (1884) mentions Udyāna only through Darel, its "former
capital".

**Assessment.** Mizutani rests on the Italian excavations around Mingora and Butkara. The older authorities rest on
the name Manglaor and on Stein. Both places lie in the same stretch of the Swat valley, 5 miles apart, and nothing
in these files settles which was the 7th-century capital. Mizutani's choice stands, with medium confidence.

### 03.01.02 忍辱仙人窣堵波 — the Kṣāntivādin stūpa: unlocated

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). The text has the stūpa 4–5 li E of Mangali. Beal gives no
site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The stūpa is unlocated. It should lie just east of whichever site is taken as the capital
(03.01.01a), but no remains have been put forward in these sources.

### 03.01.03 阿波邏羅龍泉 — Apalāla's spring: the head of the Swat at Kalam

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). He notes that the real source of the Swat is far upstream at
Kalam, and that Petech thought the legend moved upstream over time. Vivien (p. 315), Cunningham (p. 95) and Beal
(p. 126) put the spring at the source of the Swat, 250–260 li NE of the capital, without naming a place. Watters
(p. 229, tentative) and Majumdar (p. 678–679) both follow Deane: Kalam, at the junction of the Utrot and Laspur
streams.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Everyone agrees on the head of the Swat, and Deane's Kalam is the only named spot. Mizutani in
effect accepts Kalam as the river's source while doubting that the 250–260 li reach it. The gazetteers add nothing.

### 03.01.06 摩訶伐那伽藍 (mahāvana) — Shināsi stūpa at Kukrai (Mizuno), Mahaban (Cunningham) or Pinjkotai (Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Shināsi stūpa at Kukrai, on the way to Ilām, after Mizuno (uncertain).
Cunningham (p. 71): the Mahaban mountain in Buner, with the monastery on its top. Dey (after Stein): Pinjkotai near
Sunigram in Buner, c. 26 miles S of Manglaur. Beal and Watters give no site.

**Survey.** Cunningham's ASR II (1863–64, p. 98) states the Mahaban identification in passing, in his argument
about Aornos; he did not visit the hill.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** There are three candidates and no decisive evidence among them. Cunningham's Mahaban rests on the
name alone, and Stein's Pinjkotai is a field identification reported only at second hand (Dey). Mizutani's
tentative view stands, with low confidence.

### 03.01.07 摩愉伽藍 — Gōg-dara (Mizuno) or Sura in the Chumla valley (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Gōg-dara stūpa, chosen by direction (Mizuno) (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 71,
tentative): the large village of Sura in the Chumla valley, 10 miles NW of the highest peak of Mahaban. Beal and
Watters give no site.

**Survey.** ASR II (1863–64, p. 98) gives the same Sura identification, by distance from Mahaban, with no remains
described.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Each candidate depends on where its proponent puts Mahāvana (03.01.06): Cunningham measures from
Mahaban, Mizuno from Shināsi. The question cannot be settled from these sources.

### 03.01.08 尸毘迦王本生窣堵波 — the Śibi stūpa: Cunningham's NW foot of Mahaban; Mizutani says unknown

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "未詳" (unknown). Cunningham (p. 94): the NW foot of the Mahaban mountain in the Buner
valley, which he takes to be Faxian's Su-ho-to. Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Cunningham's placement follows from his Mahaban chain (03.01.06–07), not from a found monument. The
stūpa remains unlocated.

### 03.01.09a 蘇摩大窣堵波 — the Suma stūpa in the Adinzai valley (Deane)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: a large stūpa in the Adinzai valley "still known by the name Suma", after Deane in
JRAS 1896 (uncertain). Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Deane's survival of the name is the only evidence, and nothing here contradicts it. Mizutani
stands, with low-to-medium confidence.

### 03.01.11 盧醯呾迦窣堵波 (rohitaka) — a stūpa at Hazara village (Deane)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the stūpa at the village now called Hazara, "said to be still extant", after Deane
(JRAS 1896, p. 660) (uncertain). Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The only evidence is Deane's report, and nothing here contradicts it. Mizutani stands, with low
confidence.

### 03.01.12 遏部多石窣堵波 (adbhuta) — Deane says extant; Watters doubts

**Identifications.** Mizutani names no place (disputed): Deane says the stūpa "still exists", and Watters (p. 237)
calls that doubtful. Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The stūpa is unlocated. The dispute is over whether Deane's monument is the right one, and these
sources cannot decide it.

### 03.01.13 觀自在菩薩精舍 — the Avalokiteśvara vihāra: unlocated

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). He notes that the 慈恩伝 reads 3–4 li for the 30–40 li across
the great river. Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The vihāra is unlocated, and the distance itself is uncertain.

### 03.01.14 藍勃盧山 — the head of the Aushiri valley (Deane) vs the Laspur mountains (Vivien)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the head of the Aushiri valley near Darora, draining into the Panjkora, where there
is a large lake (after Deane, in Watters) (uncertain). Vivien (p. 315): the mountains of Laspur (Laspissor),
between the Chitral river and the Tal valley, 450 li NE of Manglavor. Beal and Watters give no site of their own.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Deane's lake matches the dragon-lake story. Vivien's Laspur is a map reckoning. The gazetteers do
not touch the question, so Mizutani stands, with low confidence.

### 03.03 呾叉始羅國 (takṣaśilā) — Shah-dheri/Sarai-kala, confirmed by an inscription; Vivien's Hasan Abdal is superseded

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Taxila, 21 miles NW of Rawalpindi (Sarai-kala), after Marshall (agreed). Cunningham
(p. 128, 138): "ruins of the ancient city near Shah-dheri". Watters (p. 241), Majumdar (p. 681) and Dey agree. The
one different view is Vivien's (p. 319, tentative): the neighbourhood of Hassan Abdal, perhaps an old site 7–8
miles E or SE of it. Beal (p. 136) and Dey report both views.

**Survey.** Cunningham found the site in 1863 (ASR II, 1863–64, p. 85): "the actual emplacement, which I have
discovered during the present year, being to the east of Shah-dheri", 10 miles SE of Hasan Abdal and 4 miles S of
Vivien's Usman-Katar. He supports it with the pilgrims' three marches from the Indus and with the irrigated land N
of the Tabra Nala (pp. 111–122): "This then must be the site of Taxila". A second visit (ASR V, 1872–73, p. 66)
"has only strengthened my conviction". In the same volume (pp. 67–68) he cites the copper plate of the satrap
"Liako Kujuluka", found (by Delmerick's enquiries) at Thupkia in the middle of Sirsukh, which names "the city of
Takhasila".

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 echo ASR II and add the plate: the identification "is rendered certain by
the measurements recorded by the Chinese pilgrims … and by a copper plate found by Mr. Roberts among the ruins".
Punjab 1919 mentions Taxila only as a feudatory of Kashmir.

**Assessment.** The inscribed copper plate found in the ruins confirms Cunningham's and Mizutani's site. Vivien's
Hasan Abdal, based on the Elāpattra spring (03.03.02), no longer competes.

### 03.03.01a 呾叉始羅國大都城 — Sirsukh (Marshall) vs the Bir mound (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Sirsukh, the third (Kushan) city NNE of Sirkap, after Marshall, and not the Bhir
Mound or Sirkap (agreed). Cunningham (p. 129–130): "there can be little doubt that the city of his time was
situated on the mound of Bir". Beal (p. 136) follows Cunningham's Shah-dheri without choosing among the mounds.

**Survey.** Cunningham's case (ASR II, 1863–64, pp. 117–118) has two parts. On the Bir mound "the old coins are
found in greater numbers" and a man gathered "a double handful of bits of lapis lazuli"; its size suits the 10 li
circuit. And the great stūpa in Babar-khana, his head-gift stūpa (03.03.04), lies 8,000 ft NNE of the mound, i.e.
the text's 12–13 li N. He measured Sirsukh (circuit 15,300 ft, p. 121) but does not discuss it as a candidate. In
ASR V (1872–73, pp. 67–68) he read the copper plate found in Sirsukh, which calls the satrapy NE "of the city of
Takhasila", as proof that Sirkap was Taxila proper and Sirsukh a separate place.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 echo ASR II word for word: coins, lapis, and "probably the site of the
inhabited part of the city in the time of Hwen Thsang".

**Assessment.** The survey's Bir mound rests on surface finds and on a circular argument with the head-gift stūpa,
whose own identification is contested. Cunningham's reading of the plate concerns the 1st century, not 630.
Marshall's excavations, which Mizutani follows, are not reported here, and nothing in these files counts against
Sirsukh. Mizutani stands. The choice of city matters for 03.03.04 and 03.03.06.

### 03.03.03 四大寶藏窣堵波 — Baoti Pind: the stūpa Cunningham excavated, with the bearing corrected

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the ruined stūpa on the ridge above Baoti Pind (agreed). Beal and Watters give no
site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR II, 1863–64, pp. 139–140): "The distance points to the neighbourhood of Baoti Pind,
where I found the ruins of a large town and of several Buddhist monuments. But the bearing is east", since SE would
lead into the open plain. In the gorge is the Langar-kot (Srikot) fort. On the highest, NE point of the Baoti ridge
he found a stūpa 65 ft across on a platform, with a small monastery (70 × 58 ft), and "judged that it might
possibly be the actual stupa which was said to have been built by Asoka". His excavation reached a stone chamber at
8 ft and, under a slab at 14 ft, a large red earthenware vessel.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895, and Attock 1909, echo ASR II, adding "never-failing springs" and the
local tradition giving the hill fort to Raja Sirkap. They report that "it is now impossible to ascertain which of
the ruined stupas is the right one".

**Assessment.** The survey supplies a specific monument with a stūpa, monastery and relic deposit on Mizutani's
ridge, which strengthens the identification. It still requires reading E for the text's SE, a point Mizutani passes
over, and nothing found names the place.

### 03.03.04 捨頭窣堵波 — Bhallar stūpa (Marshall) vs Babar-khana (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Bhallar stūpa at the W end of Sarda hill, c. 8 miles from Taxila station
(Marshall), with the extant stūpa early medieval (agreed). Cunningham (p. 134–135): the great ruined stūpa in the
Babar-khana tract on the Lundi Nala, 1,200 ft N of Jhandiala Pind, c. 2 miles N of the Bir mound. Beal gives no
site.

**Survey.** Cunningham argued at length (ASR II, 1863–64, pp. 114–121): the land N of the city is still called
Babar Khana, "tiger's house"; a mound to the NW is Seri-ki-pind, which he took to refer to the head-gift; and the
road through the gates of Hatial and Sirkap runs "due north" to the Jhandiala stūpa, "which proves incontestably
the very high estimation in which that monument must have been held". On a second visit (ASR V, 1872–73, p. 72) he
excavated a two-storeyed monastery beside it with square corner rooms, which he matched with Xuanzang's corner
pavilions. In ASR XIV (1878–79) he wondered whether c. 80 small stūpas he excavated at the foot of the Meri hill, E
of the old town, belonged to the head-offering's "thousand" stūpas.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 restate the 12–13 li N and Cunningham's view in summary.

**Assessment.** The survey's case rests on names, the road and the excavated monastery, but its 12–13 li N is
measured from the Bir mound. By Cunningham's own plan (p. 121) Sirsukh lies at the NE corner of Babar-khana, so
from Marshall's city the Jhandiala stūpa is not to the north at all. With Sirsukh as the city, Mizutani's Bhallar
stands.

### 03.03.06 拘浪拏太子窣堵波 — Kunāla stūpa: Marshall's Hathiāl site, contested; Cunningham moved from Karmal to the Meri hill

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): Marshall places the stūpa on the N slope of the W end of the Hathiāl
ridge S of Sirkap, beside the "Kunāla monastery". Yamamoto (1940) and others object that Xuanzang's city is
Sirsukh, not Sirkap, so the bearing SE of the city fails. Beal gives no site. The coded *Ancient Geography* has no
entry for this place.

**Survey.** Cunningham first proposed the chief tope of Karmal (ASR II, 1863–64, pp. 149–150), on a N spur of the
Margala range, ESE of the Bir mound and due SE of the Hatial citadel: its position "tallies, therefore, so exactly
with the site of Kunala Stupa … that I have but little hesitation in proposing their identity", and Kunāla may have
been assimilated to Kurmal. He dug the tope (50 ft across) to the rock and found no deposit; a monastery 120 ft
square lay beside it. In ASR XIV (1878–79) he proposed instead a great stūpa site on a terrace at the N foot of the
Meri hill, E of the old town, surrounded by some 80 small votive stūpas that he excavated, admitting "the direction
is east instead of south-east". Karmal is not mentioned there.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 quote ASR II on Karmal, including the "tallies so exactly" sentence; the
topes had been "opened by the villagers".

**Assessment.** The survey offered two sites in turn, both measured from Cunningham's Bir-mound city, and neither
yielded anything that names Kunāla. Marshall's Hathiāl site is already contested. The place should be left open,
with Marshall's site weakened and no survey site a clear replacement.

### 03.04 僧訶補羅國 (siṃhapura) — the Salt Range round Ketas; Cunningham wavered (Malot → Ketas → Malot), Stein returned to Ketas

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ketās (Ketāksh), N of the Salt Range, 16 miles from Pind Dadan Khan (agreed), after
Cunningham's *Ancient Geography* only. Cunningham (p. 143–145), Beal (p. 143, tentative), Majumdar (p. 682) and Dey
agree. Vivien (p. 321, tentative): Sangohi near Jhelam town, rejected by Beal and Cunningham. Watters (p. 249)
refuses the identification and reports that Cunningham later abandoned it.

**Survey.** Cunningham's view moved twice. In ASR II (1863–64, pp. 191–192) the bearing, distance and fish-tanks
"should be Katas; but the whole description will not apply to Katas", and he chose Malot, "known to have been the
capital of the Janjuhas at a very early period", putting the tanks in the Ganiya Nala below Katas. The *Ancient
Geography* (1871) moved to Ketas, with the capital at the Kotera fort. In ASR V (1872–73, p. 85) he went back to
Malot after marching the routes: Shahdheri to Malot is 114 miles by Manikyala and 101 by Fatehjang and Nila,
against the pilgrim's c. 117 miles, and "All these conditions are fulfilled in the site of the ancient city of
Mallot". Ketas, 9 miles E, became the stone stūpa site (03.04.03).

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 reports the Malot view as "doubted even by himself", and prints Stein's field letters
for "the Kitas locality" (forts on the surrounding hills, the stūpa before the Sat-Ghara temples, the Jaina site at
Murti; see 03.04.01a–03), which Bühler accepted "without hesitation". Dehra Dun 1911 (the Lakhamandal praśasti)
attests the kingdom but not its place.

**Assessment.** The country is the Salt Range round Ketas and Malot, 9 miles apart, on every view. Watters'
"abandoned" refers to ASR V's Malot. Stein's later fieldwork answers both Watters and Malot, so Mizutani's Ketas is
strengthened, though Mizutani cites neither Stein nor Cunningham's reports.

### 03.04.01a 僧訶補羅國大都城 — the plain N of Ketas (Stein), Malot (Cunningham, ASR), or the Kotera fort (Ancient Geography)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Kotera, the ruined fort on the hill above Ketās (uncertain). This is Cunningham's
site in the *Ancient Geography* (p. 145, tentative), and it depends on emending the text's 40–50 li to 4–5 li. Beal
gives no site, noting only that a capital ringed by crags rules out the plain of Taki. Watters (p. 253) rejects
Ketas.

**Survey.** In both his reports Cunningham put the capital not at Kotera but at Malot, the hill-fort 9 miles W of
Ketas (ASR II, 1863–64, p. 192; ASR V, 1872–73, p. 85; see 03.04). Malot fulfils, in his words, the text's city
"situated on a high mountain, which was both difficult and dangerous of access"; from it from it the 40–50 li to
the springs of Ketas stand unemended, with E for SE (p. 90). Cunningham admits one difficulty: there are "no signs
of any occupation beneath the cliffs to the south", where the Aśoka stūpa should be (03.04.02).

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 prints Stein: "I consider Ketas, or rather the field of ruins lying one or two miles
further north, to be the site of the ancient town", 40–50 li NW of the Jaina temples at Murti; chained Murti–Ketas
is 4½ miles, so "the discrepancy is not great". The forts on the surrounding hills are cited as proof of more than
a pilgrimage site.

**Assessment.** Kotera is the weakest of the three: Cunningham himself abandoned it, and it needs an emendation.
Stein's plain and Cunningham's Malot both keep the text's 40–50 li, each measured to a different stone stūpa
(03.04.03). Malot fits the "high mountain"; Stein's city fits his excavated Murti. Stein's is the later,
field-based view, but the choice is open.

### 03.04.02 僧訶補羅城南窣堵波 — the Aśoka stūpa S of the city: the basement before the Sat-Ghara temples, read as a stūpa or as Cunningham's Jaina temple

**Identifications.** Mizutani: a large base, probably of a stūpa, at the foot of the Kotera hill, with a
Kashmiri-style temple in front (uncertain), after Majumdar (p. 683): "Facing them to the east is the basement of a
great structure probably a Buddhist stupa". Beal gives no site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR V, 1872–73, p. 90), with his city at Malot, found nothing beneath the cliffs to the
south and put the stūpa on the north side, against the text. At Ketas (p. 92) he took the "large ruined temple,
marked C in my plan", a basement 68½ × 56½ ft with pilasters like those of Buddhist buildings at Shahdheri and
Manikyala, "much older than the group of Brahmanical temples in the Kashmirian style, which stand immediately above
it", to be the Jaina temple of the text (see the final section).

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 prints Stein: "In the large stupa, situated before the east front of the Sat-Ghara
temples, I believe I recognise the stupa of Asoka, which, Hiuen Tsiang says, lay to the south of the town". The
gazetteer coding marks this as "new"; see the final section.

**Assessment.** Stein, Majumdar and Cunningham appear to describe one early basement beside the Sat-Ghara temples,
though the sources do not prove it is the same. Stein and Majumdar read it as a stūpa; Cunningham as a temple. With
Stein's city to the north it lies south of the city, as the text requires. Mizutani's site is supported, but what
the structure was is uncertain.

### 03.04.03 僧訶補羅城東南石窣堵波 — the stone stūpa and Jain site: Murti (Stein) vs the Ketas spring (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ketās with its sacred pools, following Cunningham's reading of 4–5 li (uncertain).
Cunningham (p. 143): "the holy tanks of Ketaksh". Beal implies the same. Watters (p. 253) rejects it but finds
Stein's Jain sculptures from Murti "not conclusive".

**Survey.** Cunningham kept the springs at Ketas in all three versions. In ASR II (1863–64, p. 192) they were the
pools of the Ganiya Nala below Katas. In ASR V (1872–73, pp. 90–92), measuring 9 miles from Malot, he counted "no
less than seven other pools" above the holy pool, some with remains of walls, in a bend of the Ghata Nala; put the
stūpa on the 50-ft rocky spur inside the bend, where a Bairagi's house stands; took a ruined monastery 150 ft
square 400 ft W, then being dug out for stone, as the deserted monastery; and put the Jaina temple at basement C
(03.04.02).

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 prints Stein: down the Gandhala valley, Murti "seems to agree most remarkably with
Hiuen Tsiang's description" (pools with trees and fish); excavation of the c. 40-ft mound showed "a true stupa";
over 30 camel-loads of Jaina-style sculpture went to Lahore; local memory calls the temple Jaina; Murti is 4½ miles
from Ketas, chained. Stein found "at Ketas no group of tanks such as that described".

**Assessment.** Both candidates now have field evidence. Cunningham's walled pools, spur and monastery answer
Stein's denial of tanks at Ketas, but his stūpa was not excavated and his Jaina temple is a reading of a basement.
Stein's Murti has an excavated stūpa with Jaina sculpture and local tradition. Murti remains the better-supported
alternative to Mizutani's Ketas, but with less margin.

### 03.04.05 摩訶薩埵捨身處（大石門） — the body-offering site: Banj (Stein) vs Manikyala, where Court's inscription names the body-offering

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): Banj mountain on the Mahaban spur in Buner, reached by crossing the Indus
from the Taxila side. Majumdar, Petech and Chavannes agree, and Stein found an ancient well there (Vajrastūpa
inscription). Cunningham (p. 140): Manikyala, reading 信度 as the Suhan river. Beal (p. 145) follows him. Watters (p.
255) finds Manikyala "attended with serious difficulties". Majumdar (p. 682): "Everybody is now agreed that
Cunningham was mistaken"; he reports Banj, with the bearing corrected from SE to NE.

**Survey.** Cunningham argued it in ASR II (1863–64, pp. 154–165) and repeated it in ASR XIV (1878–79, p. 50): 200
li SE of Taxila "is the exact bearing and distance of Manikyala", as Songyun's three days SE agree. The
body-offering stūpa is General Court's tope (No. 2): "The Huta-murta or 'body-offering' is twice mentioned in the
inscription that was found covering the deposit" (with Kaniṣka's gold and Roman denarii). He adds the red soil,
Court's No. 5 1,200 ft to the S as the stūpa where the Bodhisattva drew his blood, and an excavated gateway ESE as
the "great stone gate". In ASR XIV (pp. 7–8) he moved the blood stūpa to a stūpa near Rama-ka-Pind, 24–25 miles S,
reading the text's 140–150 paces (百四五十歩) as li.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 echo ASR II, with the Raja Rasalu legend that Cunningham took as the tiger
story's successor.

**Assessment.** The inscription, if Cunningham read it rightly, shows that Manikyala was itself a body-offering
site in Kushan times, the strongest evidence for it. But the itinerary returns to the north border of Taxila and
crosses the Indus, which Cunningham must explain away. Banj fits the route, so Mizutani stands, with lower
confidence.

### 03.04.06 捨身北石窣堵波 — the stone stūpa N of the body-offering: unlocated; Cunningham's two incompatible proposals

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): existence unknown. He notes that Cunningham's Manikiāla stūpa is a
1st–2nd century Gandhāran monument. Watters (p. 255) rejects Manikyala. Beal gives no site.

**Survey.** In ASR II (1863–64, p. 172) Cunningham argued that Ventura's great tope at Manikyala post-dated
Xuanzang, since Sassanian-Arab coins lay 64 ft inside it, so "Hwen Thsang's silence is due to the non-existence of
the tope". In ASR XIV (1878–79, pp. 1–3) he made that same great tope Aśoka's stūpa N of the body-offering, reading
"south" for "north", since it stands S of Court's tope.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 treat Manikiala as a whole without singling out this stūpa.

**Assessment.** The survey's two proposals contradict each other, and the later one needs the bearing reversed. The
stūpa stands or falls with 03.04.05, and if the site is Banj nothing in these sources locates it.

### 03.04.07 孤山伽藍 — the monastery on the isolated hill: unlocated; Cunningham's Mera turned out a Muslim tomb

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "場所も未詳" (unknown). The text puts it 50 li E of the body-offering stūpa. Beal gives
no site.

**Survey.** Cunningham (ASR XIV, 1878–79, pp. 1–3), measuring from Manikyala, proposed Mera, 9 miles E of the
Manikyala tope: the "exact agreement of both bearing and distance" favoured it. On the ground he found that "the
thup of Mera was the dome of a Muhammadan tomb", built of old cut stones.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The survey's candidate depends on Manikyala (03.04.05) and yielded no Buddhist remains. The
monastery is unlocated.

### 03.05 烏剌尸國 (uraśā) — Hazara; the capital Haripur (Watters) vs Mangali (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Hazara district, with its capital at Haripur (after Watters) (agreed). Beal (p.
147), Majumdar (p. 680), Dey (after Stein) and Vivien (p. 321) all say broadly Hazara. Watters (p. 257, tentative)
has Hazara with its capital at Haripur, after Swynnerton. Cunningham (p. 119–120, tentative) has Ptolemy's Varsa =
the district of Rash in Dhantawar, W of Muzaffarabad, with the capital "probably … at Mangali, which is said by the
people to have been the ancient capital of the district", between Noshahra and Mansera. Dey also reports Wilson's
valley of Gurez.

**Gazetteers.** Punjab 1919 names "the Urash plain" only as a Kashmir feudatory. No Hazara gazetteer appears among
the hits.

**Assessment.** The country is agreed. The capital is not: Cunningham's Mangali rests on local tradition, and
Haripur is only Swynnerton's suggestion, reported by Watters. Mizutani's Hazara stands, but the capital should be
left open, with Mangali as a candidate.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Everyone agrees on the area between Srinagar and Pandrethan. No monument is named, and the
