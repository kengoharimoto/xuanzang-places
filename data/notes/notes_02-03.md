# Notes on disputed places: juan 2 and juan 3

Range: Lampāka, Nagarahāra, Gandhāra (juan 2); Udyāna, Balūra, Taxila, Siṃhapura, Uraśā, Kashmir, Punach,
Rājapura (juan 3). 46 notes, in master-table order.

Gazetteer volumes used here (each counted once, though most exist in two scans): Peshawar 1883 and 1899;
Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895; Attock 1909; Jhelam 1907; Dehra Dun 1911; Punjab (Rose's Glossary) 1919; Himalayan
Districts II 1884; an unidentified volume of 1874; Bombay Gazetteer XIV (Thana, Places) 1882. Hazara, Shahpur and
Kashmir volumes give no hits in this range. Most of the Panjab passages copy Cunningham, from the *Ancient
Geography* or ASR II and V. The one body of independent argument is Stein's work at Ketas and Murti, which the
Jhelam gazetteer (1907) prints at length.

---

### 02.02a 濫波國大都城 — capital unknown; Mizutani's only candidate lies outside Lampāka

**Identifications.** Mizutani: capital unknown; "possibly Begram on the Logar river 10 km SE of Kabul" (after
Mizuno) (uncertain). Vivien (p. 301): Mandrâour (Mendrâwar), in the angle where the Alingar joins the Kabul river
(tentative). No one else names a site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Mizutani's candidate does not fit the region. Cunningham (p. 50) and Beal (p. 90) bound Lampāka as
the tract north of the Kabul river between the Alingar and the Kunar, and a Begram on the Logar near Kabul lies
well to the west of that. The master table already flags the quotation as odd. Vivien's Mandrawar is the only
candidate inside Lampāka, but it is a guess made without archaeological evidence. The capital stays unidentified,
and Mizutani's suggestion should be treated as doubtful (possibly a slip in his source).

### 02.03 那揭羅曷國 (nagarahāra) — the Jalalabad district; no real dispute

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Jalalabad and the southern Kabul-river basin around it (uncertain; his wording is
hedged, but he names no rival). Vivien (p. 302), Cunningham (p. 51), Beal (p. 91), Watters (p. 184) and Dey all
put it in the Jalalabad district/Nangnihar. Cunningham and Beal add Ptolemy's Nagara (Dionysopolis). Majumdar
(p. 674) gives no view of his own.

**Gazetteers.** Two volumes (Himalayan Districts II 1884; unidentified 1874) mention Nagarahāra only in passing, as
subject to Kapiśa. They add nothing.

**Assessment.** Every authority agrees. Mizutani's "uncertain" reflects his hedged phrasing rather than any
competing site.

### 02.03.01a 那揭羅曷國大都城 — Begram W of Jalalabad (Cunningham) vs Simpson's site at the Surkhab–Kabul junction

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Dasht-i-Begrām near Chahār-bāgh, SW of Jalalabad (agreed; no authority named).
Cunningham (p. 51–52): Begram, c. 2 miles W of Jalalabad. Vivien (p. 306): the ruins at Begram next to the village
of Nagrak, and explicitly not Balabagh. Against them, Simpson's site: Beal (p. 91) puts the town "in the angle
formed by the junction of the Surkhar and Kabul rivers", and Watters (p. 187, tentative) puts it at the Nagar
fortress on Simpson's rock (= Babur's Adinapur). Majumdar (p. 675) reports Simpson's Bala Hissar ruins c. 4 miles
E of Jalalabad without choosing.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The authorities split between Begram (Cunningham, Vivien, Mizutani) and Simpson's junction site
(Beal, Watters). The gazetteers do not address the question. Nothing here decides it, so Mizutani stands with only
moderate confidence.

### 02.03.02 燃燈佛受記窣堵波 — Ahin-Posh (Higuchi) or the "Nagara Goondee" tope (Masson, Simpson)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: possibly the Ahin-Pōsh stūpa E of the old city (after Higuchi) (uncertain). Watters
(p. 187, tentative): the "Nagara Goondee" tope c. 3 furlongs E/SE of the Nagar rock (after Masson and Simpson).

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Each candidate depends on where its proponent puts the capital (02.03.01a): Higuchi measures from
Begram, Watters from Simpson's rock. The question is still open, and the gazetteers do not change it.

### 02.03.03 瞿波羅龍窟（佛影窟） — the Shadow Cave: somewhere in the cliffs S/SW of Jalalabad

**Identifications.** Mizutani: among the Syāh-sang rocks S of Chahār-bāgh, along the cliffs S of Jalalabad; which
cave cannot be told (after Higuchi) (uncertain). Watters (p. 193, tentative): the E bank of a torrent in the hills
SW of Padmapur; the range running SW from the Ahin Posh tope towards Sultanpur is acceptable; the monastery is
perhaps Gunda Chismeh.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Both put the cave in the same range of hills south-west of the city, and neither can point to the
cave itself. The gazetteers add nothing.

### 02.04.02 卑鉢羅樹 — the pipal tree 8–9 li SE of Peshawar: Babur's "stupendous tree", now gone

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown), but his note places the Kaniṣka stūpa (Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri)
just south of the tree. Cunningham (p. 91–92, tentative): 8–9 li SE of Peshawar, probably the "stupendous tree" of
Begram that Babur saw in 1505, which has since disappeared.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 repeat Cunningham: "the holy pipal tree, at 8 or 9 *li*, or 1½ mile, to the
south-east of the city". Babur saw it in 1505, but Abul Fazl (1594) does not mention it, so it had perished by
then. They also note that no remains of the stūpa beside it survive.

**Assessment.** The site of the tree follows from 02.04.03: it stood immediately north of Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri, c. 1½
miles SE of Peshawar, and nothing of it remains. The gazetteers add the Babur and Abul Fazl dating but no physical
evidence.

### 02.04.03 迦膩色迦大窣堵波 — Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri; a stray footnote suggests Manikyala

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri, c. 1 km SE of Peshawar, excavated by Spooner in 1908–09 (agreed).
Vivien (p. 308): under a lieue SE of Peshawar, no site named. Cunningham (p. 92): close S of the holy tree; "No
remains of this great stupa now exist". Majumdar (p. 678): Shah-ji-ki-Dheri, where the relic casket was found.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 repeat Cunningham: no remains. One footnote in the Thana volume of the
Bombay Gazetteer (1882), on the relic mound at Peshawar, says "This is perhaps the great Mânikyâla tope which was
opened by General Ventura in 1830". It cites Wilson's *Ariana Antiqua* and offers no argument.

**Assessment.** The excavated relic casket settles the identification. The Thana footnote is a confusion with
Manikyala, made far from the region, and carries no weight.

### 02.04.06 迦膩色迦伽藍 — Kaniṣka's monastery: Gor Khattri, by name and tradition only

**Identifications.** Mizutani: possibly Gor Khattri at the NE corner of Peshawar (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 93)
and Watters (p. 208) both make the same tentative identification with the Gurh-Katri of Babur and Akbar.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 repeat Cunningham: there are no remains of the stūpa, and the monastery to
its west "survived as Gor Khatri ('Baniya's house')". They add that Babur and Akbar knew the place, and that Vīra
Deva of Magadha was sent in the 9th–10th century to "the great Vihara of Kanishka". The 1899 copy is badly
garbled by OCR.

**Assessment.** Cunningham's argument rests on the name and later fame of Gor Khatri. He made it before the stūpa
was located at Shāh-ji-ki-Dheri, and the sources here do not test whether Gor Khatri actually lies west of that
stūpa, as Xuanzang requires. The gazetteers add no new evidence, so Mizutani's tentative view stands, though
weakly.

### 02.04.09 布色羯邏伐底城 (puṣkalāvatī) — Charsadda/Prang; Vivien's Nisatta is superseded

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Chārsadda, on the left bank of the Swat a little above its confluence with the
Kabul river, 17 miles from Peshawar (agreed). Cunningham (p. 57–58): Hashtnagar, the towns of Parang and Charsada.
Beal (p. 109) and Watters (p. 214) both say Hashtnagar, tentatively. Majumdar (p. 676): Charsada, certain after
excavation. Dey: Pukely on the Swat. The one different view is Vivien's (p. 308, tentative): approximately Nisatta,
on the left bank of the Kabul river a little *below* the Swat confluence.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 repeat Cunningham ("twin towns Charsada and Prang"). They support it with
the bearing and distance from Peshawar (100 li NE across the Kabul river) and with Ptolemy's Peukelaotis.

**Assessment.** Vivien's Nisatta was an approximation made before Cunningham's survey. The other authorities and
the gazetteers point to Charsadda/Prang, which Majumdar reports as confirmed by excavation. Mizutani stands.

### 02.04.12 化鬼子母窣堵波 — the Hārītī stūpa: unlocated

**Identifications.** Mizutani glosses only the name Hārītī and gives no site (unknown). No authority in these files
names one. The text puts the stūpa c. 50 li NW of the Brahmā–Indra stūpas near Puṣkalāvatī.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The stūpa is unlocated. Nothing in these sources helps.

### 02.04.13 商莫迦菩薩窣堵波 — the Śyāmaka stūpa: unlocated

**Identifications.** Mizutani: no site (unknown). Beal (p. 112, tentative): "some 90 to 100 li NNE of
Pushkalavati" by the route, which is a reckoning from the text, not a site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The stūpa is unlocated. Beal's reckoning only restates the route, and the gazetteers add nothing.

### 02.04.14 跋虜沙城 — Shahbaz Garhi; Cunningham's Palodheri is superseded

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Shahbaz Garhi, 65 km ENE of Peshawar (agreed), after Foucher and Dani. Majumdar
(p. 676) and Dey also say Shahbazgarhi. Majumdar notes that "Cunningham's identification … has been discarded" and
that Cunningham himself (ASR V) later moved to Shahbazgarhi. Cunningham's earlier site, in the *Ancient Geography*
(p. 60), was Palodheri ("village of Pali"), and Watters (p. 217) and Beal (p. 112) both repeated it. Beal also
wondered about Takht-i-Bahi. Vivien (p. 309, tentative): Barotch on Cunningham's map.

**Gazetteers.** None identify the town. The Peshawar volumes mention the Shahbazgarhi edict rock only in a margin
heading, which was coded as irrelevant.

**Assessment.** The dispute is historical: the *Ancient Geography* has Palodheri, while Cunningham's later reports
and everyone after him have Shahbaz Garhi. Mizutani stands.

### 02.04.14a 跋虜沙城北窣堵波 — Foucher's Chanaka Dheri undermined by excavation; no replacement

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): Foucher put the stūpa at Chanaka Dheri (Kanaka Chaitya), 20 km N of
Shahbaz Garhi. The Kyoto University excavation from 1959 showed that site to be a Kushan palace, not a
monastery. Mizutani reports this without concluding. No other authority names a site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The only proposed site has been weakened by excavation, and no alternative appears in these
sources. The stūpa is effectively unlocated.

### 02.04.15 彈多落迦山 (daṇḍaloka) — Mekha-Sanda vs the Kashmir Smast cave vs the cave near the Shahbazgarhi edicts

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mekha-Sanda, at the N end of the hills running NE from Shahbaz Garhi (Foucher;
excavated by Kyoto University in 1962–67; two rock-cut caves) (agreed). Cunningham (p. 60): the hill with the great
cave of Kashmiri-Ghar, 3–4 miles ENE of Palodheri (= the Montes Daedali of Justin). Majumdar (p. 676): "About 1½
mile N.E. of the Asokan Inscriptions is a double-chambered cave which answers exactly to the Chinese description
of Sudana's cave". Watters (p. 217) is inclined to read 2,000 li with the Fang-chih and so put the mountain far
away. Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** Peshawar 1883 and 1899 repeat Cunningham (ASR V): the "Kashmir Smats" cave temple near the summit
of the Sakri ridge of Pajja, 8 miles NW of Bazar. Below it are "the ruins of a small city", and the gazetteers say
the cave "has not been thoroughly explored yet".

**Assessment.** There are three candidates. Cunningham's Kashmir Smast was measured from his discarded Palodheri
(02.04.14) and lies far from Shahbaz Garhi. Majumdar's cave and Mizutani's Mekha-Sanda both sit in the hills NE of
Shahbaz Garhi, which fits the text's "20 li NE". The gazetteers only add a description of Cunningham's site, so
Mizutani stands.

### 02.04.16 獨角仙人窣堵波 — the Ekaśṛṅga stūpa: Takht-i-Bahi (Cunningham) or Butan (Stein), reported by Majumdar

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). Majumdar (p. 677) reports two views without choosing:
Cunningham (ASR V) put it at Takht-i-Bahai, and Stein at "Butan, a large important Buddhist site, in the Palai
valley" near the S foot of the Shahkot pass.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Mizutani leaves the place open, but Majumdar's note supplies two candidates, and Stein's Butan is
the later of the two, made on the ground. No decisive evidence is assembled here. Butan should be recorded as the
leading candidate, with low confidence.

### 02.04.17 毘摩天女像（崇山） — the Bhīmā image: Karamar hill vs Takht-i-Bahi vs "near Jamalgarhi"

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Karamar hill, ENE of Shahbaz Garhi (agreed; no authority named). Beal (p. 114,
tentative): projecting 150 li NW from Ohind "would bring us near Jamalgarhi". Dey: Takht-i-Bahi, "containing the
Yoni-tirtha and the celebrated temple of Bhimā Devī". Watters (p. 221) explains the image as a dark-blue rock but
gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The text's bearing is 50 li NE of Varuṣa (Shahbaz Garhi), which suits Mizutani's Karamar hill.
Beal's and Dey's sites come from a different reckoning (Beal from Ohind) or from a name association. By Dey's
own figures, Takht-i-Bahi (28 miles NE of Peshawar) lies short of Shahbaz Garhi (40 miles NE), not 50 li beyond it. Mizutani stands, though he cites no excavated evidence.

### 03.01.01a 瞢揭釐城 — Mingora (Italian mission) vs Manglaor (Cunningham, Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Mingora, c. 5 miles WSW of Manglaor, with the Butkara monastery site in its S
suburb (after the Italian mission) (disputed). Everyone else names Manglaor/Mangora as the capital: Vivien (p. 314,
Manglavor), Cunningham (p. 94–95, tentative), Beal (p. 121, tentative), Watters (p. 223), Majumdar (p. 678;
"supported by Stein") and Dey.

**Gazetteers.** None. The Himalayan Districts II volume (1884) mentions Udyāna only through Darel, its "former
capital".

**Assessment.** Mizutani rests on the Italian excavations around Mingora and Butkara. The older authorities rest on
the name Manglaor and on Stein. Both places lie in the same stretch of the Swat valley, 5 miles apart, and nothing
in these files settles which was the 7th-century capital. Mizutani's choice stands, with medium confidence.

### 03.01.02 忍辱仙人窣堵波 — the Kṣāntivādin stūpa: unlocated

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). The text has the stūpa 4–5 li E of Mangali. Beal gives no
site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The stūpa is unlocated. It should lie just east of whichever site is taken as the capital
(03.01.01a), but no remains have been put forward in these sources.

### 03.01.03 阿波邏羅龍泉 — Apalāla's spring: the head of the Swat at Kalam

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). He notes that the real source of the Swat is far upstream at
Kalam, and that Petech thought the legend moved upstream over time. Vivien (p. 315), Cunningham (p. 95) and Beal
(p. 126) put the spring at the source of the Swat, 250–260 li NE of the capital, without naming a place. Watters
(p. 229, tentative) and Majumdar (p. 678–679) both follow Deane: Kalam, at the junction of the Utrot and Laspur
streams.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Everyone agrees on the head of the Swat, and Deane's Kalam is the only named spot. Mizutani in
effect accepts Kalam as the river's source while doubting that the 250–260 li reach it. The gazetteers add nothing.

### 03.01.06 摩訶伐那伽藍 (mahāvana) — Shināsi stūpa at Kukrai (Mizuno), Mahaban (Cunningham) or Pinjkotai (Stein)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Shināsi stūpa at Kukrai, on the way to Ilām, after Mizuno (uncertain).
Cunningham (p. 71): the Mahaban mountain in Buner, with the monastery on its top. Dey (after Stein): Pinjkotai near
Sunigram in Buner, c. 26 miles S of Manglaur. Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** There are three candidates and no decisive evidence among them. Cunningham's Mahaban rests on the
name alone, and Stein's Pinjkotai is a field identification reported only at second hand (Dey). The gazetteers do
not touch the question, so Mizutani's tentative view stands, with low confidence.

### 03.01.07 摩愉伽藍 — Gōg-dara (Mizuno) or Sura in the Chumla valley (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Gōg-dara stūpa, chosen by direction (Mizuno) (uncertain). Cunningham (p. 71,
tentative): the large village of Sura in the Chumla valley, 10 miles NW of the highest peak of Mahaban. Beal and
Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Each candidate depends on where its proponent puts Mahāvana (03.01.06): Cunningham measures from
Mahaban, Mizuno from Shināsi. The question cannot be settled from these sources.

### 03.01.08 尸毘迦王本生窣堵波 — the Śibi stūpa: Cunningham's NW foot of Mahaban; Mizutani says unknown

**Identifications.** Mizutani: "未詳" (unknown). Cunningham (p. 94): the NW foot of the Mahaban mountain in the
Buner valley, which he takes to be Faxian's Su-ho-to. Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Cunningham's placement follows from his Mahaban chain (03.01.06–07), not from a found monument. The
stūpa remains unlocated.

### 03.01.09a 蘇摩大窣堵波 — the Suma stūpa in the Adinzai valley (Deane)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: a large stūpa in the Adinzai valley "still known by the name Suma", after Deane in
JRAS 1896 (uncertain). Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Deane's survival of the name is the only evidence, and nothing here contradicts it. Mizutani stands,
with low-to-medium confidence.

### 03.01.11 盧醯呾迦窣堵波 (rohitaka) — a stūpa at Hazara village (Deane)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the stūpa at the village now called Hazara, "said to be still extant", after Deane
(JRAS 1896, p. 660) (uncertain). Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The only evidence is Deane's report, and nothing here contradicts it. Mizutani stands, with low
confidence.

### 03.01.12 遏部多石窣堵波 (adbhuta) — Deane says extant; Watters doubts

**Identifications.** Mizutani names no place (disputed): Deane says the stūpa "still exists", and Watters (p. 237)
calls that doubtful. Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The stūpa is unlocated. The dispute is over whether Deane's monument is the right one, and these
sources cannot decide it.

### 03.01.13 觀自在菩薩精舍 — the Avalokiteśvara vihāra: unlocated

**Identifications.** Mizutani gives no site (unknown). He notes that the 慈恩伝 reads 3–4 li for the 30–40 li across
the great river. Beal and Watters give no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** The vihāra is unlocated, and the distance itself is uncertain.

### 03.01.14 藍勃盧山 — the head of the Aushiri valley (Deane) vs the Laspur mountains (Vivien)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the head of the Aushiri valley near Darora, draining into the Panjkora, where there
is a large lake (after Deane, in Watters) (uncertain). Vivien (p. 315): the mountains of Laspur (Laspissor),
between the Chitral river and the Tal valley, 450 li NE of Manglavor. Beal and Watters give no site of their own.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Deane's lake matches the dragon-lake story. Vivien's Laspur is a map reckoning. The gazetteers do
not touch the question, so Mizutani stands, with low confidence.

### 03.03 呾叉始羅國 (takṣaśilā) — Shah-dheri/Sarai-kala, confirmed by an inscription; Vivien's Hasan Abdal is superseded

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Taxila, 21 miles NW of Rawalpindi (Sarai-kala), after Marshall (agreed). Cunningham
(p. 128, 138): "ruins of the ancient city near Shah-dheri". Watters (p. 241), Majumdar (p. 681) and Dey agree.
The one different view is Vivien's (p. 319, tentative): the neighbourhood of Hassan Abdal, perhaps an old site
7–8 miles E or SE of it. Beal (p. 136) and Dey report both views.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 repeat Cunningham but add a decisive piece of local evidence: the
identification "is rendered certain by the measurements recorded by the Chinese pilgrims … and by a copper plate
found by Mr. Roberts among the ruins", which names Takṣaśilā. Punjab 1919 mentions Taxila only as a feudatory of
Kashmir.

**Assessment.** The inscribed copper plate found at the ruins confirms Cunningham's and Mizutani's site. Vivien's
Hasan Abdal, which was based on the Elāpattra spring (03.03.02), no longer competes.

### 03.03.01a 呾叉始羅國大都城 — Sirsukh (Marshall) vs the Bir mound (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Sirsukh, the third (Kushan) city NNE of Sirkap, after Marshall, and not the Bhir
Mound or Sirkap (agreed). Cunningham (p. 129–130): "there can be little doubt that the city of his time was
situated on the mound of Bir". Beal (p. 136) follows Cunningham's Shah-dheri without choosing among the mounds.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 repeat Cunningham. On the Bir mound "the old coins are found in greater
numbers than in any other part of the ruins", and a man collected "a double handful of bits of lapis lazuli" for
Cunningham. From the size of the place they conclude that it was "probably the site of the inhabited part of the
city in the time of Hwen Thsang".

**Assessment.** The gazetteers report Cunningham's surface evidence (coins, lapis), gathered before Marshall
excavated. Mizutani follows Marshall's excavations, which are not reported here. Nothing in these files counts
against Sirsukh, so Mizutani stands. The choice of city matters for 03.03.04 and 03.03.06.

### 03.03.03 四大寶藏窣堵波 — Baoti Pind, accepted with the bearing corrected

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the ruined stūpa on the ridge above Baoti Pind (agreed). Beal and Watters give no
site.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895, and Attock 1909, repeat Cunningham's argument: "The distance points to
the neighbourhood of Bāoti Pind, where are the ruins of a large town and of several Buddhist monuments. But the
bearing is east", since SE would lead "far away from the hills into the open plain". They add ancient coins in the
ruins, "never-failing springs", and the Langarkot/Srikot hill fort, which local tradition gives to Raja Sirkap.
Cunningham "has little or no doubt" about the site, but "it is now impossible to ascertain which of the ruined
stupas is the right one".

**Assessment.** The gazetteers show that the identification is Cunningham's and that it requires changing the
text's SE to E. Mizutani passes over that point. The site is otherwise well supported by ruins and terrain, so
Mizutani stands. The gazetteers make the bearing problem visible rather than strengthening the case.

### 03.03.04 捨頭窣堵波 — Bhallar stūpa (Marshall) vs Babar-khana (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: the Bhallar stūpa at the W end of Sarda hill, c. 8 miles from Taxila station
(Marshall), with the extant stūpa early medieval (agreed). Cunningham (p. 134–135): the great ruined stūpa in the
Babar-khana tract on the Lundi Nala, 1,200 ft N of Jhandiala Pind, c. 2 miles N of the Bir mound. Beal gives no
site.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 restate Xuanzang's 12–13 li N of the city and Cunningham's view in
summary. They add nothing new.

**Assessment.** Each candidate follows from its proponent's capital: Cunningham measures from the Bir mound,
Marshall from Sirsukh (03.03.01a). With Sirsukh as the city, Mizutani's Bhallar stands. The gazetteers do not
bear on the question.

### 03.03.06 拘浪拏太子窣堵波 — Kunāla stūpa: Marshall's Hathiāl site, objections to it, and Cunningham's Karmal

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): Marshall places the stūpa on the N slope of the W end of the Hathiāl
ridge S of Sirkap, beside the "Kunāla monastery". Yamamoto (1940) and others object that Xuanzang's city is
Sirsukh, not Sirkap, so the bearing SE of the city fails. Beal gives no site. The coded *Ancient Geography* has no
entry for this place.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 quote Cunningham (ASR II) on the ruined topes, monasteries and caves near
Karmal (Kurmal), next to the villages of Karm Gujar and Karm Parcha: "The position of the chief tope of Karmal
tallies so exactly with the site of Kunāla stūpa, as described by Hwen Thsang, as to leave little doubt of their
identity". Cunningham also argues that the name Kunāla was assimilated to Kurmal. The topes had been "opened by the
villagers".

**Assessment.** The gazetteers supply a candidate Mizutani does not mention: Cunningham's Karmal, supported by
position and a place-name argument. Cunningham measured from his Bir-mound city, however, and the sources here do
not show whether Karmal also lies SE of Sirsukh. Marshall's Hathiāl site is already contested. Karmal should be
added as an alternative to be checked on the map, with low confidence.

### 03.04 僧訶補羅國 (siṃhapura) — the Ketas locality in the Salt Range; strongly supported

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ketās (Ketāksh), N of the Salt Range, 16 miles from Pind Dadan Khan (agreed), after
Cunningham only. Cunningham (p. 143–145), Beal (p. 143, tentative), Majumdar (p. 682) and Dey agree. Vivien
(p. 321, tentative) proposed Sangohi near Jhelam town, which Beal and Cunningham reject. Watters (p. 249) refuses
to accept the identification: Ketas "is not enough for a basis of identification". He also reports that Cunningham
later abandoned it.

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 is the key source. It reports that Cunningham wavered and moved to Malot, which was
"doubted even by himself, and later investigators, such as Dr. M. A. Stein, have decided against it". It then
prints Stein's field letters arguing for "the Kitas locality". The evidence is the remains of ancient forts on the
surrounding hills, the stūpa in front of the Sat-Ghara temples, and the Jaina site at Murti (see 03.04.01a–03).
Bühler accepted Stein's argument "without hesitation". Dehra Dun 1911 cites the Lakhamandal praśasti, in which a
princess "of the royal race of Singhapura" married into Jalandhara. This confirms that a 7th–8th-century kingdom
of Siṃhapura existed in the Panjab, though not where it was. Punjab 1919 calls it "Singhapura (the Salt Range)".

**Assessment.** Stein's work, which Watters had only partly seen, answers Watters' objection and Cunningham's own
doubts. The gazetteers strengthen Mizutani's Ketas identification. Mizutani himself cites neither Stein nor the
Malot detour.

### 03.04.01a 僧訶補羅國大都城 — the plain 1–2 miles N of Ketas (Stein), not the Kotera fort (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Kotera, the ruined fort on the hill above Ketās (uncertain). This is Cunningham's
site (p. 145, tentative), and it depends on emending the text's 40–50 li to 4–5 li. Beal gives no site, noting
only that a capital ringed by crags rules out the plain of Taki. Watters (p. 253) rejects Ketas.

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 prints Stein: "I consider Ketas, or rather the field of ruins lying one or two miles
further north, to be the site of the ancient town, which according to the Chinese pilgrim stood 40–50 li … north-west
of the Jaina temples". The compiler adds that the chained distance Murti–Ketas is 4½ miles, and that since the
city stood "on the plain to the north of it, the discrepancy is not great". The ancient forts on the surrounding
hills are cited as proof of more than a pilgrimage site.

**Assessment.** Stein's placement keeps the text's 40–50 li and fixes the city by measurement from an excavated
site (Murti). Cunningham's Kotera needs an emendation. The evidence assembled here favours Stein's field of ruins N
of Ketas over Kotera. Mizutani does not mention Stein.

### 03.04.02 僧訶補羅城南窣堵波 — the Aśoka stūpa S of the city: the stūpa in front of the Sat-Ghara temples

**Identifications.** Mizutani: a large base, probably of a stūpa, at the foot of the Kotera hill, with a
Kashmiri-style temple in front (uncertain), after Majumdar (p. 683): "Facing them to the east is the basement of a
great structure probably a Buddhist stupa". Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 prints Stein: "In the large stupa, situated before the east front of the Sat-Ghara
temples, I believe I recognise the stupa of Asoka, which, Hiuen Tsiang says, lay to the south of the town". The
gazetteer coding marks this as "new"; see the data remarks.

**Assessment.** Stein and Majumdar appear to describe the same structure, a large stūpa basement facing the
Sat-Ghara temples at Ketas. Stein's placement of the city to the north (03.04.01a) also makes it lie south of the
city, as the text requires. The gazetteer strengthens Mizutani's site while changing the reference point from
Kotera to Stein's city.

### 03.04.03 僧訶補羅城東南石窣堵波 — the stone stūpa and Jain site: Murti in the Gandhala valley (Stein), not the Ketas tanks (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Ketās with its sacred pools, following Cunningham's reading of 4–5 li (uncertain).
Cunningham (p. 143): "the holy tanks of Ketaksh". Beal implies the same. Watters (p. 253) rejects it but finds
Stein's Jain sculptures from Murti "not conclusive" and to "wait for further developments".

**Gazetteers.** Jhelam 1907 prints Stein's argument at length:
- Following the Ketas brook down the Gandhala valley, Stein reached Murti, "a spot which seems to agree most
  remarkably with Hiuen Tsiang's description".
- The brook forms several small pools there, with "dense groups of trees … reflected in the limpid water", which
  "still swarm with fish".
- Excavation of the c. 40 ft mound on the hill exposed mortar-covered sandstone walls, "showing that this is not a
  natural mound but a true stupa".
- Over 30 camel-loads of Jaina-style sculpture went to Lahore.
- Local memory still holds that the temple "belonged to the Jainas".
- The chained distance to Ketas is 4½ miles.
- Conversely, Stein found "at Ketas no group of tanks such as that described", and the Sat-Ghara temples were
  Kashmirian, not Jaina.
- Murti stones had been reused in a Sikh-period temple at Ketas.

**Assessment.** This is the strongest case of new evidence in the range. An excavated stūpa with Jaina sculpture,
pools and trees, at about the right distance without emending the text, outweighs Cunningham's tanks. The evidence
here supports Murti as an alternative to Mizutani's Ketas.

### 03.04.05 摩訶薩埵捨身處（大石門） — the body-offering site: Banj (Stein) stands; the gazetteers repeat Cunningham's Manikyala

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): Banj mountain on the Mahaban spur in Buner, reached by crossing the Indus
from the Taxila side. Majumdar, Petech and Chavannes agree, and Stein found an ancient well there (Vajrastūpa
inscription). Cunningham (p. 140): Manikyala, the great stūpa opened by Court. He reads 信度 as the Suhan river and
counts 200 li SE of Taxila. Beal (p. 145) follows him: Manikyala stūpa No. 5. Watters (p. 255) says Manikyala "seems
to be attended with serious difficulties". Majumdar (p. 682): "Everybody is now agreed that Cunningham was
mistaken", and reports Stein's Banj, though with a correction of the bearing (SE to NE).

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 repeat Cunningham's Manikiala. The scene is "33⅓ miles to the south-east
of Taxila, which is the exact bearing and distance of Manikiala". They add a local legend: Raja Rasalu kills seven
demons, one of whom, Thera, is "still alive in a cavern of Gandgarh". Cunningham took this as the successor of the
tiger-cub story. The Thana gazetteer's Manikyala footnote concerns 02.04.03, not this site.

**Assessment.** The gazetteers only restate Cunningham. Their reckoning ignores the text's return to the *north*
border of Taxila and the crossing of the Indus, which is exactly the point on which Majumdar and Mizutani reject
Manikyala. The Rasalu legend is a general Panjab tradition and does not pin the site. Mizutani's Banj stands, but
the bearing correction Majumdar notes means confidence is only medium.

### 03.04.06 捨身北石窣堵波 — the stone stūpa N of the body-offering: existence unknown

**Identifications.** Mizutani (disputed): existence unknown. He notes that Cunningham's Manikiāla stūpa is a 1st–2nd
century Gandhāran monument. Watters (p. 255) rejects Manikyala. Beal gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** Rawalpindi 1883 and 1895 treat Manikiala as a whole without singling out this stūpa.

**Assessment.** The stūpa stands or falls with 03.04.05. If the site is Banj, nothing in these sources locates a
stone stūpa north of it.

**Gazetteers.** None.

### 03.05 烏剌尸國 (uraśā) — Hazara; the capital Haripur (Watters) vs Mangali (Cunningham)

**Identifications.** Mizutani: Hazara district, with its capital at Haripur (after Watters) (agreed). Beal (p. 147),
Majumdar (p. 680), Dey (after Stein) and Vivien (p. 321) all say broadly Hazara. Watters (p. 257, tentative) has
Hazara with its capital at Haripur, after Swynnerton. Cunningham (p. 119–120, tentative) has Ptolemy's Varsa = the
district of Rash in Dhantawar, W of Muzaffarabad, with the capital "probably … at Mangali, which is said by the
people to have been the ancient capital of the district", between Noshahra and Mansera. Dey also reports Wilson's
valley of Gurez.

**Gazetteers.** Punjab 1919 names "the Urash plain" only as a Kashmir feudatory. No Hazara gazetteer appears among
the hits.

**Assessment.** The country is agreed. The capital is not: Cunningham's Mangali rests on local tradition, and
Haripur is only Swynnerton's suggestion, reported by Watters. Mizutani's Hazara stands, but the capital
should be left open, with Mangali as a candidate.

### 03.06.06 佛牙伽藍 — the Tooth monastery: on the S slope of Takht-i-Sulaimān, or near Pandrethan

**Identifications.** Mizutani: name and site unknown, perhaps near the Shankar Acharya temple on
Takht-i-Sulaimān; a later note puts it on the S slope of the hill, at the SE corner of Srinagar (uncertain).
Cunningham (p. 108): near the old capital (Pandrethan), no site. Beal (p. 158, tentative): 10 li SE of the new city,
N of the old one, S of "Hariparvata, now Takht-i-Suliman". Beal conflates the two hills. Watters gives no site.

**Gazetteers.** None.

**Assessment.** Everyone agrees on the area between Srinagar and Pandrethan. No monument is named, and the
gazetteers add nothing.
