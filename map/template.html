Xuanzang's Places

The study's findings: Locating Xuanzang's Places; the sources: bibliography.

Every country and site of the Da Tang Xiyu ji (大唐西域記) on one map. The points follow the standard identifications, those of 季羨林 et al., Da Tang Xiyu ji jiaozhu (1985), with Mizutani Shinjō's (1971) where Ji is silent, wherever the evidence agrees with them; a dark ring marks the places where the study departs from the standard (a different site, or open between the standard's and another), a light ring where it refines the standard or proposes a site the standard lacks. The evidence weighed: the earlier authorities (Julien, Vivien de Saint-Martin, Cunningham, Majumdar, Beal, Watters, Dey); the Archaeological Survey's reports, Annual Reports and Indian Archaeology – A Review; Epigraphia Indica and the CII; the British Indian gazetteers; the Life; route tests on Xuanzang's distances and bearings; and the scholarship since 1985. Colour shows the study's confidence, taken from the notes' assessments: secure, probable, open, or untested where the study has not assessed the standard view. The "Mizutani's points" layer shows his sites where the map's point differs. The "country areas" layer gives each country an approximate extent: grown over land from its capital until it covers the area its stated circuit (周N里, at the juan's km per li from the route tests) implies, meeting its neighbours half-way; where the text names a river or a range as the country's border (東臨信度河, 北背雪山 etc.), the area stays on its side of it, and where it says mountains surround the country (山周四境), the area stays below the ridges. It shows size and rough reach, not historical borders. The rivers are today's courses of those with at least 20,000 km² upstream (HydroRIVERS, Lehner & Grill 2013); several have moved since the seventh century.

Identifications: Mizutani's notes (平凡社 中国古典文学大系 22, 1971); the authorities' and the gazetteers' words are coded from OCR with verbatim quotes checked by script (repository folder xuanzang-places/). Every work used is listed in the bibliography. Each point marks the study's current site: its own proposed site where it settles on one, the sources' point for places added to Mizutani's list, and otherwise Mizutani's point for the standard identification; where no site is identified, the point is placed approximately from Xuanzang's own bearing and distance, and places that cannot be placed even so are listed but not mapped. All points are approximate. Gazetteer counts are of passages: a passage found in two scans of the same volume counts once.
